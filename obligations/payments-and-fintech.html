Obligations for payments and fintech businesses

The 9 regimes that usually apply to payments and fintech businesses, grouped by theme, with 26 plain-English obligation summaries, control objectives, evidence and notification deadlines.

9 regimes 26 obligation summaries 7 notification deadlines Last reviewed: 8 October 2026

Key takeaways

  • This page lists the regimes that typically apply to payments and fintech businesses. Whether each one applies to a particular organisation depends on its licences, products, size and thresholds.
  • Each summary groups related obligations in plain English. It is a starting point for an obligations register, not a substitute for the law: always read the official source.
  • The IDs match the obligations library and the GRC model builder, so you can download the full set or build a linked model.

Governance and accountability

Whistleblower protections

ASIC Applies to: Public companies, large proprietary companies and super trustee companies must have a whistleblower policy; the protections apply more widely.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-WB-01Have a whistleblower policy (public companies, large proprietary companies, super trustees)
4 individual obligations
  1. Public companies must have a whistleblower policy and make it available to officers and employees Corporations Act s 1317AI(1)
  2. Large proprietary companies must have one from 6 months after the end of their first financial year as a large proprietary company; proprietary companies that are trustees of registrable superannuation entities must have one Corporations Act s 1317AI(2)–(3)
  3. The policy must explain the protections available, to whom and how protected disclosures can be made, how the company will support and protect whistleblowers, how disclosures will be investigated, how people mentioned in disclosures will be treated fairly, and how the policy is made available Corporations Act s 1317AI(5)
  4. Failing to have the policy is a strict liability offence Corporations Act s 1317AI(4)
The policy is current and accessiblePolicy; communications
OB-WB-02Protect whistleblowers: confidentiality of identity, protection from detriment, and anonymous disclosures
7 individual obligations
  1. Disclosures qualify for protection when an eligible whistleblower (including current and former officers, employees, suppliers and their relatives) has reasonable grounds to suspect misconduct or an improper state of affairs, and discloses to ASIC, APRA, an eligible recipient or a lawyer; disclosers need not identify themselves Corporations Act ss 1317AA, 1317AAA
  2. Eligible recipients include officers and senior managers, auditors, actuaries, people the entity authorises to receive disclosures and, for super funds, trustees and trustee directors Corporations Act s 1317AAC
  3. Public interest and emergency disclosures to journalists or parliamentarians are protected only in limited circumstances, such as after a prior disclosure to a regulator and (for public interest disclosures) at least 90 days and written notice Corporations Act s 1317AAD
  4. Disclosures about a personal work-related grievance are generally not protected, unless they concern victimisation or are made to a lawyer Corporations Act s 1317AADA
  5. Do not disclose the whistleblower’s identity, or information likely to identify them, except as the law allows (for example to ASIC, APRA, the AFP, a lawyer, or with consent) Corporations Act s 1317AAE
  6. Do not cause or threaten detriment to anyone because of a belief or suspicion that they made, or could make, a protected disclosure Corporations Act s 1317AC
  7. A protected discloser is not subject to civil, criminal or administrative liability for making the disclosure, and contractual remedies cannot be enforced against them for it Corporations Act s 1317AB
Protections operate in practiceCase records; training

Licensing, conduct and disclosure

AFS licence general obligations (s 912A, RG 104, RG 105)

ASIC Applies to: Australian financial services licensees.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-AFSL-01Provide financial services efficiently, honestly and fairly
6 individual obligations
  1. Do all things necessary to ensure the financial services covered by the licence are provided efficiently, honestly and fairly Corporations Act s 912A(1)(a)
  2. Comply with the conditions on the licence and with the financial services laws Corporations Act s 912A(1)(b)–(c)
  3. Take reasonable steps to ensure representatives comply with the financial services laws Corporations Act s 912A(1)(ca)
  4. Comply with the Reference Checking and Information Sharing Protocol (for representatives who give personal advice to retail clients about relevant financial products) Corporations Act s 912A(1)(cc), (3A)
  5. If providing services to retail clients, have arrangements to compensate them for loss caused by breaches by the licensee or its representatives Corporations Act s 912B
  6. Contravening most of the general obligations is a civil penalty provision Corporations Act s 912A(5A)
Services meet the efficiently, honestly and fairly standardMonitoring results; complaints and breach trends
OB-AFSL-02Maintain adequate risk management systems and compliance arrangements
3 individual obligations
  1. Have adequate risk management systems (not required of APRA-regulated bodies, other than RSE licensees that are also responsible entities of registered schemes) Corporations Act s 912A(1)(h), (5)
  2. Have adequate arrangements for managing conflicts of interest Corporations Act s 912A(1)(aa)
  3. If providing services to retail clients, have a dispute resolution system (internal dispute resolution meeting ASIC’s standards, and AFCA membership) and give ASIC the IDR information it specifies Corporations Act s 912A(1)(g), (2), (2A)
Risk and compliance systems are adequate for the businessFrameworks; monitoring plans
OB-AFSL-03Maintain organisational competence through responsible managers with appropriate knowledge and skills (RG 105)
1 individual obligation
  1. Maintain the competence to provide the financial services covered by the licence Corporations Act s 912A(1)(e)
Responsible managers are nominated, competent and currentResponsible manager records
OB-AFSL-04Have adequate resources and ensure representatives are trained and competent
2 individual obligations
  1. Have adequate financial, technological and human resources to provide the financial services and carry out supervisory arrangements (not required of APRA-regulated bodies, other than RSE licensees that are also responsible entities of registered schemes) Corporations Act s 912A(1)(d), (4)
  2. Ensure representatives are adequately trained (including meeting continuing professional development requirements) and competent to provide the financial services Corporations Act s 912A(1)(f)
Training and competence are trackedTraining records

Internal dispute resolution (RG 271)

ASIC Applies to: AFS and credit licensees and others required to have IDR.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

Notification deadlines

  • 30 calendar days (generally); 45 calendar days for most super complaints: Complaint response (IDR) (Customer, with AFCA rights)
IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-RG271-01Accept complaints in any form, including on social media, without requiring them in writing
6 individual obligations
  1. Deal with every expression of dissatisfaction that meets the complaint definition (adopted from AS/NZS 10002:2014) through the IDR process RG 271.27–271.28
  2. Posts on the firm’s own social media channels that meet the definition are complaints, where the author is identifiable and contactable RG 271.32
  3. The IDR process must, at a minimum, deal with complaints by retail clients (including small businesses with fewer than 100 employees) and, for super funds, by members and beneficiaries eligible to go to AFCA RG 271.36–271.38; RG 271.41–271.42
  4. The process must be free, easy to understand and use (including for people with disability or language difficulties), and supported by a public complaints policy and an internal procedure RG 271.134; RG 271.141; RG 271.172
  5. Record all complaints in an effective system that tracks the progress of each one RG 271.179
  6. Firms that outsource IDR must choose providers with care, monitor them, and deal with their failures RG 271.48
Every complaint is capturedComplaints system
OB-RG271-02Resolve complaints within maximum timeframes: generally 30 calendar days, 45 days for most super complaints
7 individual obligations
  1. Give an IDR response no later than 30 calendar days after receiving a standard complaint RG 271.56; Table 2
  2. Superannuation trustee complaints (other than about death benefit distributions) and traditional trustee complaints: no later than 45 calendar days RG 271.58–271.59; Table 2; RG 271.76–271.78
  3. Death benefit distribution complaints: no later than 90 calendar days after the 28-day objection period ends; credit complaints about default notices or hardship: no later than 21 calendar days Table 2; RG 271.80–271.93
  4. Complaints about insurance in super: trustees, insurers and administrators must meet the timeframe wherever the complaint is first lodged RG 271.79
  5. A firm may exceed the timeframe only if the complaint is particularly complex or delayed by circumstances beyond its control, and it sends an IDR delay notification (reasons, AFCA rights and contact details) before the timeframe ends RG 271.64–271.66
  6. If a customer advocate review is offered, it must not be a mandatory step before AFCA, and the total time must stay within the maximum timeframe RG 271.109–271.110
  7. Resource and staff the IDR function to resolve complaints fairly within the timeframes, including during spikes, and give staff the authority to resolve complaints RG 271.142–271.143; RG 271.146–271.147
Complaints are resolved within timeframesTimeliness reports
OB-RG271-03Give a written IDR response explaining the outcome and the right to go to AFCA
3 individual obligations
  1. The IDR response is in writing and gives the final outcome (with reasons for any rejection), the right to go to AFCA and AFCA’s contact details RG 271.53–271.54
  2. No IDR response is needed if the complaint is resolved, or an explanation or apology given, by the end of the fifth business day, unless the complainant asks for one or it is about hardship, a declined claim, a claim’s value, or a super trustee decision RG 271.71; RG 271.75
  3. Implement complaint outcomes (refunds, fee waivers, corrections, compensation) in a timely manner RG 271.165
IDR responses are completeResponse samples
OB-RG271-04Identify and escalate systemic issues; report complaints data to the board and to ASIC
3 individual obligations
  1. The board sets clear accountabilities for complaints handling, including managing systemic issues identified through complaints RG 271.118
  2. Reports to the board or executive committees include complaints metrics and analysis, including systemic issues RG 271.119; RG 271.183
  3. Enable staff to escalate possible systemic issues, analyse complaints data regularly, escalate promptly for investigation, and report on outcomes RG 271.120
Systemic issues are identified and reportedBoard reports; ASIC data lodgements

Financial crime

AML/CTF

AUSTRAC Applies to: Reporting entities that provide designated services. Major reforms applied from 31 March 2026, with tranche 2 sectors from 1 July 2026.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

Notification deadlines

  • Within 3 business days (24 hours for terrorism financing): Suspicious matter report (AUSTRAC)
  • Within 10 business days: Threshold transaction report ($10,000 or more in physical currency) (AUSTRAC)
  • Within 10 business days: International funds transfer instruction report (AUSTRAC)
IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-AML-01Assess money laundering and terrorism financing riskThe ML/TF risk assessment is currentRisk assessment
OB-AML-02Maintain AML/CTF policies (the program), with governing body oversight and an AML/CTF compliance officer reporting at least every 12 monthsThe program is approved, owned and reported onProgram; compliance officer reports
OB-AML-03Know your customer through customer due diligenceCustomers are identified and risk-ratedDue diligence records
OB-AML-04Report to AUSTRAC: suspicious matters, threshold transactions, international funds transfers and annual compliance reportsReports are complete and on timeLodgement records
OB-AML-05Keep records, train staff and have the program independently evaluated at least every three yearsRecords, training and evaluation are currentTraining records; evaluation report
