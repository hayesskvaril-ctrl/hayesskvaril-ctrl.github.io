Obligations for charities and not-for-profits

The 3 regimes that usually apply to charities and not-for-profits, grouped by theme, with 9 plain-English obligation summaries, control objectives, evidence and notification deadlines.

3 regimes 9 obligation summaries 2 notification deadlines Last reviewed: 8 October 2026

Key takeaways

  • This page lists the regimes that typically apply to charities and not-for-profits. Whether each one applies to a particular organisation depends on its licences, products, size and thresholds.
  • Each summary groups related obligations in plain English. It is a starting point for an obligations register, not a substitute for the law: always read the official source.
  • The IDs match the obligations library and the GRC model builder, so you can download the full set or build a linked model.

Governance and accountability

Whistleblower protections

ASIC Applies to: Public companies, large proprietary companies and super trustee companies must have a whistleblower policy; the protections apply more widely.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-WB-01Have a whistleblower policy (public companies, large proprietary companies, super trustees)The policy is current and accessiblePolicy; communications
OB-WB-02Protect whistleblowers: confidentiality of identity, protection from detriment, and anonymous disclosuresProtections operate in practiceCase records; training

Privacy and data

Privacy Act and notifiable data breaches

OAIC Applies to: Australian Government agencies and most businesses with annual turnover over $3 million, plus some smaller ones such as health service providers.

Notification deadlines

  • Notify the OAIC and affected individuals as soon as practicable: Eligible data breach (OAIC)
