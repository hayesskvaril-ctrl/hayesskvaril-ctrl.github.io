Obligations for charities and not-for-profits

The 3 regimes that usually apply to charities and not-for-profits, grouped by theme, with 9 plain-English obligation summaries, control objectives, evidence and notification deadlines.

3 regimes 9 obligation summaries 2 notification deadlines Last reviewed: 10 October 2026

Key takeaways

  • This page lists the regimes that typically apply to charities and not-for-profits. Whether each one applies to a particular organisation depends on its licences, products, size and thresholds.
  • Each summary groups related obligations in plain English. It is a starting point for an obligations register, not a substitute for the law: always read the official source.
  • The IDs match the obligations library and the GRC model builder, so you can download the full set or build a linked model.

Governance and accountability

Whistleblower protections

ASIC Applies to: Public companies, large proprietary companies and super trustee companies must have a whistleblower policy; the protections apply more widely.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-WB-01Have a whistleblower policy (public companies, large proprietary companies, super trustees)
4 individual obligations
  1. Public companies must have a whistleblower policy and make it available to officers and employees Corporations Act s 1317AI(1)
  2. Large proprietary companies must have one from 6 months after the end of their first financial year as a large proprietary company; proprietary companies that are trustees of registrable superannuation entities must have one Corporations Act s 1317AI(2)–(3)
  3. The policy must explain the protections available, to whom and how protected disclosures can be made, how the company will support and protect whistleblowers, how disclosures will be investigated, how people mentioned in disclosures will be treated fairly, and how the policy is made available Corporations Act s 1317AI(5)
  4. Failing to have the policy is a strict liability offence Corporations Act s 1317AI(4)
The policy is current and accessiblePolicy; communications
OB-WB-02Protect whistleblowers: confidentiality of identity, protection from detriment, and anonymous disclosures
7 individual obligations
  1. Disclosures qualify for protection when an eligible whistleblower (including current and former officers, employees, suppliers and their relatives) has reasonable grounds to suspect misconduct or an improper state of affairs, and discloses to ASIC, APRA, an eligible recipient or a lawyer; disclosers need not identify themselves Corporations Act ss 1317AA, 1317AAA
  2. Eligible recipients include officers and senior managers, auditors, actuaries, people the entity authorises to receive disclosures and, for super funds, trustees and trustee directors Corporations Act s 1317AAC
  3. Public interest and emergency disclosures to journalists or parliamentarians are protected only in limited circumstances, such as after a prior disclosure to a regulator and (for public interest disclosures) at least 90 days and written notice Corporations Act s 1317AAD
  4. Disclosures about a personal work-related grievance are generally not protected, unless they concern victimisation or are made to a lawyer Corporations Act s 1317AADA
  5. Do not disclose the whistleblower’s identity, or information likely to identify them, except as the law allows (for example to ASIC, APRA, the AFP, a lawyer, or with consent) Corporations Act s 1317AAE
  6. Do not cause or threaten detriment to anyone because of a belief or suspicion that they made, or could make, a protected disclosure Corporations Act s 1317AC
  7. A protected discloser is not subject to civil, criminal or administrative liability for making the disclosure, and contractual remedies cannot be enforced against them for it Corporations Act s 1317AB
Protections operate in practiceCase records; training

Privacy and data

Privacy Act and notifiable data breaches

OAIC Applies to: Australian Government agencies and most businesses with annual turnover over $3 million, plus some smaller ones such as health service providers.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

Notification deadlines

  • Assess quickly, generally within 30 days: Suspected eligible data breach (OAIC)
  • Notify the OAIC and affected individuals as soon as practicable: Eligible data breach (OAIC)
IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-PRIV-01Handle personal information in line with the 13 Australian Privacy Principles across its whole life
6 individual obligations
  1. Most businesses with annual turnover of $3 million or less are not covered (small business operators), with exceptions such as health service providers Privacy Act s 6D
  2. APP entities must not breach the Australian Privacy Principles Privacy Act s 15
  3. Take reasonable steps to put in place practices, procedures and systems that ensure compliance with the APPs and allow privacy inquiries and complaints to be handled Privacy Act Sch 1, APP 1.2
  4. Have a clearly expressed, up-to-date privacy policy covering the kinds of information held, how it is collected and held, its purposes, access and correction, complaints, and likely overseas disclosures, and make it available free of charge Privacy Act Sch 1, APP 1.3–1.5
  5. Collect, notify, use, disclose, send overseas, keep accurate, and give access to and correct personal information in line with APPs 3 to 13 Privacy Act Sch 1, APPs 3–13
  6. A serious interference with privacy is a civil penalty provision; for a company the maximum is the greatest of $50 million, three times the benefit obtained, or 30% of adjusted turnover in the breach period Privacy Act s 13G
Personal information is collected, used and disclosed lawfullyData inventory; privacy impact assessments
OB-PRIV-02Protect personal information from misuse, loss and unauthorised access (APP 11)
3 individual obligations
  1. Take reasonable steps to protect personal information from misuse, interference and loss, and from unauthorised access, modification or disclosure Privacy Act Sch 1, APP 11.1
  2. Destroy or de-identify personal information that is no longer needed (unless the law requires it to be kept) Privacy Act Sch 1, APP 11.2
  3. Reasonable steps include technical and organisational measures Privacy Act Sch 1, APP 11.3
Security controls protect personal informationSecurity controls; testing
OB-PRIV-03Assess suspected eligible data breaches quickly (generally within 30 days) and notify the OAIC and affected people as soon as practicable
5 individual obligations
  1. An eligible data breach is unauthorised access to, disclosure or loss of personal information that a reasonable person would conclude is likely to result in serious harm to any of the individuals affected Privacy Act s 26WE(2)
  2. It is not an eligible data breach if remedial action taken before serious harm results means serious harm is no longer likely Privacy Act s 26WF
  3. Where there are reasonable grounds to suspect an eligible data breach, carry out a reasonable and expeditious assessment and take all reasonable steps to complete it within 30 days Privacy Act s 26WH
  4. Where there are reasonable grounds to believe there has been an eligible data breach, prepare a statement (entity, description, kinds of information, recommended steps for individuals) and give it to the Commissioner as soon as practicable Privacy Act s 26WK
  5. Notify the individuals affected or at risk (or, if that is not practicable, publish and publicise the statement) as soon as practicable after preparing it Privacy Act s 26WL
Suspected breaches are assessed and notified on timeBreach assessments; notifications
OB-PRIV-04From 10 December 2026, explain in the privacy policy when personal information is used in substantially automated decisionsAutomated decisions are identified and disclosedDecision inventory; privacy policy

Sustainability, reporting and sector rules

ACNC governance and external conduct standards

ACNC Applies to: Registered charities; the external conduct standards apply to charities operating overseas.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-ACNC-01Meet the six governance standards; responsible people act with care, honestly in the charity’s best interests, and manage conflicts
6 individual obligations
  1. Standard 1: be able to show the charity’s purposes and not-for-profit character, make its purposes public, and comply with them ACNC Regulations s 45.5
  2. Standard 2: if the charity has members, take reasonable steps to be accountable to them and give them an adequate opportunity to raise governance concerns ACNC Regulations s 45.10
  3. Standard 3: do not engage in conduct that could be an indictable offence or attract a civil penalty of 60 penalty units or more ACNC Regulations s 45.15
  4. Standard 4: take reasonable steps to be satisfied that responsible people are not disqualified from managing a corporation or by the ACNC Commissioner, and remove any who are ACNC Regulations s 45.20
  5. Standard 5: take reasonable steps to ensure responsible people act with reasonable care and diligence, in good faith in the charity’s best interests, do not misuse their position or information, disclose material conflicts (including related party transactions), manage finances responsibly and do not allow insolvent trading ACNC Regulations s 45.25
  6. Standard 6: if the charity is, or is likely to be, named in a National Redress Scheme application, take reasonable steps to join the Scheme ACNC Regulations s 45.30
Governance standards are metBoard records; conflicts register
OB-ACNC-02Report to the ACNC according to sizeAnnual reporting is on timeLodgement records
OB-ACNC-03Charities operating overseas meet the external conduct standards
4 individual obligations
  1. External Conduct Standard 1: run overseas activities consistently with the charity’s purpose, keep reasonable internal controls over resources used overseas, check funds given to third parties are properly applied, and comply overseas with Australian laws on money laundering, terrorism financing, child sexual offences, slavery, trafficking, people smuggling, sanctions, tax and bribery, with internal controls to do so ACNC Regulations s 50.20
  2. External Conduct Standard 2: keep records to prepare a country-by-country summary of overseas activities and expenditure each year ACNC Regulations s 50.25
  3. External Conduct Standard 3: take reasonable steps to minimise fraud, corruption and bribery by people and third parties overseas, and document material conflicts of interest ACNC Regulations s 50.30
  4. External Conduct Standard 4: take reasonable steps to ensure the safety of vulnerable people overseas who receive the charity’s services or work on its programs ACNC Regulations s 50.35
Overseas activities are controlledOverseas activity records
