Obligations for private health insurers

The 13 regimes that usually apply to private health insurers, grouped by theme, with 49 plain-English obligation summaries, control objectives, evidence and notification deadlines.

13 regimes 49 obligation summaries 8 notification deadlines Last reviewed: 8 October 2026

Key takeaways

  • This page lists the regimes that typically apply to private health insurers. Whether each one applies to a particular organisation depends on its licences, products, size and thresholds.
  • Each summary groups related obligations in plain English. It is a starting point for an obligations register, not a substitute for the law: always read the official source.
  • The IDs match the obligations library and the GRC model builder, so you can download the full set or build a linked model.

Private health insurance is not a "financial product" under the Corporations Act, so ASIC's licensing, conduct and breach reporting regimes don't apply to health insurance products. Product, premium and community rating rules come from the Private Health Insurance Act 2007 (see "Insurance sector rules" below), complaints go to the Commonwealth Ombudsman (as Private Health Insurance Ombudsman), and consumer law applies. The private health insurance guide explains these rules.

Whistleblower protections

ASIC Applies to: Public companies, large proprietary companies and super trustee companies must have a whistleblower policy; the protections apply more widely.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-WB-01Have a whistleblower policy (public companies, large proprietary companies, super trustees)
4 individual obligations
  1. Public companies must have a whistleblower policy and make it available to officers and employees Corporations Act s 1317AI(1)
  2. Large proprietary companies must have one from 6 months after the end of their first financial year as a large proprietary company; proprietary companies that are trustees of registrable superannuation entities must have one Corporations Act s 1317AI(2)–(3)
  3. The policy must explain the protections available, to whom and how protected disclosures can be made, how the company will support and protect whistleblowers, how disclosures will be investigated, how people mentioned in disclosures will be treated fairly, and how the policy is made available Corporations Act s 1317AI(5)
  4. Failing to have the policy is a strict liability offence Corporations Act s 1317AI(4)
The policy is current and accessiblePolicy; communications
OB-WB-02Protect whistleblowers: confidentiality of identity, protection from detriment, and anonymous disclosures
7 individual obligations
  1. Disclosures qualify for protection when an eligible whistleblower (including current and former officers, employees, suppliers and their relatives) has reasonable grounds to suspect misconduct or an improper state of affairs, and discloses to ASIC, APRA, an eligible recipient or a lawyer; disclosers need not identify themselves Corporations Act ss 1317AA, 1317AAA
  2. Eligible recipients include officers and senior managers, auditors, actuaries, people the entity authorises to receive disclosures and, for super funds, trustees and trustee directors Corporations Act s 1317AAC
  3. Public interest and emergency disclosures to journalists or parliamentarians are protected only in limited circumstances, such as after a prior disclosure to a regulator and (for public interest disclosures) at least 90 days and written notice Corporations Act s 1317AAD
  4. Disclosures about a personal work-related grievance are generally not protected, unless they concern victimisation or are made to a lawyer Corporations Act s 1317AADA
  5. Do not disclose the whistleblower’s identity, or information likely to identify them, except as the law allows (for example to ASIC, APRA, the AFP, a lawyer, or with consent) Corporations Act s 1317AAE
  6. Do not cause or threaten detriment to anyone because of a belief or suspicion that they made, or could make, a protected disclosure Corporations Act s 1317AC
  7. A protected discloser is not subject to civil, criminal or administrative liability for making the disclosure, and contractual remedies cannot be enforced against them for it Corporations Act s 1317AB
Protections operate in practiceCase records; training

Operational risk management (CPS 230)

APRA Applies to: APRA-regulated entities, in proportion to their size and complexity. Smaller entities had until 1 July 2026 for some business continuity and scenario analysis requirements; the full standard now applies to all.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

Notification deadlines

  • Within 72 hours: Material operational risk incident (APRA)
  • Within 24 hours: Disruption to a critical operation outside tolerance (APRA)
  • Within 20 business days: Entering into or materially changing an agreement for a service relied on to undertake a critical operation (APRA)
IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-CPS230-01Board is ultimately accountable for operational risk, business continuity and service providers; senior management implements
7 individual obligations
  1. The Board is ultimately accountable for oversight of operational risk management, including business continuity and service provider arrangements CPS 230 para 19
  2. The Board makes sure clear roles and responsibilities are set for senior managers for operational risk, business continuity and service providers CPS 230 para 20
  3. The Board oversees operational risk management and key internal controls, gets regular updates on the operational risk profile, and makes sure senior management acts on concerns CPS 230 para 21(a)
  4. The Board approves the business continuity plan (BCP) and tolerance levels, reviews testing results and oversees the follow-up CPS 230 para 21(b)
  5. The Board approves the service provider management policy and reviews risk and performance reporting on material service providers CPS 230 para 21(c)
  6. Senior management gives the Board clear and comprehensive information on the expected impact on critical operations when the Board makes decisions that could affect their resilience CPS 230 para 22
  7. Senior management is responsible for operational risk management across end-to-end processes for all business operations CPS 230 para 23
Board oversight of operational resilience is evidencedBoard papers; accountability statements
OB-CPS230-02Identify, assess and manage operational risks; design, implement and test internal controls; keep weaknesses in the risk profile until fixed
13 individual obligations
  1. Identify, assess and manage operational risks from inadequate or failed processes or systems, people, and external drivers and events CPS 230 para 12
  2. As part of the risk management framework, maintain governance arrangements, an operational risk profile with a defined appetite (indicators, limits and tolerance levels), effective internal controls, monitoring and escalation, BCPs and service provider processes CPS 230 para 15
  3. Review operational risk management as part of the regular reviews of the risk management framework CPS 230 para 16
  4. Integrate operational risk management into the overall risk management framework, and keep business continuity planning consistent with recovery and exit planning CPS 230 para 17
  5. Manage the full range of operational risks, including legal, regulatory, compliance, conduct, technology, data and change management risk CPS 230 para 23
  6. Maintain sound IT capability for current and projected needs, monitor the age and health of information assets, and meet CPS 234 CPS 230 para 24
  7. Assess the impact of business and strategic decisions, including new products, services, geographies and technologies, on the operational risk profile and resilience CPS 230 para 25
  8. Maintain a comprehensive assessment of the operational risk profile: operational risk information systems, documented processes and resources for critical operations (with interdependencies, risks, obligations, key data and controls) and scenario analysis of severe events CPS 230 para 26
  9. Carry out a comprehensive risk assessment before providing a material service to another party CPS 230 para 27
  10. Design, implement and embed internal controls in line with risk appetite and compliance obligations CPS 230 para 28
  11. Regularly monitor, review and test controls for design and operating effectiveness, as often as the risk warrants; report results to senior management and fix gaps in a timely manner CPS 230 para 29
  12. Remediate material weaknesses with clear accountability and assurance, address root causes, and keep open gaps in the risk profile until fixed CPS 230 para 30
  13. Identify, escalate, record and address operational risk incidents and near misses in a timely manner, and reflect them in the risk profile and control assessments CPS 230 para 31
Operational risks and controls are current and testedRisk registers; control testing results; issues register
OB-CPS230-03Identify critical operations and set board-approved tolerance levels
4 individual obligations
  1. Define, identify and keep a register of critical operations CPS 230 para 33(a)
  2. At a minimum, treat the operations listed for the industry as critical unless it can justify otherwise (for example claims processing for insurers, investment management and fund administration for super trustees, and customer enquiries and supporting systems for all) CPS 230 para 35
  3. For each critical operation, set tolerance levels for the maximum period of disruption, the maximum data loss and the minimum service levels under alternative arrangements CPS 230 para 37
  4. Monitor compliance with tolerance levels and report any failure to meet them, with a remediation plan, to the Board CPS 230 para 40
Every critical operation has approved tolerancesCritical operations register; tolerance statements
OB-CPS230-04Maintain a credible business continuity plan and test it, including an annual exercise with severe but plausible scenarios
8 individual obligations
  1. Take reasonable steps to minimise the likelihood and impact of disruptions to critical operations CPS 230 para 33(b)
  2. Maintain a credible BCP for keeping critical operations within tolerance through disruptions (including disaster recovery for critical information assets), activate it when needed and return to normal operations promptly CPS 230 para 33(c)–(e)
  3. The BCP includes the register of critical operations and tolerances, activation triggers, the actions to stay within tolerance, an assessment of execution risks, resources and dependencies, and a communications strategy CPS 230 para 39
  4. Maintain the people, resources and technology needed to execute the BCP CPS 230 para 40
  5. Run a systematic BCP testing program covering all critical operations, including an annual business continuity exercise CPS 230 para 42
  6. Tailor testing to material risks, with severe but plausible scenarios, including disruption to material service providers and scenarios that need contingency arrangements CPS 230 para 43
  7. Update the BCP as needed each year for changes in structure, business mix, strategy or risk profile, and for shortcomings found in testing CPS 230 para 44
  8. Internal audit periodically reviews the BCP and gives the Board assurance that it is credible and that testing is adequate CPS 230 para 45
The organisation can stay within tolerance through severe disruptionBCP; test reports and lessons
OB-CPS230-05Identify material service providers; due diligence, formal agreements and ongoing monitoring
11 individual obligations
  1. Do not rely on a service provider unless prudential obligations can still be met in full and the risks managed CPS 230 para 14
  2. Maintain a service provider management policy covering how material service providers are identified, entered into, monitored, substituted and exited, and how their risks and fourth-party risks are managed CPS 230 paras 46–47
  3. Identify and keep a register of material service providers and manage the material risks of using them CPS 230 para 48
  4. At a minimum, treat providers of the listed services as material unless it can justify otherwise (including risk management, core technology services and internal audit for all entities) CPS 230 para 49
  5. Before entering into or materially changing a material arrangement, carry out due diligence and assess financial and non-financial risks, including location and concentration CPS 230 para 52
  6. Keep a formal legally binding agreement for every material arrangement, covering services and service levels, rights and responsibilities (including data, audit access and liability), compliance, sub-contracting, force majeure and termination CPS 230 para 53
  7. The agreement gives APRA access to documentation and information and a right to visit the provider, and the provider agrees not to impede APRA CPS 230 para 54
  8. For each material arrangement, manage risks to the provider’s ability to deliver and risks to the entity (such as step-in or contagion risk), and make sure the BCP can be executed and an orderly exit is possible CPS 230 para 55
  9. Limited exemption: some contract, exit and monitoring requirements need not be met for listed categories of provider (such as government agencies, regulators, central banks, exchanges, clearing and settlement, payment systems and financial messaging) where the arrangement uses standardised terms or has no formal agreement CPS 230 paras 57–58
  10. Monitor material arrangements, with reporting to senior management on performance against service levels, control effectiveness and both parties’ compliance with the agreement CPS 230 para 59
  11. Internal audit reviews any proposed material arrangement to outsource a critical operation, and reports regularly to the Board or audit committee on compliance with the policy CPS 230 para 61
Material service providers are assessed, contracted and monitoredDue diligence reports; agreements; monitoring records
OB-CPS230-06Submit the material service provider register to APRA each year
1 individual obligation
  1. Submit the register of material service providers to APRA every year CPS 230 para 50
The register is complete and submitted on timeRegister; submission record
OB-CPS230-07Notify APRA of material operational risk incidents and of disruptions to critical operations outside tolerance
4 individual obligations
  1. Notify APRA as soon as possible, and no later than 72 hours after becoming aware, of an operational risk incident likely to have a material financial impact or a material impact on the ability to maintain critical operations (an incident already notified under CPS 234 need not be notified again) CPS 230 para 32
  2. Notify APRA as soon as possible, and no later than 24 hours, after a disruption to a critical operation outside tolerance, covering the disruption, the action taken, the likely impact and the time to return to normal CPS 230 para 41
  3. Notify APRA as soon as possible, and no more than 20 business days after entering into or materially changing an agreement for a service relied on to undertake a critical operation CPS 230 para 60(a)
  4. Notify APRA before entering into a material offshoring arrangement, or when a significant change to one is proposed, including where data or personnel will be offshore CPS 230 para 60(b)
Notifiable events are identified and notified within the deadlinesIncident register; notification records

Information security (CPS 234)

APRA Applies to: APRA-regulated entities.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

Notification deadlines

  • Within 72 hours: Material information security incident (APRA)
  • Within 10 business days: Material information security control weakness that can’t be remediated in a timely way (APRA)
IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-CPS234-01Board is ultimately responsible for information security
3 individual obligations
  1. The Board is ultimately responsible for information security and must make sure it is maintained in proportion to the threats, so the entity can keep operating soundly CPS 234 para 13
  2. Clearly define the information security roles and responsibilities of the Board, senior management, governing bodies and individuals CPS 234 para 14
  3. Escalate and report to the Board or senior management any testing results showing control deficiencies that can’t be fixed in a timely manner CPS 234 para 29
Board oversight of information security is evidencedBoard papers; security reporting
OB-CPS234-02Know and classify information assets by criticality and sensitivity, including those managed by third parties
4 individual obligations
  1. Classify information assets, including those managed by related parties and third parties, by criticality and sensitivity, reflecting the potential impact of an incident on the entity and its customers CPS 234 para 20
  2. Maintain an information security capability in proportion to the threats, and keep it current as vulnerabilities, threats, assets and the business environment change CPS 234 paras 15 and 17
  3. Assess the information security capability of related parties and third parties that manage its information assets CPS 234 para 16
  4. Maintain an information security policy framework in proportion to its exposures, giving direction to everyone with information security responsibilities CPS 234 paras 18–19
Every information asset is classified and ownedInformation asset register
OB-CPS234-03Protect information assets with controls proportionate to the threats
5 individual obligations
  1. Implement information security controls in a timely manner, in proportion to vulnerabilities and threats, the criticality and sensitivity of the assets, their life-cycle stage and the potential consequences of an incident CPS 234 para 21
  2. Evaluate the design of the controls that related parties and third parties use to protect its information assets CPS 234 para 22
  3. Have robust mechanisms to detect and respond to information security incidents in a timely manner CPS 234 para 23
  4. Maintain response plans for incidents that could plausibly occur, covering every stage from detection to post-incident review, and escalation to the Board CPS 234 paras 24–25
  5. Review and test the response plans every year CPS 234 para 26
Controls match the criticality and sensitivity of assetsControl library; maturity assessments
OB-CPS234-04Test controls through a systematic testing program, with internal audit review
6 individual obligations
  1. Test control effectiveness through a systematic testing program, with nature and frequency matched to changing threats, criticality and sensitivity, consequences, untrusted environments and change CPS 234 para 27
  2. Where it relies on a related or third party’s control testing, assess whether that testing is adequate CPS 234 para 28
  3. Testing is done by appropriately skilled and functionally independent specialists CPS 234 para 30
  4. Review the sufficiency of the testing program at least annually, or when assets or the business environment change materially CPS 234 para 31
  5. Internal audit reviews the design and operating effectiveness of information security controls, including those of related and third parties, using appropriately skilled people CPS 234 paras 32–33
  6. Internal audit assesses a related or third party’s control assurance where an incident could materially affect the entity and internal audit intends to rely on that assurance CPS 234 para 34
Security controls are tested and gaps fixedTesting program; audit reports
OB-CPS234-05Notify APRA of material information security incidents, and of material control weaknesses that cannot be fixed in a timely way
2 individual obligations
  1. Notify APRA as soon as possible, and no later than 72 hours after becoming aware, of an information security incident that materially affected (or could have) the entity or its customers, or that has been notified to other regulators CPS 234 para 35
  2. Notify APRA as soon as possible, and no later than 10 business days after becoming aware, of a material information security control weakness the entity expects it can’t remediate in a timely manner CPS 234 para 36
Notifiable security events are notified within the deadlinesIncident log; notification records

Capital, liquidity and investment

Capital management and ICAAP

APRA Applies to: ADIs (APS 110) and insurers (their own capital standards).

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-CAP-01Hold capital above the minimum requirements, with buffers (for ADIs, Common Equity Tier 1 of at least 4.5% of risk-weighted assets before buffers)
6 individual obligations
  1. ADIs: the Board ensures capital is commensurate with the type, amount and concentration of risks, having regard to prospective changes in the risk profile APS 110 para 12
  2. ADIs: maintain capital ratios above the prudential capital requirements at all times; the minimums are 4.5% Common Equity Tier 1, 6.0% Tier 1 and 8.0% Total Capital, and APRA may set higher requirements (which must not be publicly disclosed) APS 110 paras 24–25
  3. ADIs: hold a capital conservation buffer of Common Equity Tier 1 (2.5% for standardised ADIs, 3.75% for IRB ADIs, plus 1.0% for domestic systemically important banks), or face limits on distributions APS 110 paras 27–28, 30
  4. ADIs: hold a countercyclical capital buffer of Common Equity Tier 1, calculated by jurisdiction APS 110 para 32
  5. IRB ADIs: maintain a leverage ratio of at least 3.5% APS 110 para 37
  6. ADIs: get APRA’s approval before any planned reduction in capital, and notify APRA of any breach or likely breach of the capital requirements and the remedial action APS 110 paras 40, 44
Capital stays above targets and triggersCapital reporting
OB-CAP-02Run an ICAAP to assess capital needs, including under stress
4 individual obligations
  1. ADIs: have an internal capital adequacy assessment process (ICAAP), documented, approved by the Board and appropriate to size, business mix and complexity APS 110 paras 14–15
  2. ADIs: have the ICAAP reviewed by qualified, operationally independent people at least every three years APS 110 para 19
  3. ADIs: prepare an annual ICAAP report, including current and three-year projected capital against requirements and targets, and give it to APRA within three months of the period end APS 110 paras 20–21
  4. ADIs: the ICAAP report comes with a declaration approved by the Board and signed by the CEO on whether capital was managed in line with the ICAAP APS 110 para 22

Privacy and data

Privacy Act and notifiable data breaches

OAIC Applies to: Australian Government agencies and most businesses with annual turnover over $3 million, plus some smaller ones such as health service providers.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

Notification deadlines

  • Assess quickly, generally within 30 days: Suspected eligible data breach (OAIC)
  • Notify the OAIC and affected individuals as soon as practicable: Eligible data breach (OAIC)
IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-PRIV-01Handle personal information in line with the 13 Australian Privacy Principles across its whole life
6 individual obligations
  1. Most businesses with annual turnover of $3 million or less are not covered (small business operators), with exceptions such as health service providers Privacy Act s 6D
  2. APP entities must not breach the Australian Privacy Principles Privacy Act s 15
  3. Take reasonable steps to put in place practices, procedures and systems that ensure compliance with the APPs and allow privacy inquiries and complaints to be handled Privacy Act Sch 1, APP 1.2
  4. Have a clearly expressed, up-to-date privacy policy covering the kinds of information held, how it is collected and held, its purposes, access and correction, complaints, and likely overseas disclosures, and make it available free of charge Privacy Act Sch 1, APP 1.3–1.5
  5. Collect, notify, use, disclose, send overseas, keep accurate, and give access to and correct personal information in line with APPs 3 to 13 Privacy Act Sch 1, APPs 3–13
  6. A serious interference with privacy is a civil penalty provision; for a company the maximum is the greatest of $50 million, three times the benefit obtained, or 30% of adjusted turnover in the breach period Privacy Act s 13G
Personal information is collected, used and disclosed lawfullyData inventory; privacy impact assessments
OB-PRIV-02Protect personal information from misuse, loss and unauthorised access (APP 11)
3 individual obligations
  1. Take reasonable steps to protect personal information from misuse, interference and loss, and from unauthorised access, modification or disclosure Privacy Act Sch 1, APP 11.1
  2. Destroy or de-identify personal information that is no longer needed (unless the law requires it to be kept) Privacy Act Sch 1, APP 11.2
  3. Reasonable steps include technical and organisational measures Privacy Act Sch 1, APP 11.3
Security controls protect personal informationSecurity controls; testing
OB-PRIV-03Assess suspected eligible data breaches quickly (generally within 30 days) and notify the OAIC and affected people as soon as practicable
5 individual obligations
  1. An eligible data breach is unauthorised access to, disclosure or loss of personal information that a reasonable person would conclude is likely to result in serious harm to any of the individuals affected Privacy Act s 26WE(2)
  2. It is not an eligible data breach if remedial action taken before serious harm results means serious harm is no longer likely Privacy Act s 26WF
  3. Where there are reasonable grounds to suspect an eligible data breach, carry out a reasonable and expeditious assessment and take all reasonable steps to complete it within 30 days Privacy Act s 26WH
  4. Where there are reasonable grounds to believe there has been an eligible data breach, prepare a statement (entity, description, kinds of information, recommended steps for individuals) and give it to the Commissioner as soon as practicable Privacy Act s 26WK
  5. Notify the individuals affected or at risk (or, if that is not practicable, publish and publicise the statement) as soon as practicable after preparing it Privacy Act s 26WL
Suspected breaches are assessed and notified on timeBreach assessments; notifications

Modern slavery reporting

Attorney-General’s Department Applies to: Entities with annual consolidated revenue of at least $100 million. A proposed offence was announced in July 2026; check the current position.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-MSA-01Publish an annual modern slavery statement addressing the seven mandatory criteria, approved by the board
6 individual obligations
  1. Reporting entities include entities based or operating in Australia with consolidated revenue of at least $100 million for the reporting period (others may volunteer) Modern Slavery Act ss 5–6
  2. Give the Minister a modern slavery statement for each reporting period (alone or in a joint statement), in the approved form, within 6 months after the period ends Modern Slavery Act ss 13(1), 13(2)(b) and (e), 14
  3. The statement is approved by the principal governing body and signed by a responsible member Modern Slavery Act s 13(2)(c)–(d)
  4. Address the mandatory criteria: identify the entity; describe its structure, operations and supply chains, the modern slavery risks (including in entities it owns or controls), the actions taken to assess and address them (including due diligence and remediation), how effectiveness is assessed, and the consultation process; and include any other relevant information Modern Slavery Act s 16(1)
  5. Include details of the governing body’s approval Modern Slavery Act s 16(2)
  6. If an entity fails to comply, the Minister may ask for an explanation or remedial action within 28 days or more, and may publish details if the request is not met Modern Slavery Act s 16A
The statement is complete and approvedStatement; supplier assessments
