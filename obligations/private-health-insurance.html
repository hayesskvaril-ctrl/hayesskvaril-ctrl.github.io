Obligations for private health insurers

The 13 regimes that usually apply to private health insurers, grouped by theme, with 49 plain-English obligation summaries, control objectives, evidence and notification deadlines.

13 regimes 49 obligation summaries 8 notification deadlines Last reviewed: 9 October 2026

Key takeaways

  • This page lists the regimes that typically apply to private health insurers. Whether each one applies to a particular organisation depends on its licences, products, size and thresholds.
  • Each summary groups related obligations in plain English. It is a starting point for an obligations register, not a substitute for the law: always read the official source.
  • The IDs match the obligations library and the GRC model builder, so you can download the full set or build a linked model.

Private health insurance is not a "financial product" under the Corporations Act, so ASIC's licensing, conduct and breach reporting regimes don't apply to health insurance products. Product, premium and community rating rules come from the Private Health Insurance Act 2007 (see "Insurance sector rules" below), complaints go to the Commonwealth Ombudsman (as Private Health Insurance Ombudsman), and consumer law applies. The private health insurance guide explains these rules.

Governance and accountability

Governance and fit and proper (CPS 510, CPS 520, SPS 510, SPS 520)

APRA Applies to: APRA-regulated entities. APRA has proposed one consolidated governance standard; check the current position.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-CPS510-01Meet minimum requirements for board size, independence, committees, renewal and performance review
15 individual obligations
  1. The Board is ultimately responsible for oversight of the sound and prudent management of the institution, and has a formal charter CPS 510 paras 16–17
  2. Have at least five directors and a majority of independent directors, with an independent chair, and a majority of non-executive directors at every meeting (some exceptions for subsidiaries) CPS 510 paras 26–29
  3. The chair cannot have been CEO in the previous three years (an interim CEO role over 90 days needs APRA approval) CPS 510 para 30
  4. Assess the Board’s performance, and each director’s, at least annually CPS 510 para 44
  5. Have a formal Board renewal policy that considers whether long tenure could interfere with acting in the institution’s best interests, and covers appointing, re-appointing and removing directors CPS 510 para 45
  6. Have a Board Audit Committee of at least three non-executive directors, a majority independent, with an independent chair who is not the Board chair CPS 510 paras 51–55
  7. Have a Board Risk Committee with an independent chair (not the Board chair) to oversee the risk management framework CPS 510 paras 79–83
  8. Have an independent, adequately resourced internal audit function (or an APRA-approved alternative) CPS 510 para 68
  9. Super (SPS 510): the Board is ultimately responsible for sound and prudent management, documents and monitors delegations, and makes sure directors and senior management collectively have the skills needed SPS 510 paras 8–10
  10. Super (SPS 510): keep a governance framework including a Board charter, voting procedures, and policies on Board size and composition, renewal, and nominating, appointing and removing directors with defined terms and maximum tenure SPS 510 paras 16–18, 22–23
  11. Super (SPS 510): the Board chair is a director, a majority of directors and all senior management are ordinarily resident in Australia, and only a director chairs a committee that could materially affect members SPS 510 paras 11–12, 19–20
  12. Super (SPS 510): assess the Board’s and each director’s performance at least annually SPS 510 para 21
  13. Super (SPS 510): have a Board Audit Committee of at least three non-executive directors (the Board chair may chair it only if they are the only independent director), which reviews the RSE auditor’s independence each year and audit plans and findings, and runs a confidential channel for staff concerns SPS 510 paras 24–36
  14. Super (SPS 510): have an independent, adequately resourced internal audit function (or seek an APRA exemption), obtain an independence declaration from the RSE auditor, and observe the cooling-off and rotation rules for auditors SPS 510 paras 37–47
  15. Super (SPS 510): do not stop anyone, by confidentiality clauses or otherwise, from giving information to APRA SPS 510 paras 48–49
Board structure meets the standard and is reviewedBoard charter; skills matrix; review reports
OB-CPS510-02Assess responsible persons as fit and proper before appointment and at least annually
10 individual obligations
  1. Fit and proper means it would be prudent to conclude the person has the competence, character, diligence, honesty, integrity and judgement for the role, is not disqualified, and has no material conflict of interest CPS 520 para 30
  2. Complete a fit and proper assessment before a person takes a responsible person position (within 28 days where they are appointed by members’ resolution or APRA determination) CPS 520 para 41
  3. Interim appointments without a full assessment are allowed for up to 90 days, after reasonable checks CPS 520 para 42
  4. Assess every responsible person at least annually (or as close as practicable), making all reasonable enquiries CPS 520 paras 43–44
  5. If someone is not fit and proper, take all reasonable steps to make sure they are not appointed, or do not continue CPS 520 para 54
  6. Super (SPS 520): responsible persons include directors, the secretary, senior managers, the RSE auditor and actuary, and people in connected entities who could materially affect the business; define the competencies for each position SPS 520 paras 12–18
  7. Super (SPS 520): a person is fit and proper if it is prudent to conclude they have the competence, character, diligence, experience, honesty, integrity, judgement and qualifications for the role, are not disqualified under the SIS Act, and have no conflict that creates a material risk SPS 520 para 19
  8. Super (SPS 520): RSE auditors and actuaries meet extra criteria, including at least five years’ relevant experience and living in Australia SPS 520 paras 20–25
  9. Super (SPS 520): assess before appointment (interim appointments up to 90 days after reasonable checks), at least annually, and again if new information raises a concern SPS 520 paras 29–33
  10. Super (SPS 520): take all reasonable steps to stop someone assessed as not fit and proper from being appointed or continuing, and check no responsible person is a disqualified person SPS 520 paras 42, 48
Every responsible person has a current assessmentAssessment records
OB-CPS510-03Keep a fit and proper policy and records, and notify APRA if someone is assessed as not fit and proper
7 individual obligations
  1. Maintain a Board-approved Fit and Proper Policy, as part of the risk management framework, and make sure responsible persons understand it CPS 520 paras 12–15
  2. Tell APRA each responsible person’s title, name, date of birth, responsibilities and assessment status, and update it within 28 days of any change or appointment CPS 520 paras 55–56
  3. Notify APRA within 10 business days of assessing that a responsible person is not fit and proper, with reasons if they stay in the role CPS 520 para 57
  4. Super (SPS 520): keep a Board-approved Fit and Proper Policy, part of the risk management framework, covering who assesses, what information is gathered, how decisions are made, and what happens if someone fails SPS 520 paras 7–10, 26–28
  5. Super (SPS 520): the policy allows whistleblowing to the assessor or APRA about a responsible person, protects people who disclose in good faith, and the trustee must not restrict such disclosures SPS 520 paras 36–41
  6. Super (SPS 520): give APRA each responsible person’s details and keep them correct, and promptly tell APRA in writing if someone stays in a role despite being assessed as not fit and proper SPS 520 paras 43–45
  7. Super (SPS 520): keep enough records of each assessment to show the fitness and propriety of current and recent responsible persons SPS 520 para 35
Policy and records are complete; notifications are madePolicy; register; notifications

Financial Accountability Regime (FAR)

APRA and ASIC Applies to: Banks (from 15 March 2024) and insurers and super trustees (from 15 March 2025).

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-FAR-01Register accountable persons and keep an accountability statement for each and an accountability map for the entity
4 individual obligations
  1. Make sure accountable persons’ responsibilities together cover all parts of the group’s operations and each prescribed responsibility FAR Act s 23(1)(a)
  2. Make sure no one acts as an accountable person unless registered with the regulator (with grace periods, for example 90 days for a temporary or unforeseen vacancy) and not disqualified FAR Act ss 23(1)(b), 24
  3. Notify the regulator within 30 days (or as the Regulator rules provide) when an accountable person leaves, is dismissed or suspended, or has variable pay reduced for failing their obligations, or when there are reasonable grounds to believe the entity or a person has failed to comply, and of material changes to registered information FAR Act ss 31(1), (6), 32
  4. Entities above the enhanced notification threshold give the regulator an accountability statement for each accountable person (with the person’s declaration that it is accurate) and an accountability map of names, reporting lines and responsibilities, and notify material changes FAR Act ss 31(2), 33–34
Every key responsibility is allocated to a registered accountable personStatements; map; registrations
OB-FAR-02Accountable persons act with honesty, integrity, due skill, care and diligence, deal openly with APRA and ASIC, and take reasonable steps
5 individual obligations
  1. The entity takes reasonable steps to conduct its business with honesty, integrity, due skill, care and diligence, to deal with the regulators openly and cooperatively, and to prevent matters that would harm its prudential standing or reputation FAR Act s 20(a)–(c)
  2. The entity takes reasonable steps to make sure each accountable person meets their obligations, and that significant related entities do the same FAR Act s 20(d)–(e)
  3. Accountable persons act with honesty, integrity, due skill, care and diligence, deal with the regulators openly and cooperatively, and take reasonable steps to prevent harm to prudential standing and material breaches of the financial sector laws FAR Act s 21(1)
  4. Where two or more accountable persons share a responsibility, each is fully accountable for it FAR Act s 21(2)
  5. Reasonable steps include appropriate governance, control and risk management, safeguards against inappropriate delegation, procedures to find and fix problems, and action on non-compliance FAR Act s 22
Reasonable steps are evidenced for each accountable personReasonable steps records; information received by accountable persons
OB-FAR-03Defer at least 40% of accountable persons’ variable remuneration for at least four years, and reduce it for failures to meet obligations
3 individual obligations
  1. Defer at least 40% of each accountable person’s variable remuneration for at least four years FAR Act ss 25(1)(a), 27(1), 28
  2. Have a remuneration policy that reduces an accountable person’s variable remuneration in proportion to any failure to meet their obligations (possibly to zero), and do not pay the reduced amount FAR Act s 25(1)(b)–(c), (2)
  3. Deferral is not required where the amount to be deferred for the year is less than $50,000 (or any amount set by the Minister rules) FAR Act s 29
Deferral and adjustment rules are appliedRemuneration records

Remuneration (CPS 511)

APRA Applies to: APRA-regulated entities; significant financial institutions face extra requirements.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-CPS511-01Board-approved remuneration framework that supports sound risk management and long-term outcomes
8 individual obligations
  1. Keep a remuneration framework that aligns with the business plan, strategy and risk management framework, promotes management of financial and non-financial risks and long-term soundness (for super trustees, members’ best financial interests), and helps prevent conduct risk CPS 511 paras 21, 74
  2. Document a remuneration policy covering how the framework meets those aims, the structure of remuneration arrangements (including staff of related service companies), conflicts from third-party service provider pay, and the supporting systems CPS 511 paras 22, 75
  3. The Board is ultimately responsible for the framework and its effective application, and approves the remuneration policy CPS 511 paras 23–24, 76–77
  4. Design variable pay to reflect financial and non-financial risks, with payout and vesting schedules matched to the time horizon of risk CPS 511 paras 33(a)–(b), 78(a)–(b)
  5. The Board approves variable pay outcomes individually for senior managers and executive directors, and by cohort for material risk-takers and risk and financial control staff CPS 511 paras 52, 87
  6. Do not pay remuneration through vehicles that undermine the standard: no indemnity or insurance against its consequences, and a documented ban on hedging unvested equity-linked pay CPS 511 paras 61, 89
  7. Publish remuneration disclosures on the website each financial year, no more than six months after the year ends CPS 511 paras 63–66, 91–94
  8. Assess and mitigate conflicts of interest in the design of remuneration arrangements, including from service contracts CPS 511 paras 40, 83
Remuneration design reflects risk and conduct outcomesFramework; remuneration committee papers
OB-CPS511-02Variable pay can be reduced for poor risk or conduct outcomes (in-period adjustment, malus and clawback)
7 individual obligations
  1. Build in adjustment tools (Board discretion, in-period adjustment, malus and, where appropriate, clawback) with a downward-adjustment process that has clear triggers and can reduce pay to nil CPS 511 paras 33(c), 78(c)
  2. All variable remuneration is subject to malus CPS 511 paras 35, 79
  3. Set criteria for using the tools, at least: misconduct with significant adverse outcomes, significant risk management failures, significant breaches of accountability, fit and proper or compliance obligations, significant errors or misstatements, and significant adverse outcomes for customers, members or counterparties CPS 511 paras 37, 80
  4. Take reasonable steps to reduce variable pay when a criterion is met, in proportion to the severity of the outcome CPS 511 paras 38, 81
  5. Variable pay of someone under investigation for one of those criteria must not vest until the investigation closes CPS 511 paras 39, 82
  6. Variable pay outcomes align with performance and risk outcomes and reflect the use of the adjustment tools CPS 511 paras 44, 85
  7. Do not accelerate vesting for people in specified roles who leave, except for death or serious incapacity, disability or illness CPS 511 paras 47, 86
Adjustments are considered and applied consistentlyAdjustment decisions and records
OB-CPS511-03Significant financial institutions: board remuneration committee, material weight on non-financial measures, minimum deferral
10 individual obligations
  1. Establish a Board Remuneration Committee of at least three non-executive directors with a written charter. Outside super it needs a majority of independent members and an independent chair; a super trustee’s Board chair may be a member but may chair it only if they are the Board’s only independent director CPS 511 paras 25–29
  2. The committee consults the Board Risk Committee and the Chief Risk Officer under a documented process, gets comprehensive reporting, and has free access to other committees and to risk and financial control staff CPS 511 paras 30–32
  3. Give material weight to non-financial measures in performance-related variable pay; no component may depend entirely on share price or profit CPS 511 para 34 and footnote 12
  4. Clawback applies to senior managers, executive directors and highly paid material risk-takers for at least two years after payment or vesting, even after they leave CPS 511 para 36
  5. Defer at least 60% of the CEO’s variable pay over at least six years, and at least 40% for other senior managers and executive directors over five years (vesting pro rata, only after four years), and for highly paid material risk-takers over four years (only after two) CPS 511 para 41
  6. Deferral does not apply to anyone with deferred variable pay of less than $50,000 in a financial year CPS 511 para 43
  7. Pay or vest variable remuneration only if justified by the effectiveness of risk management and by individual, business unit and entity performance CPS 511 para 45
  8. The committee recommends specified roles’ arrangements and outcomes to the Board each year, and pay for risk and financial control staff is not unduly influenced by the businesses they control CPS 511 paras 49–53
  9. Review the framework’s compliance at least annually and its effectiveness, independently, at least every three years, reporting results to the committee CPS 511 paras 54–57
  10. Publish the detailed disclosure tables, with Tables 2 to 4 in a machine-readable format such as CSV CPS 511 paras 64, 67–73
SFI requirements are metCommittee charter; scorecards; deferral records

Whistleblower protections

ASIC Applies to: Public companies, large proprietary companies and super trustee companies must have a whistleblower policy; the protections apply more widely.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-WB-01Have a whistleblower policy (public companies, large proprietary companies, super trustees)
4 individual obligations
  1. Public companies must have a whistleblower policy and make it available to officers and employees Corporations Act s 1317AI(1)
  2. Large proprietary companies must have one from 6 months after the end of their first financial year as a large proprietary company; proprietary companies that are trustees of registrable superannuation entities must have one Corporations Act s 1317AI(2)–(3)
  3. The policy must explain the protections available, to whom and how protected disclosures can be made, how the company will support and protect whistleblowers, how disclosures will be investigated, how people mentioned in disclosures will be treated fairly, and how the policy is made available Corporations Act s 1317AI(5)
  4. Failing to have the policy is a strict liability offence Corporations Act s 1317AI(4)
The policy is current and accessiblePolicy; communications
OB-WB-02Protect whistleblowers: confidentiality of identity, protection from detriment, and anonymous disclosures
7 individual obligations
  1. Disclosures qualify for protection when an eligible whistleblower (including current and former officers, employees, suppliers and their relatives) has reasonable grounds to suspect misconduct or an improper state of affairs, and discloses to ASIC, APRA, an eligible recipient or a lawyer; disclosers need not identify themselves Corporations Act ss 1317AA, 1317AAA
  2. Eligible recipients include officers and senior managers, auditors, actuaries, people the entity authorises to receive disclosures and, for super funds, trustees and trustee directors Corporations Act s 1317AAC
  3. Public interest and emergency disclosures to journalists or parliamentarians are protected only in limited circumstances, such as after a prior disclosure to a regulator and (for public interest disclosures) at least 90 days and written notice Corporations Act s 1317AAD
  4. Disclosures about a personal work-related grievance are generally not protected, unless they concern victimisation or are made to a lawyer Corporations Act s 1317AADA
  5. Do not disclose the whistleblower’s identity, or information likely to identify them, except as the law allows (for example to ASIC, APRA, the AFP, a lawyer, or with consent) Corporations Act s 1317AAE
  6. Do not cause or threaten detriment to anyone because of a belief or suspicion that they made, or could make, a protected disclosure Corporations Act s 1317AC
  7. A protected discloser is not subject to civil, criminal or administrative liability for making the disclosure, and contractual remedies cannot be enforced against them for it Corporations Act s 1317AB
Protections operate in practiceCase records; training

Risk, resilience and cyber

Risk management (CPS 220, or SPS 220 for super)

APRA Applies to: APRA-regulated entities (SPS 220 for super trustees). Check which version applies to your entity.

Read our guide · See it in the obligations library

IDObligation (summary)Control objectiveEvidence
OB-CPS220-01Maintain a risk management framework covering all material risks, including credit, market and investment, liquidity, insurance, operational and strategic risks
11 individual obligations
  1. Maintain a risk management framework that gives the Board an institution-wide view of material risks and allows strategies, policies, procedures and controls to manage them CPS 220 paras 19–20
  2. The framework is consistent with the business plan and provides a structure for identifying and managing each material risk, suited to size, business mix and complexity CPS 220 paras 21–22
  3. At a minimum, the framework includes a risk appetite statement, a risk management strategy and a business plan, among other elements CPS 220 para 23
  4. At a minimum, it addresses credit, market and investment, liquidity, insurance and operational risk, risks from strategic objectives and business plans, and any other material risks CPS 220 para 26
  5. Notify APRA within 10 business days of becoming aware of a significant breach of, or material deviation from, the framework, or that it did not adequately address a material risk CPS 220 para 53
  6. Notify APRA within 10 business days of material or prospective material changes to size, business mix and complexity CPS 220 para 54
  7. Super (SPS 220): keep a risk management framework at all times covering all material financial and non-financial risks, assessed against the business as a whole, each fund and the obligations to members SPS 220 paras 5–6, 10–11
  8. Super (SPS 220): cover at least governance, investment governance, liquidity (including investment options), operational, insurance and strategic risks, and contagion risk from any non-super business SPS 220 paras 12–13
  9. Super (SPS 220): the framework includes the risk appetite statement, risk management strategy, risk management function, policies and controls for each material risk, defined roles, an adequate management information system and a review process SPS 220 para 16
  10. Super (SPS 220): the Board is ultimately responsible for the framework, for solvency and for adequate resources SPS 220 paras 7–8
  11. Super (SPS 220): notify APRA within 10 business days of a significant breach of, or material deviation from, the framework, or finding it did not address a material risk; and as soon as practicable of material changes to size, business mix and complexity SPS 220 paras 35–36
The framework is complete, approved and reflects every material riskBoard-approved framework; material risk assessment
OB-CPS220-02Board approves a risk appetite statement and a risk management strategy
6 individual obligations
  1. The Board is ultimately responsible for the risk management framework and its oversight, sets the risk appetite and approves the risk appetite statement and risk management strategy CPS 220 paras 9(a), 27, 29
  2. The risk appetite statement sets out the risk appetite, a risk tolerance (limit) for each material risk, how tolerances are set and monitored, what happens if one is breached, and when they are reviewed CPS 220 para 28
  3. The Board makes sure senior management manage material risks within appetite, the structure supports risk management, risk-taking policies match the strategy, and enough resources go to risk management CPS 220 para 9(c)–(f)
  4. Submit the risk appetite statement, business plan and risk management strategy to APRA on adoption and after material revisions, within 10 business days of Board approval CPS 220 para 52
  5. Super (SPS 220): keep an up-to-date, Board-approved risk appetite statement with a risk tolerance for each material risk, how tolerances are set and monitored, what happens on a breach, and when they are reviewed SPS 220 paras 19–20
  6. Super (SPS 220): keep an up-to-date, Board-approved risk management strategy describing each material risk, the key risk policies and their review dates, roles, and how risk culture is instilled SPS 220 paras 21–22
Appetite and strategy are current, approved and used in decisionsBoard papers and minutes; appetite metrics reporting
OB-CPS220-03Board forms a view of the entity’s risk culture
1 individual obligation
  1. The Board forms a view of the risk culture, how far it supports operating within appetite, identifies desirable changes and makes sure they are addressed CPS 220 para 9(b)
Risk culture is assessed and reported to the boardRisk culture assessment; board minutes
OB-CPS220-04Operationally independent risk management function headed by a Chief Risk Officer, and a designated compliance function
5 individual obligations
  1. Have a designated risk management function that is operationally independent, appropriately resourced, has access to all sources of material risk and tells the Board of significant breaches of or deviations from the framework CPS 220 para 37
  2. Designate a Chief Risk Officer who can effectively challenge decisions affecting the risk profile, is independent of business lines and finance, and is not the CEO, CFO, Appointed Actuary or Head of Internal Audit CPS 220 paras 38–39
  3. The CRO reports directly to the CEO and has regular and unfettered access to the Board and Board Risk Committee CPS 220 para 40
  4. Have a designated compliance function, adequately staffed, with enough authority and a reporting line independent of business lines CPS 220 para 43
  5. Super (SPS 220): have a designated risk management function that is operationally independent, properly resourced, has access to all sources of material risk and tells the Board of material deviations or breaches (it may sit in the group or with an external provider if it meets these tests) SPS 220 paras 24–26
Second-line functions are independent and resourcedOrganisation chart; CRO reporting lines; function charters
OB-CPS220-05Comprehensive, operationally independent review of the framework at least every three years
4 individual obligations
  1. Internal or external audit reviews compliance with, and the effectiveness of, the framework at least annually, reporting to the Board Audit Committee CPS 220 para 44
  2. Operationally independent, competent people carry out a comprehensive review of the framework at least every three years, reporting to the Board Risk Committee CPS 220 paras 45–47
  3. Super (SPS 220): an operationally independent comprehensive review at least every three years, and a review of the framework in each other year SPS 220 paras 27–29
  4. Super (SPS 220): have internal audit procedures and external audit arrangements that check compliance with the framework SPS 220 para 30
The framework is independently reviewed on time and findings are fixedReview report; action tracker
OB-CPS220-06Annual risk management declaration to APRA (CPS 220 for banks and insurers; SPS 220 for super trustees)
4 individual obligations
  1. The Board makes an annual risk management declaration to APRA, signed by the Board chair and the Board Risk Committee chair CPS 220 para 49; Attachment A
  2. Qualify the declaration if there has been a significant breach of, or material deviation from, the framework, explaining the cause and the remedy CPS 220 para 50
  3. Submit it within three months of the annual balance date (four months for ADIs that are not disclosing entities, and for Level 3 heads), unless APRA approves otherwise CPS 220 para 51
  4. Super (SPS 220): the Board gives APRA an annual risk management declaration signed by two directors, on or before the day annual information is due under APRA’s reporting standards, and explains any qualification SPS 220 paras 32–34; Attachment A
The declaration is supported by evidence and approved by the boardDeclaration; supporting attestations

Operational risk management (CPS 230)

OB-CPS230-02Identify, assess and manage operational risks; design, implement and test internal controls; keep weaknesses in the risk profile until fixed
13 individual obligations

  5. Manage the full range of operational risks, including legal, regulatory, compliance, conduct, technology, data and change management risk CPS 230 para 23

  9. Carry out a comprehensive risk assessment before providing a material service to another party CPS 230 para 27
  10. Design, implement and embed internal controls in line with risk appetite and compliance obligations CPS 230 para 28
  11. Regularly monitor, review and test controls for design and operating effectiveness, as often as the risk warrants; report results to senior management and fix gaps in a timely manner CPS 230 para 29
  12. Remediate material weaknesses with clear accountability and assurance, address root causes, and keep open gaps in the risk profile until fixed CPS 230 para 30

  2. Maintain a credible BCP for keeping critical operations within tolerance through disruptions (including disaster recovery for critical information assets), activate it when needed and return to normal operations promptly CPS 230 para 33(c)–(e)
  3. The BCP includes the register of critical operations and tolerances, activation triggers, the actions to stay within tolerance, an assessment of execution risks, resources and dependencies, and a communications strategy CPS 230 para 39
  4. Maintain the people, resources and technology needed to execute the BCP CPS 230 para 40
  5. Run a systematic BCP testing program covering all critical operations, including an annual business continuity exercise CPS 230 para 42
  6. Tailor testing to material risks, with severe but plausible scenarios, including disruption to material service providers and scenarios that need contingency arrangements CPS 230 para 43
  7. Update the BCP as needed each year for changes in structure, business mix, strategy or risk profile, and for shortcomings found in testing CPS 230 para 44

  2. Maintain a service provider management policy covering how material service providers are identified, entered into, monitored, substituted and exited, and how their risks and fourth-party risks are managed CPS 230 paras 46–47
