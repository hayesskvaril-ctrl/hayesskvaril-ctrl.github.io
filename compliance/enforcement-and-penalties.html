Enforcement and penalties: how regulators respond

The tools regulators use, how maximum civil penalties are worked out, how courts set the actual penalty, and what that means for how organisations respond to problems.

Advanced About 15 minutes Last reviewed: 28 September 2026

Key takeaways

  • Regulators have a ladder of responses, from supervisory engagement to civil penalties and criminal referral. Most matters are resolved lower down.
  • For companies, the maximum Corporations Act and ASIC Act civil penalty is the greatest of 50,000 penalty units, three times the benefit, or 10% of turnover (capped at 2.5 million penalty units). A penalty unit is $364 from 1 July 2026.
  • The High Court has confirmed deterrence is the main purpose of civil penalties.
  • Cooperation, self-reporting and remediation are the most controllable mitigating factors. A paper-only compliance program can aggravate.
  • Because each affected customer can be a separate contravention, systemic failures create very large exposure.

The idea in plain English

When a regulator finds a problem, it has a menu of responses, from a warning letter to a criminal referral. Which one it chooses depends on how serious the conduct is, how the organisation responded, and what outcome the regulator wants: stopping harm, compensating people, deterring others, or removing people from the industry.

For a risk or compliance professional, understanding enforcement helps with three things: judging how serious an issue really is, deciding how to engage with a regulator, and explaining consequences to executives and boards in concrete terms.

Some well-known outcomes show the range: APRA required CBA to hold an extra $1 billion of operational risk capital after its 2018 prudential inquiry; AUSTRAC's cases against CBA and Westpac ended in penalties of $700 million and $1.3 billion. How maximum penalties are set

Since reforms in 2019, the maximum civil penalty under the Corporations Act and ASIC Act is linked to the size of the wrongdoing and of the company, not just a fixed dollar figure. For a company it is the greatest of 50,000 penalty units, three times the benefit obtained or detriment avoided, or 10% of annual turnover (capped at 2.5 million penalty units). For an individual it is the greater of 5,000 penalty units or three times the benefit. A Commonwealth penalty unit is $364 for contraventions from 1 July 2026.

Try it: maximum civil penalty per contravention

Illustrative only. Applies to civil penalty provisions of the Corporations Act and ASIC Act with the post-2019 maximums; some provisions and other laws have different maximums. Turnover is the 12 months ending at the end of the month the contravention happened, and has a specific legal definition.

The maximum matters less than it looks. Courts rarely approach it for a single contravention, but many contraventions can be alleged (for example one per affected customer), and courts then consider the overall penalty.

How courts decide the actual penalty

The High Court has confirmed that the main purpose of a civil penalty is deterrence: specific (this wrongdoer) and general (everyone else), not punishment or retribution (Australian Building and Construction Commissioner v Pattinson, 2022). Courts commonly consider factors such as:

FactorPushes the penalty upPushes the penalty down
Nature and extent of the conductDeliberate, prolonged, widespreadIsolated, short, inadvertent
Loss or harm causedLarge or affecting vulnerable peopleSmall, and fully remediated
Size and resources of the contravenorLarge, profitable company (a small penalty won't deter it)Small entity or individual of limited means
Involvement of senior managementSenior people knew or were involvedConduct by junior staff, contrary to policy
Culture of complianceWeak systems, ignored warnings, prior contraventionsGenuine compliance program that the conduct got around
Cooperation and contritionObstruction, denial, late admissionsSelf-reporting, early admissions, cooperation, remediation

Many cases end with an agreed penalty put to the court by both sides. The High Court has held that courts may receive and consider agreed penalty submissions, though the court decides the penalty (Commonwealth v Director, Fair Work Building Industry Inspectorate, 2015). Courts also apply the course of conduct and totality principles so that the overall penalty is proportionate to the overall wrongdoing.

What this means inside an organisation

  • Self-reporting and cooperation count. How an organisation responds after finding a problem is itself relevant to penalties. Timely, complete breach reports, cooperation and genuine remediation are the most controllable mitigating factors.
  • Evidence of a real compliance culture matters. A program that exists on paper but was ignored can aggravate, not mitigate.
  • Counting matters. When a failure repeats across thousands of transactions, the number of alleged contraventions drives the exposure. Fix systemic issues early.
  • People face consequences too. Individuals can be penalised, disqualified or banned. See directors' and officers' duties: key cases.
  • Enforceable undertakings are not a soft option. They usually require independent expert reviews, remediation plans and public reporting, sometimes for years.

Uncertainty and change

Penalty frameworks differ between laws, and regulators' enforcement approaches change over time. For example, after the Financial Services Royal Commission ASIC adopted a "why not litigate?" starting question, which it later moved away from as its approach evolved. Check each regulator's current published approach and priorities.

Check your understanding

1. What is the main purpose of a civil penalty, according to the High Court in Pattinson (2022)?

Deterrence, both specific and general, is the primary purpose.

2. For a company, the maximum Corporations Act civil penalty is the greatest of 50,000 penalty units, three times the benefit, or:

The turnover-based limit is 10%, capped at 2.5 million penalty units.

3. Which of these most reliably reduces a penalty?

Cooperation and genuine remediation are recognised mitigating factors. An ignored program can aggravate.
