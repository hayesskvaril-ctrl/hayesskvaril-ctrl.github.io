Climate-related financial disclosures and sustainability reporting

Large Australian companies, super funds and managed funds must now report on climate-related risks, opportunities and emissions in a "sustainability report" in their annual report.

Intermediate About 13 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026

Key takeaways

  • Since 2025, the Corporations Act requires entities above set size thresholds to prepare an annual sustainability report, containing climate statements prepared under the Australian standard AASB S2 Climate-related Disclosures.
  • Reporting is phased in by size: Group 1 from financial years starting on or after 1 January 2025, Group 2 from 1 July 2026 and Group 3 from 1 July 2027. Super funds and registered schemes with $5 billion or more in assets are in Group 2.
  • Disclosures follow four themes: governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics and targets, including greenhouse gas emissions and climate scenario analysis.
  • Assurance is phased in, from limited assurance over a few disclosures in the first year to reasonable assurance over the whole report for years starting on or after 1 July 2030.
  • A temporary "modified liability" period limits who can sue over some forward-looking statements, but misleading climate claims elsewhere remain fully exposed. Greenwashing enforcement is active.

Why climate reporting became mandatory

Investors, lenders and regulators want consistent, comparable information about how climate change could affect organisations' finances, and how organisations contribute to it. Until 2025, Australian climate reporting was voluntary and patchy, often loosely based on the international Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) framework. The Treasury Laws Amendment (Financial Market Infrastructure and Other Measures) Act 2024 made it mandatory, using an Australian version of the International Sustainability Standards Board's climate standard.

Who must report, and from when

The requirements apply to entities that must prepare annual financial reports under Chapter 2M of the Corporations Act and meet the thresholds below. An entity meets a size test if it meets at least two of the three criteria (at the consolidated level).

Group 11 January 2025Revenue $500m+; gross assets $1b+; 500+ employeesNGER reporters meeting the publication threshold

Group 31 July 2027Revenue $50m+; gross assets $25m+; 100+ employeesGroup 3 entities with no material climate risks or opportunities can say so instead of full disclosure

NGER reporters are entities required to report emissions and energy under the National Greenhouse and Energy Reporting Act 2007. Check the legislation and ASIC's RG 280 for the precise tests.

What must be disclosed: AASB S2

AASB S2, issued by the Australian Accounting Standards Board, is based closely on the ISSB's IFRS S2, with some Australian changes. It organises disclosures into four themes that will be familiar from TCFD:

  • Scenario analysis: the Corporations Act requires an assessment of the entity's climate resilience using at least two scenarios: one in which global warming well exceeds 2°C, and one in which it is limited to 1.5°C.
  • Greenhouse gas emissions: scope 1 (direct) and scope 2 (from purchased energy) from the first year; scope 3 (the rest of the value chain, including financed emissions for financial institutions) from the second reporting year.
  • AASB S1: a broader sustainability standard exists, but it is voluntary. The mandatory regime covers climate.
  • Directors' declaration: directors must make a declaration about the sustainability report. For financial years starting between 1 January 2025 and 31 December 2027, they need only declare that, in their opinion, the entity has taken reasonable steps to ensure the report complies with the Act. A full compliance declaration applies after that.

Assurance and liability

TopicPosition
Assurance standardsThe Auditing and Assurance Standards Board's ASSA 5000 (general requirements) and ASSA 5010 (the phasing timeline) apply.
Year 1Limited assurance only over governance, parts of strategy (risks and opportunities), and scope 1 and 2 emissions.
Years 2 and 3Assurance extends to more disclosures, still mostly at the limited level.
Full reasonable assuranceOver the whole sustainability report for financial years starting on or after 1 July 2030.
Modified liabilityFor financial years starting between 1 January 2025 and 31 December 2027, statements about scope 3 emissions, scenario analysis and transition plans in the sustainability report can only be the subject of criminal proceedings or action by ASIC. Private claims over those statements are not available during this period.

The modified liability period is narrow. It doesn't cover climate claims in marketing, PDSs, websites or investor presentations, which remain subject to the ordinary prohibitions on misleading conduct.

Greenwashing risk

ASIC has made greenwashing an enforcement priority. It has won civil penalty cases against superannuation and funds management businesses over misleading sustainability claims, including penalties against Mercer Superannuation ($11.3 million), Vanguard Investments Australia ($12.9 million) and Active Super ($10.5 million). The lessons for any climate or sustainability claim:

  • Make sure claims match what the product or organisation actually does, including screens and exclusions.
  • Be specific; avoid vague terms like "sustainable" or "green" without explanation.
  • Have a reasonable basis for forward-looking claims such as net zero targets, and explain the plan and assumptions.
  • Keep the sustainability report, PDSs, websites and marketing consistent.

See Disclosure obligations for the wider rules on misleading conduct.

Practical implications for risk and compliance teams

  • Governance: board and committee charters, skills and reporting lines for climate oversight.
  • Data and controls: emissions data needs the same control environment as financial data, especially as assurance ramps up. Scope 3 and financed emissions depend heavily on third-party data and estimates.
  • Risk integration: climate risk should sit within the enterprise risk framework, not as a standalone project. See Climate risk management.
  • Disclosure review: apply the organisation's disclosure review process to the sustainability report and to every other climate-related statement.
