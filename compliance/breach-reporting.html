Breach and incident reporting obligations

A regime-by-regime guide to who must be told what, and how fast, when something goes wrong in an Australian financial services business.

Intermediate About 24 minutes Last reviewed: 2 October 2026 Checked against sources

Key takeaways

  • There is no single reporting regime. One event can trigger reports to ASIC, APRA, the OAIC, AUSTRAC and others, each with its own test and deadline.
  • ASIC's reportable situations regime is the broadest for AFS and credit licensees: significant breaches of core obligations must be reported within 30 calendar days.
  • Licensees giving personal advice to retail clients must also notify, investigate and remediate affected clients.
  • APRA's clocks are shorter: 24 or 72 hours for serious operational and information security incidents.
  • Most clocks start from awareness (or recklessness), not from the end of an investigation. Good processes capture the date of awareness from day one.

1. ASIC reportable situations

Who: AFS licensees and Australian credit licensees, including super trustees (who hold an AFS licence).
Law: Corporations Act ss 912D, 912DAA and 912DAB; National Consumer Credit Protection Act equivalents. In force since 1 October 2021.
Guidance: ASIC Regulatory Guide 78.

What is reportable?

CategoryIn brief
Significant breaches of core obligationsActual or likely breaches that are significant, either because they are "deemed significant" or because they are significant having regard to the relevant factors.
InvestigationsAn investigation into whether there is a reportable significant breach that continues beyond the set period. ASIC relief extended this from 30 to 60 days, from 27 June 2025.
Additional reportable situationsIncluding conduct constituting gross negligence or serious fraud by the licensee or its representatives.
About other licenseesLicensees must report reportable situations about certain other licensees' individual advisers or mortgage brokers in specified circumstances.

"Deemed significant" breaches

A breach of a core obligation is automatically significant if:

  • it is an offence punishable by imprisonment of 12 months or more (3 months or more if it involves dishonesty);
  • it is a contravention of a civil penalty provision (unless excluded by regulation);
  • it contravenes the prohibition on misleading or deceptive conduct in relation to financial products or services (Corporations Act s 1041H(1) or ASIC Act s 12DA(1)); or
  • it results, or is likely to result, in material loss or damage to clients or members.

Otherwise, significance is assessed against factors such as the number or frequency of similar breaches, the impact on the licensee's ability to provide its services, and the extent to which the breach indicates that compliance arrangements are inadequate.

Timing and lodgement

Report within 30 calendar days after the licensee first knows, or is reckless about whether, there are reasonable grounds to believe a reportable situation has arisen, using ASIC's prescribed form through the ASIC Regulatory Portal. ASIC publishes aggregated reportable situations data, which lets licensees compare themselves with the industry.

ASIC's 2025 relief

ASIC's breach reporting instrument (ASIC Instrument 2024/620, amended by Instrument 2025/289 from 27 June 2025) gives targeted relief. In summary:

  • Investigations are reportable only if they continue for more than 60 days, not 30.
  • Some minor breaches of the misleading or deceptive conduct provisions and certain civil penalty provisions need not be reported where the breach was rectified within 30 days of first occurring (including any remediation), no more than five consumers were affected, and their total loss was no more than $500. The conditions are detailed; check the instrument.
  • One report for APRA and ASIC: a breach report given to APRA that contains all the information APRA asks for is taken to be lodged with ASIC.

Getting it wrong

ASIC's review of 14 licensees (February 2025) found that 31% of reported breaches took more than a year to identify, mainly because incidents weren't identified, escalated and recorded well. In 2026 the Federal Court ordered Mercer Super to pay $10.3 million in penalties for not reporting seven investigations into significant member services issues, reporting another late with understated numbers, and having inadequate reporting systems. See Incident and breach management.

2. Notify, investigate and remediate affected clients

Who: AFS licensees that provide personal advice to retail clients about relevant financial products (and credit licensees providing credit assistance), where there are reasonable grounds to suspect a reportable situation involving a representative has caused loss or damage to clients.
Law: Corporations Act ss 912EA and 912EB (and credit equivalents).

  • Take reasonable steps to notify affected clients within 30 days after first knowing (or being reckless about) the circumstances.
  • Investigate the full extent of the issue and tell affected clients the outcome.
  • Take reasonable steps to pay affected clients an amount equal to their loss or damage within 30 days after the investigation is complete.

More broadly, ASIC's Regulatory Guide 277 sets its expectations for all consumer remediation by AFS and credit licensees.

3. APRA

SourceWhatTimeframe
CPS 230Disruption to a critical operation outside toleranceAs soon as possible, no later than 24 hours
CPS 230Operational risk incident likely to have a material financial impact or a material impact on the ability to maintain critical operationsAs soon as possible, no later than 72 hours after becoming aware
CPS 230Entering into or materially changing a material service provider arrangementNo later than 20 business days (and before material offshoring)
CPS 234Material information security incidentAs soon as possible, no later than 72 hours after becoming aware
CPS 234Material information security control weakness not able to be remediated in a timely wayNo later than 10 business days after becoming aware
SIS Act s 29JASignificant breaches by RSE licensees and certain other mattersIn writing as soon as practicable, and no later than 30 days after becoming aware

Banks and insurers have further breach notification requirements in their governing Acts and prudential standards. APRA's "Notify a breach or incident" page explains how to lodge, and its breach form allows a single notification to be shared with ASIC where both need to know.

4. OAIC: Notifiable Data Breaches scheme

Who: organisations and agencies covered by the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth).
What: an eligible data breach: unauthorised access to, disclosure or loss of personal information that is likely to result in serious harm to any of the individuals, where remedial action hasn't prevented that likely harm.

  • If you suspect an eligible data breach, carry out a reasonable and expeditious assessment, generally within 30 days.
  • If it is eligible, notify the OAIC and affected individuals as soon as practicable.
  • If the data was held by a service provider, both may have obligations; usually one entity notifies on behalf of all.

More: Privacy law.

5. Design and distribution: significant dealings

Under the design and distribution obligations, a product issuer must notify ASIC within 10 business days after becoming aware of a significant dealing in a product that is not consistent with its target market determination (Corporations Act s 994G). Distributors must report complaints and other information to issuers so they can monitor this. More: Consumer protection.

6. AUSTRAC

AML/CTF reports aren't "breach" reports, but they are time-critical obligations that often arise from the same events (for example a fraud or account takeover).

ReportTriggerTimeframe
Suspicious matter report (SMR)A suspicion formed on specified grounds, e.g. possible money laundering, fraud or identity issuesWithin 3 business days; within 24 hours if related to terrorism financing
Threshold transaction report (TTR)Transfer of physical currency of $10,000 or moreWithin 10 business days
International funds transfer instruction (IFTI)Sending or receiving an international funds transfer instructionWithin 10 business days. The 2024 AML/CTF reforms replace IFTI reports with international value transfer service (IVTS) reports, but under AUSTRAC's transitional rules IFTI reporting continues until each entity's transition date, on or after 31 March 2029.

Don't tip off

It is an offence to disclose to a customer (or anyone else, other than as permitted) that an SMR has been made or is being considered. This affects how incident communications are handled. More: AML/CTF fundamentals.

7. Cyber-specific regimes

  • Ransomware payments: since 30 May 2025, businesses with annual turnover of $3 million or more (and responsible entities for critical infrastructure assets) that make a ransomware or cyber extortion payment must report it to the Australian Signals Directorate within 72 hours, under the Cyber Security Act 2024 (Cth).
  • Critical infrastructure: responsible entities for critical infrastructure assets under the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act 2018 (Cth), which include some financial services assets, must report critical cyber security incidents within 12 hours and other relevant incidents within 72 hours.

What a good breach assessment records

Whatever the outcome, the assessment should let someone else follow the reasoning later. Typical contents:

ElementWhat to record
FactsWhat happened, when, who is affected and how many, and the source of each fact.
Awareness dateWhen the licensee first had reasonable grounds to believe a reportable situation had arisen, and who knew.
ObligationsEach law, standard, licence condition or rule that may be breached, and whether each is a core obligation.
Breach?Whether each obligation was breached, or is likely to be, with reasons.
SignificanceWhether any deemed significance test applies; if not, the assessment against the significance factors.
Other regimesAPRA, OAIC, AUSTRAC, DDO and cyber obligations considered, and the outcome for each.
DecisionReport or not, who decided, when, and the lodgement date and reference.
Follow-upRemediation, root cause, control fixes and any update reports needed.

Common mistakes

  • Starting the clock at the end of the investigation rather than at awareness.
  • Treating "not significant" as the default without checking the deemed significance tests first.
  • Forgetting investigations, which are reportable in their own right after 60 days.
  • Assessing each regime separately and missing one, or giving regulators inconsistent facts.
  • No record of decisions not to report, so the reasoning can't be shown later.
  • Not updating a report when the number of people affected or the cause turns out to be different.

Frequently asked questions

If we report to APRA, do we also have to report to ASIC?

Often both regimes apply. Under ASIC's 2025 relief, a breach report to APRA that contains all the information APRA requests is taken to be lodged with ASIC, which avoids duplicate forms. The two regimes' tests and deadlines still need to be assessed.

Do we report if we fixed the breach quickly?

Usually fixing a breach doesn't remove the obligation to report a significant breach. ASIC's relief covers only certain minor breaches that meet all its conditions.

What if we aren't sure yet whether it is a breach?

An investigation into whether there is a significant breach becomes reportable if it runs for more than 60 days. And a likely breach can be reportable even before it is confirmed.

Putting it together: one event, many reports

Worked example

A super trustee's administrator is hit by a cyber attack. Member data is stolen and payments stop for five days. The trustee may need to consider:

  • APRA: CPS 230 (critical operation outside tolerance: 24 hours; material operational risk incident: 72 hours), CPS 234 (material information security incident: 72 hours), and SIS Act breach reporting if obligations to members were breached.
  • OAIC: assess and, if eligible, notify the data breach as soon as practicable, and tell affected members.
  • ASIC: a reportable situation if a core obligation was significantly breached (e.g. failure to act efficiently, honestly and fairly, or material loss to members): 30 days.
  • ASD: if a ransom payment is made: 72 hours.
  • AUSTRAC: if stolen identities are later used for fraudulent withdrawals, suspicious matter reports.

A single coordinated incident team and a consistent set of facts help make sure each regulator gets an accurate, timely and consistent picture.
