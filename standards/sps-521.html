SPS 521 Conflicts of Interest

APRA's standard on how super trustees find, avoid and manage conflicts between what they owe members and their other duties and interests.

Intermediate About 12 minutes Last reviewed: 12 October 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA)
Status
Binding prudential standard (F2012L02230), in force since 1 July 2013. APRA's draft single governance standard, released on 16 June 2026, would replace it (see what's proposed).
Applies to
All RSE licensees (trustees of APRA-regulated super funds), their responsible persons and their employees.
Main related law
Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993 (SIS Act): the covenants to give priority to members' duties and interests when there is a conflict, and to comply with the prudential standards on conflicts (s 52(2)(d) for trustees and s 52A(2)(d) for directors of corporate trustees).

Key takeaways

  • SPS 521 covers four kinds of conflict: between what the trustee owes members and its duties to others, or the interests of the trustee, its associates, its responsible persons and its staff.
  • The trustee must have a Board-approved conflicts management framework that identifies all potential and actual conflicts and makes sure they are avoided or prudently managed. The Board is ultimately responsible for it.
  • At a minimum the framework includes a Board-approved policy, clear roles and resources, and up-to-date registers of relevant duties and relevant interests, with a documented test for what counts as relevant.
  • The policy must require each conflict, and the action taken, to be recorded in Board and committee minutes, and regular enquiry into conflicts involving service providers and advisers.
  • The framework is reviewed every year, with results reported to the Board, and comprehensively reviewed by operationally independent people at least every three years.

SPS 521 in plain English

A super trustee manages other people's retirement savings. The people who run it often have other roles: directors may be nominated by employer or member organisations, sit on other boards or hold investments, and the trustee may belong to a corporate group that also sells services to the fund. None of that is wrong in itself, but each connection can pull a decision away from what is best for members.

The SIS Act deals with this directly. Where there is a conflict, the trustee and its directors must give priority to members' duties and interests, make sure duties to members are met and members' interests are not harmed despite the conflict, and comply with APRA's prudential standards on conflicts (ss 52(2)(d) and 52A(2)(d)). SPS 521 is that standard: it sets out the framework, policy, registers and reviews a trustee needs so it can show that conflicts are being found and handled.

Avoid or manage?

SPS 521 asks trustees to avoid conflicts or manage them prudently, but it doesn't let a trustee manage a conflict that the general law requires it to avoid (footnote 6). In practice, the response depends on how serious the conflict is:

  • Record and monitor where a relevant interest exists but has no bearing on the decision at hand.
  • Control the conflict where it does bear on a decision: the conflicted person doesn't receive the papers, leaves the meeting and doesn't vote, and the minutes record the conflict and the action taken.
  • Avoid the conflict where it can't be controlled well enough for members' interests to come first: the person steps away from the role or the relationship, or the trustee doesn't enter into the arrangement.

Disclosure alone is rarely enough: it tells members or the Board about the conflict but doesn't stop it affecting the decision. The conflicts of interest guide sets out a general framework for choosing a response.

Worked example: a director with a link to a tender bidder

A trustee is tendering its member administration. One director's spouse is a senior executive at one of the bidders.

  1. The director disclosed the spouse's role when appointed (para 12), and it is in the register of relevant interests. The trustee's relevance test says it is relevant to any decision involving that company (paras 15, 16).
  2. When the tender starts, the company secretary flags the conflict. The director doesn't receive tender papers, leaves the room for tender discussions and doesn't vote. The minutes record the conflict and these steps (para 18(e)).
  3. The trustee asks every bidder about conflicts of their own, such as links to the trustee's staff or its other providers (para 19).
  4. If that bidder wins, the conflict continues for the life of the contract: the director stays out of decisions on the administrator's performance, fees and renewal, and the arrangement is re-checked at the annual review (para 23).
  5. If the director's role made the conflict impossible to control, for example if they would need to oversee the contract, the trustee would consider whether the conflict must be avoided altogether.

An illustrative application for learning, not advice about any trustee's obligations.

Common mistakes

  • Registers that list every interest but never apply the relevance test, or that are only updated once a year.
  • Minutes that say "conflicts were noted" without recording what each conflict was and what was done about it (para 18(e)).
  • Relying on disclosure when the conflict needed to be controlled or avoided.
  • Using group policies without Board approval or tailoring to the trustee (para 13).
  • Looking at service provider conflicts only at appointment, not regularly (para 19).
  • A three-yearly review run by people who also run the framework, so it isn't operationally independent (para 20).
  • New directors or executives disclosing their interests only after they start (para 12).

How SPS 521 fits with other rules

  • SIS Act covenants: the priority rule in ss 52(2)(d) and 52A(2)(d), which SPS 521 supports, and the best financial interests duty.
  • SPS 510 and SPS 520: board governance, and the fit and proper standard, which defines who is a responsible person.
  • SPS 250: extra steps when the fund's insurer is connected to the trustee, including independent certification.
  • CPS 230: service provider management. SPS 521's footnote 7 still refers to SPS 231 Outsourcing, which CPS 230 replaced on 1 July 2025.
  • ASIC RG 181: trustees that also hold an Australian financial services licence must have adequate arrangements for managing conflicts under the Corporations Act (s 912A(1)(aa)).

What's proposed

APRA's draft single governance standard (CPS 510), released on 16 June 2026, would replace SPS 521 along with CPS 510, SPS 510, CPS 520 and SPS 520. In the draft, every APRA-regulated entity would have to identify and assess conflicts, including those from intra-group arrangements; avoid conflicts where the law requires it or where they can't be managed effectively; keep a register of actual and potential conflicts and the controls over them; minute conflicts and the action taken; and have its conflicts policy reviewed by operationally independent people at least annually. Super trustees would still keep registers of relevant duties and relevant interests, and would have to show in writing how each actual conflict was managed under the SIS Act priority rule (draft paras 74 to 76). Consultation has closed, APRA plans to finalise the standard by the end of 2026 and the new requirements would start from early 2028. Until then, SPS 521 applies. See CPS 510 and CPS 520 for the proposals.

Questions a board can ask

  • When did we last update our registers of relevant duties and interests, and who decides what is relevant?
  • Which conflicts came up at Board and committee meetings this year, and what did we do about each one?
  • Which of our service providers and advisers have links to the trustee, its group or our directors, and how do we monitor them?
  • Have we avoided any conflict outright in the past three years, or do we only ever manage them?
  • Who carried out our last comprehensive review, and how independent were they?

Frequently asked questions

Does SPS 521 ban conflicts?

No. It requires trustees to identify all potential and actual conflicts and to avoid or prudently manage them. Some conflicts must be avoided under the general law, and SPS 521 doesn't change that.

Does every interest have to go on the register?

No. The registers cover relevant duties and relevant interests: those that might reasonably be considered to have the potential to significantly affect someone's capacity to act in members' best interests. The trustee needs a documented process for making that call (para 16).

Is SPS 521 being replaced?

APRA has proposed folding it into a single cross-industry governance standard, CPS 510, with new requirements expected from early 2028. Until the new standard starts, SPS 521 applies.
