ASIC RG 104 General obligations and RG 105 Organisational competence

ASIC's guidance on the core, ongoing obligations every AFS licensee must meet, and on having competent responsible managers.

Beginner About 16 minutes Last reviewed: 7 October 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)
Status
Guidance on the law. The obligations are in s 912A of the Corporations Act.
Applies to
AFS licensees. Some obligations (such as adequate resources and risk management systems) don't apply to APRA-regulated bodies, which meet APRA's standards instead.

Key takeaways

  • Every AFS licensee must meet the general obligations in s 912A, starting with doing all things necessary to provide financial services efficiently, honestly and fairly.
  • RG 104 explains how ASIC assesses compliance with the general obligations.
  • RG 105 covers the organisational competence obligation: licensees must have people with the knowledge and skills to provide their financial services.
  • Licensees demonstrate competence through responsible managers: people with appropriate qualifications and experience who are directly responsible for significant day-to-day decisions.
  • Most of these general obligations are core obligations for breach reporting.

The general obligations (s 912A)

Obligation (summarised)What it means in practice
Efficiently, honestly and fairlyThe overarching standard for how services are provided. Courts have treated it as a broad obligation.
Comply with licence conditions and financial services lawsAn obligations register and compliance program.
Representatives complyTake reasonable steps to ensure representatives comply with the law: supervision and monitoring.
Conflicts of interestAdequate arrangements to manage conflicts. See RG 181.
CompetenceMaintain the competence to provide the services (RG 105).
TrainingRepresentatives are adequately trained and competent.
Resources and risk managementAdequate financial, technological and human resources, and adequate risk management systems (not applicable to APRA-regulated bodies). See RG 259 for fund operators.
Dispute resolutionInternal dispute resolution and AFCA membership for retail clients. See RG 271.
Compensation arrangementsArrangements for compensating retail clients, usually professional indemnity insurance (s 912B).

Organisational competence and responsible managers (RG 105)

ASIC assesses organisational competence mainly through a licensee's responsible managers (RMs). RMs must:

  • be directly responsible for significant day-to-day decisions about the licensed financial services;
  • together cover all the financial services and products under the licence;
  • have appropriate knowledge and skills, shown through relevant qualifications and experience (RG 105 sets out ASIC's expectations and options);
  • be of good fame and character.

Licensees must notify ASIC of changes to responsible managers. ASIC expects responsible managers to be genuinely involved in the business, not nominated in name only.

See Licensing basics for how licensing fits together across ASIC and APRA.

The general obligations in more detail

A summary in our own words; read s 912A and the guides for the exact obligations.

Obligation (s 912A and related)In plain English
Efficiently, honestly and fairlyProvide financial services in a way that is competent, honest and fair to clients. Courts have read this as a high-level, overarching standard.
Conflicts of interestHave adequate arrangements to manage conflicts (see RG 181).
Licence conditions and the lawComply with licence conditions and financial services laws, and take reasonable steps so representatives comply too.
ResourcesHave adequate financial, technological and human resources (unless APRA-regulated).
CompetenceMaintain the competence to provide the services, including through responsible managers (RG 105).
TrainingMake sure representatives are adequately trained and competent.
Dispute resolutionHave IDR (RG 271) and belong to AFCA for retail clients.
Risk managementHave adequate risk management systems (unless APRA-regulated).
Compensation arrangementsHave arrangements to compensate retail clients for losses from breaches, usually professional indemnity insurance (s 912B).

Why these obligations matter

  • They are core obligations for breach reporting, so a significant breach of them must be reported to ASIC (see RG 78).
  • Cyber security sits within them. In ASIC v RI Advice (2022) the Federal Court found that inadequate cyber security risk management breached s 912A; ASIC later obtained the first civil penalty on this basis against FIIG (2026). See the case study.
  • They underpin claims handling and remediation expectations, through the efficiently, honestly and fairly obligation.

Common mistakes

  • Responsible managers in name only, who aren't involved in the business.
  • Resourcing that doesn't grow with the business, especially compliance and technology.
  • Treating cyber security as an IT matter rather than a licence obligation.

Frequently asked questions

What is a responsible manager?

A person nominated on the AFS licence who has the knowledge and skills for the licensed business and is directly responsible for significant day-to-day decisions. RG 105 explains how ASIC assesses them.

Do APRA-regulated licensees have lighter obligations?

Some s 912A obligations (adequate resources and risk management systems) don't apply to APRA-regulated bodies because APRA's standards cover them. The other obligations apply in full.
