ISO/IEC 27001 Information security management

The international standard for an information security management system (ISMS): a structured, risk-based way to protect information, which organisations can be independently certified against.

Intermediate About 11 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026
Publisher
International Organization for Standardization and International Electrotechnical Commission (ISO/IEC)
Current edition
ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (with Amendment 1:2024 on climate action). The transition from the 2013 edition ended on 31 October 2025.
Status
Voluntary, but certifiable by accredited certification bodies. Often required by customers and in contracts.
Companion documents
ISO/IEC 27002:2022 (guidance on implementing the controls) and the wider ISO/IEC 27000 family.

Copyright note

ISO/IEC 27001 is copyrighted. This page describes it in our own words and doesn't reproduce its requirements or control text.

Key takeaways

  • An ISMS is a management system: policies, roles, risk assessment, controls, monitoring and continual improvement for information security.
  • The core requirements (clauses 4–10) follow the same structure as other ISO management system standards, broadly a plan–do–check–act cycle.
  • Annex A lists 93 reference controls in four themes: organisational (37), people (8), physical (14) and technological (34).
  • Organisations choose controls based on their risk assessment and record their choices in a Statement of Applicability.
  • Certification doesn't equal compliance with APRA's CPS 234, but the two overlap a lot.

How an ISMS works

Annex A control themes

ThemeNumber of controlsExamples of the areas covered (our summary)
Organisational37Policies, roles, asset management, access policy, supplier relationships, incident management, business continuity aspects, compliance
People8Screening, terms of employment, awareness and training, disciplinary process, remote working
Physical14Secure areas, equipment protection, clear desk and screen, disposal of media
Technological34Authentication, malware protection, vulnerability management, backup, logging and monitoring, secure development

The 2022 edition reorganised the previous 114 controls into these four themes and added some new ones, for example on threat intelligence, cloud services and data leakage prevention.

Key concepts

Scope
The boundary of the ISMS: which parts of the organisation, locations and systems it covers. Certificates only apply to the stated scope, so read it carefully.
Risk assessment and treatment
The organisation identifies information security risks, evaluates them against its criteria, and decides how to treat them.
Statement of Applicability (SoA)
A document listing the Annex A controls, whether each is applied or excluded, and why. It links risk treatment to controls.
Certification
An accredited certification body audits the ISMS (typically a two-stage initial audit), issues a certificate usually valid for three years, and carries out surveillance audits in between.

ISO/IEC 27001 and APRA's CPS 234

ISO/IEC 27001CPS 234
NatureVoluntary international standard; certifiableBinding prudential standard for APRA-regulated entities
ApproachManagement system with a reference control setOutcome-based requirements
Board roleTop management leadership and commitmentBoard explicitly ultimately responsible
Regulator notificationNot covered72 hours for material incidents; 10 business days for material control weaknesses
ScopeChosen by the organisationAll information assets, including those managed by third parties

A certified ISMS can provide useful evidence for CPS 234, and a service provider's ISO/IEC 27001 certificate is often part of third-party due diligence. But certification alone doesn't show CPS 234 compliance: the scope may be narrower, and CPS 234 has specific requirements (such as notification and internal audit review) that ISO/IEC 27001 doesn't. Many Australian organisations also use the Australian Signals Directorate's Essential Eight mitigation strategies alongside these.
