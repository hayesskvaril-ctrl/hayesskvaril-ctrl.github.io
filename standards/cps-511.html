CPS 511 Remuneration

APRA's standard for how banks, insurers and super trustees design and govern pay, so that incentives support sound risk management and good outcomes.

Intermediate About 8 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA)
Status
Binding prudential standard. Current version commenced 1 January 2024, including disclosure requirements; earlier versions applied from 2023.
Applies to
ADIs, general and life insurers, private health insurers and RSE licensees. Extra requirements apply to significant financial institutions.
Guidance
Prudential practice guide CPG 511 Remuneration.

Key takeaways

  • CPS 511 requires a board-approved remuneration framework that supports sound risk management and long-term outcomes, not just short-term profit.
  • Variable pay must be able to be reduced for poor risk or conduct outcomes, through in-period adjustments, malus and clawback.
  • Significant financial institutions face extra rules: a board remuneration committee, material weight on non-financial measures, and minimum deferral, with at least 60% of the CEO's variable pay deferred over six years.
  • The standard followed the Financial Services Royal Commission's findings that incentives had contributed to misconduct.

What CPS 511 requires

AreaRequirement (summarised)Applies to
Remuneration frameworkA board-approved remuneration framework and policy that promotes effective risk management, sustainable performance and long-term soundness, and supports the prevention and mitigation of conduct risk.All
Board oversightThe board is responsible for the framework and must approve remuneration outcomes for senior managers and certain other roles. SFIs must have a board remuneration committee.All; committee for SFIs
Non-financial measuresVariable remuneration must give material weight to non-financial measures (such as risk management and customer outcomes). No component may depend entirely on share price or profitability.Material weight requirement for SFIs
Adjustment toolsVariable remuneration must be able to be reduced for poor risk and conduct outcomes, through in-period adjustment, malus (reducing unvested amounts) and clawback (recovering paid amounts).All
DeferralAt least 60% of the CEO's variable remuneration deferred over at least six years, and at least 40% for other senior managers and executive directors over at least five years, vesting no faster than pro rata and only after four years.SFIs
ClawbackClawback must be available for senior managers, executive directors and highly paid material risk-takers, for a period after vesting.SFIs
ReviewPeriodic review of the framework's effectiveness, including independent review.All (more for SFIs)
DisclosurePublic disclosure of remuneration information.SFIs, with more limited requirements for others

The Remuneration governance page explains why these rules exist and includes an interactive deferral calculator. The Financial Accountability Regime sets separate minimum deferral rules for accountable persons.

Practical points

  • Make risk adjustment real. Regulators look at whether malus and clawback are actually used when things go wrong, not just whether the policy allows them.
  • Document the decisions. Keep records of how risk and conduct outcomes were considered for each senior executive's variable pay.
  • Connect to the risk function. The CRO and risk function should provide input on risk outcomes to the remuneration committee.
  • For super trustees, remuneration spending must also meet the best financial interests duty.
