CPS 511 Remuneration

APRA's standard for how banks, insurers and super trustees design and govern pay, so that incentives support sound risk management and good outcomes.

Intermediate About 14 minutes Last reviewed: 9 October 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA)
Status
Binding prudential standard. Current version commenced 1 January 2024, including disclosure requirements; earlier versions applied from 2023.
Applies to
ADIs, general and life insurers, private health insurers and RSE licensees. Extra requirements apply to significant financial institutions.
Guidance
Prudential practice guide CPG 511 Remuneration.

Key takeaways

  • CPS 511 requires a board-approved remuneration framework that supports sound risk management and long-term outcomes, not just short-term profit.
  • Variable pay must be able to be reduced for poor risk or conduct outcomes, through in-period adjustments, malus and clawback.
  • Significant financial institutions face extra rules: a board remuneration committee, material weight on non-financial measures, and minimum deferral, with at least 60% of the CEO's variable pay deferred over six years.
  • The standard followed the Financial Services Royal Commission's findings that incentives had contributed to misconduct.

What CPS 511 requires

AreaRequirement (summarised)Applies to
Remuneration frameworkA board-approved remuneration framework and policy that promotes effective risk management, sustainable performance and long-term soundness, and supports the prevention and mitigation of conduct risk.All
Board oversightThe board is responsible for the framework and must approve remuneration outcomes for senior managers and certain other roles. SFIs must have a board remuneration committee.All; committee for SFIs
Non-financial measuresVariable remuneration must give material weight to non-financial measures (such as risk management and customer outcomes). No component may depend entirely on share price or profitability.Material weight requirement for SFIs
Adjustment toolsVariable remuneration must be able to be reduced for poor risk and conduct outcomes, through in-period adjustment, malus (reducing unvested amounts) and clawback (recovering paid amounts).All
DeferralAt least 60% of the CEO's variable remuneration deferred over at least six years, and at least 40% for other senior managers and executive directors over at least five years, vesting no faster than pro rata and only after four years. Highly paid material risk-takers (total pay of $1 million or more) defer at least 40% over at least four years, vesting only after two. Deferral doesn't apply where the deferred amount would be under $50,000 in a year (paragraphs 41 to 43).SFIs
ClawbackClawback must be available for senior managers, executive directors and highly paid material risk-takers for at least two years after payment or vesting, even after the person leaves (paragraph 36).SFIs
ReviewPeriodic review of the framework's effectiveness, including independent review.All (more for SFIs)
DisclosurePublic disclosure of remuneration information.SFIs, with more limited requirements for others

The Remuneration governance page explains why these rules exist and includes an interactive deferral calculator. The Financial Accountability Regime sets separate minimum deferral rules for accountable persons.

Who counts as a significant financial institution (SFI)?

PointDetail
Why it mattersSFIs face the heavier CPS 511 requirements: a remuneration committee, material weight on non-financial measures, minimum deferral and clawback.
Banks (ADIs)APRA raised the asset threshold for ADIs to $20 billion (from $15 billion) when it finalised the standard. Since 1 July 2026 the threshold for banks is $30 billion (once a bank has been above it for 12 months), with a top tier of "most significant financial institutions" above $300 billion, as APRA confirmed on 27 May 2026.
Insurers and super trusteesDifferent criteria apply. APRA can also decide that an entity is an SFI because of its complexity or other factors.
ReviewAPRA has said it will revisit the thresholds, including when it reviews the standard (planned for 2027).

Worked example: applying malus

A senior manager at a large super trustee was awarded $200,000 of variable pay for 2024, with 40% ($80,000) deferred over five years. In 2026 an investigation finds that a fee overcharging problem started in her area during 2024, and that warning signs were missed.

  1. The remuneration committee reviews the deferred amounts against the trustee's risk adjustment policy.
  2. It considers the severity of the outcome, the manager's accountability, and whether she raised or ignored warnings.
  3. It applies malus, reducing the unvested $80,000, and records the reasons and the CRO's input.
  4. If the deferred amount weren't enough, the policy would also allow clawback of amounts already paid, for SFIs' senior managers.

Illustrative only. Real situations depend on the entity's own framework and facts.

What APRA found before the standard started

  • APRA's pre-implementation review of 39 entities found that about 80% needed the most uplift in remuneration design.
  • Gaps in how entities oversaw pay arrangements at third-party providers that perform material functions.
  • Consequence management processes that weren't rigorous enough to make sure poor risk outcomes actually affected pay.

Common mistakes

  • Non-financial measures with no real weight, such as a risk "gateway" that everyone passes.
  • Malus and clawback that are never used, even after serious incidents.
  • Little or no input from the risk function into remuneration outcomes.
  • Ignoring pay at service providers whose incentives can drive poor conduct.

Questions a board can ask

These are prompts for discussion, not requirements.

  1. When did we last reduce variable pay because of a risk or conduct outcome, and was it proportionate?
  2. How much weight do non-financial measures really carry in our scorecards?
  3. How does the CRO's view reach the remuneration committee?

Frequently asked questions

Does CPS 511 cap bonuses?

No. It doesn't set a maximum amount. It controls how variable pay is designed, deferred and adjusted.

How does CPS 511 relate to FAR deferral?

They overlap. The Financial Accountability Regime has its own minimum deferral rules for accountable persons. Entities need to meet both.

Practical points

  • Make risk adjustment real. Regulators look at whether malus and clawback are actually used when things go wrong, not just whether the policy allows them.
  • Document the decisions. Keep records of how risk and conduct outcomes were considered for each senior executive's variable pay.
  • Connect to the risk function. The CRO and risk function should provide input on risk outcomes to the remuneration committee.
  • For super trustees, remuneration spending must also meet the best financial interests duty.
