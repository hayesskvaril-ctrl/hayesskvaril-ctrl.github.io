SPS 515 Strategic Planning and Member Outcomes

APRA's standard requiring super trustees to plan strategically around member outcomes, spend members' money wisely, and check every year whether members are getting good results.

Intermediate About 8 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026
Issuer
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA)
Status
Binding prudential standard. Strengthened version in force since 1 July 2025.
Applies to
RSE licensees (trustees of APRA-regulated super funds).
Guidance
Prudential practice guide SPG 515.

Key takeaways

  • SPS 515 requires super trustees to set board-approved strategic objectives that support good outcomes for members, and a business plan to deliver them.
  • Trustees must manage financial resources so that spending is in members' best financial interests.
  • Trustees must assess member outcomes regularly, including the annual outcomes assessment, and review business performance each year.
  • APRA strengthened the standard from 1 July 2025 to tighten the link between strategy, spending and member outcomes.

What SPS 515 requires

AreaIn plain English
Strategic objectivesBoard-approved objectives for the business that support the outcomes the trustee wants for members.
Business planningA business plan that sets out how the objectives will be achieved, with initiatives, resources and risks, reviewed regularly.
Financial resource managementSpending and financial decisions must be tested against members' best financial interests, with monitoring of expenditure.
Member outcomesRegular monitoring and assessment of outcomes for members, including the annual outcomes assessment required by the SIS Act, comparing fees, returns, insurance and services with other products.
Business performance reviewAn annual review of the trustee's performance against its objectives, with action where outcomes fall short.
Notifying APRATelling APRA if a MySuper authorisation may be, or is to be, cancelled because of poor outcomes.

SPS 515 sits alongside the annual performance test for MySuper and trustee-directed products, and the best financial interests duty. See Superannuation trustee governance for how they fit together.

Practical points

  • Use the outcomes assessment for decisions, not just compliance. Poor results should lead to action: fee changes, product changes or mergers.
  • Evidence spending decisions. Significant expenditure, such as marketing, sponsorships and major projects, needs a documented rationale in members' best financial interests.
  • Link to risk management. Business plan risks should connect to the risk management framework under SPS 220 and to CPS 230.
