CPS 234 Information Security

APRA's standard requiring banks, insurers and super trustees to protect their information assets from cyber and other security threats, test that protection, and tell APRA quickly when things go wrong.

Intermediate About 12 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA)
Status
Binding prudential standard, in force since 1 July 2019.
Applies to
All APRA-regulated entities: banks, general and life insurers, private health insurers and RSE licensees.
Guidance
Prudential Practice Guide CPG 234 Information Security.
Works with
CPS 230 (operational resilience and service providers), CPS 220 (risk management framework).

Key takeaways

  • The board is ultimately responsible for information security.
  • Entities must know their information assets, classify them by criticality and sensitivity (including those held by third parties), and protect them with controls proportionate to the threats.
  • Controls must be tested through a systematic testing program, and internal audit must review them.
  • APRA must be notified within 72 hours of a material information security incident, and within 10 business days of a material control weakness that can't be fixed in a timely way.
  • APRA's independent assessments from 2022 found common gaps, including incomplete asset classification, weak third-party assessments and untested incident response plans.

The CPS 234 cycle

What it requires, part by part

AreaRequirement (summary)
Board and rolesThe board is ultimately responsible for information security. Roles and responsibilities for information security must be clearly defined, including for governing bodies and individuals.
CapabilityMaintain information security capability commensurate with the size and extent of threats to information assets, including where assets are managed by related or third parties.
Policy frameworkMaintain an information security policy framework that gives direction on responsibilities of everyone with obligations.
Information assetsClassify information assets, including those managed by related and third parties, by criticality and sensitivity.
ControlsImplement controls to protect information assets, commensurate with vulnerabilities and threats, criticality and sensitivity, stage in the asset lifecycle, and potential consequences. Evaluate third parties' information security controls where they manage information assets.
Incident managementHave robust mechanisms to detect and respond to incidents, and incident response plans that are reviewed and tested at least annually.
TestingTest the effectiveness of controls through a systematic testing program, with frequency and nature reflecting factors such as the rate of change in threats, and testing by appropriately skilled and functionally independent people. Report and escalate deficiencies.
Internal auditInternal audit must review the design and operating effectiveness of information security controls, including those maintained by third parties.
APRA notificationNotify APRA as soon as possible and no later than 72 hours after becoming aware of a material information security incident (including one notified to another regulator); and no later than 10 business days after becoming aware of a material control weakness the entity doesn't expect to remediate in a timely manner.

What APRA has found

In 2022 APRA began a program of independent "tripartite" assessments of CPS 234 compliance (involving APRA, the entity and an independent auditor). Findings published in 2023 identified six common gaps:

  1. incomplete identification and classification of critical and sensitive information assets;
  2. limited assessment of third parties' information security capability;
  3. inadequate definition and execution of control testing programs;
  4. incident response plans not regularly reviewed or tested;
  5. limited internal audit review of information security controls; and
  6. inconsistent and untimely reporting of material incidents and control weaknesses to APRA.

CPS 234 and CPS 230 together

CPS 234 focuses on protecting information; CPS 230 focuses on keeping critical operations running and managing service providers. A cyber attack on a key provider will usually engage both: CPS 234 for the security incident and control weaknesses, and CPS 230 for the disruption to critical operations and the service provider relationship. Notification timeframes can overlap (72 hours under both, 24 hours under CPS 230 for disruption outside tolerance).

Check your understanding

CPS 234 in practice

1. A fund's administrator tells it that attackers accessed a database of member records. The fund judges the incident material. When must APRA be told?

A material information security incident must be notified as soon as possible and no later than 72 hours. It also applies to assets managed by third parties. OAIC notification may also be needed.

2. Testing finds that multi-factor authentication is missing on a critical system, and the fix will take six months. When must APRA be told, if the weakness is material?

A material control weakness that can't be remediated in a timely manner must be notified within 10 business days of becoming aware.

3. Who should test the effectiveness of security controls?

CPS 234 requires testing by appropriately skilled and functionally independent people, as part of a systematic testing program.
