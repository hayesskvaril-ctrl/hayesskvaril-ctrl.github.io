ASIC RG 270 Whistleblower policies

ASIC's guidance on the whistleblower policy that public companies, large proprietary companies and super trustees must have.

Intermediate About 7 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)
Status
Guidance on the law. The requirement to have a policy is in s 1317AI of the Corporations Act (since 1 January 2020).
Applies to
Public companies, large proprietary companies, and proprietary companies that are trustees of registrable superannuation entities.

Key takeaways

  • Covered companies must have a whistleblower policy and make it available to officers and employees.
  • The Act sets out what the policy must cover: the protections available, how and to whom disclosures can be made, support and protection for whistleblowers, investigations, fair treatment of people mentioned, and how the policy is made available.
  • RG 270 gives ASIC's guidance and good practice tips on meeting these requirements.
  • ASIC's REP 827 (December 2025) surveyed 134 companies and found wide variation in practices. ASIC said it will follow up with companies falling short and focus on whether programs work, not just whether policies exist.

What the policy must cover

Required content (s 1317AI, summarised)Good practice from RG 270
The protections available to whistleblowers under the lawExplain them in plain English, including confidentiality and protection from detriment.
To whom disclosures can be made, and howOffer several channels, including anonymous and independent external options.
How the company will support whistleblowers and protect them from detrimentName who is responsible, and how risks of detriment are assessed and managed.
How disclosures will be investigatedSet out steps, likely timeframes and how the whistleblower will be kept informed.
How people mentioned in disclosures will be treated fairlyConfidentiality and a fair process for those involved.
How the policy will be made availablePublish it on the website and intranet, and include it in training.

What ASIC found in 2025

In REP 827, ASIC reported on a questionnaire of 134 companies across 18 industries covering July 2024 to June 2025. It found significant variation in the maturity of whistleblower programs. More than a third of participants didn't have a dedicated whistleblower web page, and companies with dedicated web pages and hotlines received more disclosures, which ASIC linked to the ease and anonymity of reporting. ASIC said it would contact companies whose practices fall short and increase its monitoring of how well programs work.

For the full picture of who is protected and what counts as a disclosable matter, see Whistleblower protections.
