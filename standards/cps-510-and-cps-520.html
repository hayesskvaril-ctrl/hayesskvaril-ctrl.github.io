CPS 510 Governance and CPS 520 Fit and Proper (and SPS equivalents)

APRA's core rules on how boards are structured and run, and on making sure the people in key roles have the competence, character and integrity to hold them.

Intermediate About 14 minutes Last reviewed: 9 October 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA)
Status
Binding prudential standards. APRA consulted in 2026 on a single, consolidated cross-industry governance standard that would replace these standards (see below).
Applies to
CPS 510 and CPS 520: ADIs and insurers. SPS 510 and SPS 520: RSE licensees. SPS 521 covers conflicts of interest for super trustees.
Guidance
Prudential practice guides including SPG 510 and SPG 520.

Key takeaways

  • The governance standards set minimum requirements for board size, independence, committees, board renewal and performance review.
  • The fit and proper standards require entities to assess the competence, character, diligence, honesty and integrity of "responsible persons", such as directors and senior managers, before appointment and at least annually.
  • Entities must have a fit and proper policy, keep records of assessments, and notify APRA if someone is assessed as not fit and proper.
  • APRA has proposed replacing these standards with one cross-industry governance standard, with proposals such as tenure limits for directors.

Governance: what the standards cover

AreaTypical requirements (summarised)
Board compositionMinimum board size; for banks and insurers, a majority of independent directors and an independent chair.
Board committeesA board audit committee and a remuneration committee (with a risk committee required under CPS 220 for many entities), with rules on membership and independence.
Board renewal and performancePolicies on director nomination, appointment, tenure and renewal, and regular assessment of the board's performance.
Internal and external auditAn independent internal audit function with access to the audit committee, and arrangements for the external auditor. See Internal audit.
Conflicts (super)SPS 521 requires RSE licensees to identify and manage conflicts of interest and duty.

Fit and proper: what the standards cover

  1. Identify responsible persons

    Directors, senior managers, the auditor and actuary (where relevant) and others in key roles.

  2. Set criteria

    Competence (skills, knowledge and experience for the role) and propriety (honesty, integrity, character and diligence, including checks such as criminal history and bankruptcy).

  3. Assess

    Before appointment and at least annually, with documented evidence.

  4. Act and notify

    Take action if someone isn't fit and proper, and notify APRA within 10 business days of such an assessment.

Accountable persons under the Financial Accountability Regime must also meet FAR's own requirements.

Proposed changes

On 16 June 2026 APRA released a draft of a new, single CPS 510 Governance for consultation. It would replace CPS 510, SPS 510, CPS 520, SPS 520 and SPS 521. Submissions closed on 28 August 2026. APRA plans to finalise the standard and its guide (CPG 510) by the end of 2026, with the new requirements starting from early 2028. Until then, the current standards apply. See What APRA's proposed governance standard would change.

Main proposals in the June 2026 draft

Summary based on APRA's release and law firm analyses of the draft. The final standard may differ.

AreaProposal (summary)
TenureA maximum of 12 years for non-executive directors at an entity (raised from 10 years in APRA's 2025 discussion paper), including non-consecutive terms and time at a predecessor entity after a merger. The board could extend by up to 12 months in exceptional circumstances and would have to tell APRA.
Board sizeA minimum of five directors for all entities, including super trustees (with allowance for filling vacancies under the super equal representation rules).
IndependenceA new definition of an independent director aligned with the ASX Corporate Governance Principles. The majority-independent rule would extend to boards of entities with an APRA-regulated parent. Super trustees would not have to have a majority of independent directors or an independent chair.
CommitteesSeparate risk and audit committees for significant super trustees, and no longer required for smaller banks and insurers.
Fit and properFolded into CPS 510, with a clearer policy and defined triggers for reassessment, but less reporting to APRA because the Financial Accountability Regime now covers senior executives.
ConflictsA register of relevant duties and interests for all entities; banks and insurers would not have to publish it.
Board performanceStronger annual reviews of the board, committees and directors, with an independent external review every three years for significant institutions.

Questions a board can ask

These are prompts for discussion, not requirements.

  1. Which of our directors would reach a 12-year limit, and what is our renewal plan?
  2. How do we assess whether each director is independent in fact, not just by definition?
  3. When did a fit and proper reassessment last happen outside the annual cycle, and what triggered it?
  4. Does our board skills matrix drive appointment decisions, or is it filled in afterwards?

Frequently asked questions

Does the Financial Accountability Regime replace fit and proper?

No. FAR sets obligations for accountable persons, and the fit and proper standards cover a wider group of responsible persons. APRA's draft would reduce duplicate reporting between the two, not remove fit and proper assessments.

Do the proposed tenure limits apply now?

No. They are proposals in a draft standard. Current CPS 510 requires a board renewal policy that considers whether long service could interfere with a director's independence, and current SPS 510 requires a super trustee's policies to set a maximum tenure limit for each director. Neither sets a fixed number of years: the board chooses its own limit (CPS 510 paragraph 45; SPS 510 paragraph 23).
