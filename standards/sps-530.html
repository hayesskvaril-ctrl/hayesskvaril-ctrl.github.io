SPS 530 Investment Governance

APRA's standard for how super trustees invest members' money: setting objectives and strategies, choosing and monitoring investments, valuing them reliably, keeping enough liquidity to pay members, and stress testing the lot.

Intermediate About 22 minutes Last reviewed: 2 October 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA)
Status
Binding prudential standard. The current version was finalised on 19 July 2022 and has applied since 1 January 2023, with stronger valuation, liquidity and stress testing requirements. The first version applied from 1 July 2013. APRA consulted on further changes in 2026 (see below).
Applies to
All RSE licensees (trustees of APRA-regulated super funds), for every investment option they offer, including MySuper products and platform menus. Not self-managed super funds.
Guidance
Prudential practice guide SPG 530 Investment Governance (final version released 20 July 2023), covering liquidity, stress testing, valuation and how to consider environmental, social and governance (ESG) risks. Guidance is not binding, but APRA uses it when supervising.
Main related law
Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993 (SIS Act): the investment covenant (s 52(6)) and the best financial interests duty (s 52(2)(c)).

Key takeaways

  • SPS 530 requires a board-approved investment governance framework: how investments are chosen, managed, monitored, valued and reviewed, in members' best financial interests.
  • Each investment option needs return and risk objectives and an investment strategy that considers risk and return, diversification, liquidity and whether reliable valuations are available.
  • Since 2023 the standard has detailed requirements for valuation governance: a board-approved valuation policy, valuation methods for each asset class, triggers for revaluing between scheduled valuations, and when to get independent valuations.
  • Each fund needs a board-approved liquidity management plan, and a comprehensive stress testing program run at least annually, including liquidity stress tests.
  • The whole framework must be reviewed by operationally independent, competent people at least every three years.
  • APRA found in December 2024 that 12 of 23 large trustees needed material improvement in valuation governance, liquidity risk management or both. In 2026 it began requiring independent deep-dive reviews of valuation governance at selected large trustees.
  • After the Shield and First Guardian collapses, APRA consulted in June 2026 on strengthening SPS 530, including member-level investment limits on platforms. The proposals are not yet law.

SPS 530 in plain English

Most of a super fund's job is investing. Members hand over their savings for decades, usually through a default option they never chose, and the trustee decides where the money goes. SPS 530 sets the discipline for that. The trustee decides what each investment option is trying to achieve, builds a strategy to get there, checks each investment carefully before buying, watches it afterwards, values it fairly, keeps enough cash-like assets to pay members who leave or switch, and tests what would happen in a crisis. The board approves the framework and has an independent check of it at least every three years.

Two things make this harder than it sounds. First, funds hold growing amounts of unlisted assets (property, infrastructure, private equity and private credit), which have no daily market price. Second, members can switch options or leave at short notice. Get valuations wrong, and members who leave or switch are paid too much or too little at the expense of those who stay. Run short of liquid assets in a crisis, and the fund may have to sell good assets cheaply.

The requirements, one by one

This section explains each part of the standard in plain English. It summarises the standard rather than quoting it; read the standard itself for the exact wording and paragraph numbers. The framework must suit the size, business mix and complexity of the trustee's business.

1. The investment governance framework

The trustee must develop, implement and maintain an investment governance framework: the structures, policies, processes and people for making, monitoring and reviewing investment decisions, consistent with members' best financial interests. The 2023 version made clear that valuation governance and stress testing are part of this framework, not separate add-ons. The board must receive regular reporting for each investment option and MySuper product; this does not have to include detail on every individual investment.

The framework's appropriateness, effectiveness and adequacy must be comprehensively reviewed at least every three years by people who are operationally independent, appropriately trained and competent.

2. Objectives and investment strategy for each option

For each investment option, the trustee sets investment objectives, including return and risk objectives, and an investment strategy to achieve them. This puts into practice the SIS Act investment covenant (s 52(6)), which requires a strategy for the fund as a whole and for each option, having regard to:

  • the risk and likely return of the investments, given the strategy's objectives and the fund's expected cash flows;
  • the composition of the investments, including diversification and the risks of inadequate diversification;
  • the liquidity of the investments, given expected cash flow needs; and
  • whether reliable valuation information is available,

among other matters set out in the Act. APRA's guidance also explains how trustees are expected to consider material ESG risks, such as climate risk, as part of overall investment risk management.

3. Selecting investments: due diligence

Before investing, the trustee must carry out due diligence that is proportionate to the investment's size, risk and complexity. That covers the investment itself, the manager or provider, fees and costs, liquidity, how it will be valued, conflicts of interest, and how it fits the option's strategy. Platform trustees, who offer menus of thousands of investments, have the same duty for what they put on the menu.

4. Monitoring

After investing, the trustee monitors each option's performance against its objectives, its investment managers and the risks, and acts when things go off track: for example, placing an investment on a watch list, reducing exposure or removing it. Monitoring and onboarding were at the heart of the Shield and First Guardian failures: the Federal Court declared that Macquarie, as platform trustee, contravened the law by failing to place the Shield options on a watch list, and ASIC has alleged onboarding failures by another trustee (see the case study).

5. Valuation governance

The trustee must have an adequate valuation governance framework: the structures, processes, procedures and controls needed to identify and manage valuation risk. At its centre is a board-approved valuation policy.

What the valuation policy must cover, at a minimum (summary)
ItemIn plain English
Roles and responsibilitiesWho oversees and runs valuations: the board, board committees and senior management.
ReportingThe key metrics and information reported to the board, committees and management, and how often.
MethodsThe valuation method for each asset class (and sub-class or holding vehicle where relevant), including where the inputs come from.
Independent valuationsWhen independent external valuations are obtained.
ValidationHow valuations are checked, including back-testing (for example, comparing past valuations with actual sale prices).
Accept, reject or reassessWhen a valuation is accepted, rejected or redone, with an escalation process for disputed valuations.
ReviewHow the policy is kept effective over time.

APRA expects unlisted assets to be valued at least quarterly, and more often when conditions change. Its guidance says trustees can best show they meet the standard by using both quantitative revaluation triggers (such as a set market movement since the last valuation) and qualitative ones (such as a major event affecting the asset). APRA can also require a trustee to obtain an independent external valuation.

6. Liquidity management

The trustee must have a board-approved liquidity management plan for each fund, covering each investment option. It sets out how liquidity is measured and managed day to day, who is responsible for managing and overseeing liquidity risk, and the key metrics reported to and reviewed by the board. Liquidity matters because members can switch options, roll over to another fund or retire, and because currency hedging and investment commitments can call for cash at the worst moment. APRA's guidance expects trigger frameworks that warn of potential liquidity stress and action plans that say what the trustee will do. For more, see Liquidity stress testing for super funds.

7. Stress testing

The trustee needs a comprehensive investment stress testing program, approved by the board and built into the investment governance framework. It applies adverse scenarios to each option's strategy, is completed at least annually, and includes liquidity stress testing as part of both the program and the liquidity management plan. The methodology and assumptions must also be reviewed at least annually so they keep up with market conditions, investment strategy and anything that changes an option's liquidity needs. Good scenarios combine stresses: a market fall, heavy switching out of an option and a currency move all at once. See Scenario analysis and stress testing.

Why valuation and liquidity matter so much

Unlisted asset values aren't set by a market every day, so they can lag. When listed markets fall sharply, unlisted values often fall later and by less, at least on paper. If members leave or switch while unlisted assets are overvalued, they are paid out at too high a price, and the members who stay bear the difference. When this happens in reverse, those leaving lose out. Selling liquid assets to pay leaving members also pushes up the unlisted share of what remains (the denominator effect, shown in the video above), which can push an option outside its strategy's limits just when it is hardest to fix. That is why valuation and liquidity sit side by side in SPS 530: both are about treating members fairly.

Worked example: setting revaluation triggers

This is an illustrative example for learning only. The fund, assets and thresholds are made up; each trustee sets its own.

A trustee holds unlisted infrastructure, property and private equity, valued quarterly by external valuers or the managers. Its valuation policy adds off-cycle triggers:

Trigger typeExample triggerWhat happens
Quantitative: marketA listed proxy index for the asset class moves more than a set percentage since the last valuation.The valuation committee asks the manager or valuer for an interim update and considers an adjustment.
Quantitative: rates and currencyA large move in long-term interest rates (affecting discount rates) or in the Australian dollar.The impact is estimated; a revaluation is requested if it is material.
Qualitative: asset eventLoss of a major tenant, a regulatory change, a capital raising or sale at a different price, or a manager's own write-down.Revaluation is required before the next unit price strike if the effect could be material.
Qualitative: liquidity eventHeavy switching out of an option holding the asset.Valuations are reviewed urgently, because stale prices now transfer value between members.

Each trigger is recorded, its outcome reported to the valuation committee, and back-testing later compares valuations with actual transaction prices. Errors that reach unit prices may need correcting and compensating: see Unit pricing and unit pricing errors.

What APRA has found in practice

Canva valuation review (September 2023)

APRA chose Canva, a high-profile private technology company, for a targeted review of unlisted asset valuation governance after investment markets deteriorated during 2022. It found most trustees' governance of their Canva valuations appropriate, but identified areas for improvement. Better practice included questioning the valuation approaches of appointed investment managers, and adjusting valuations to reflect the trustee's own circumstances and policies.

Thematic review of unlisted valuation and liquidity (December 2024)

Starting in December 2023, APRA assessed responses and data from 23 trustees covering about 80% of APRA-regulated super assets. It found that 12 of the 23 needed material improvement in valuation governance, liquidity risk management or both, and said it would engage directly with them on remediation and take further action where necessary.

Independent deep-dive reviews (2026–27)

APRA's 2026–27 corporate plan says it will require selected large trustees to appoint an independent party to review their valuation governance for unlisted assets, testing whether their processes support reliable valuations and fair outcomes for members, and will hold trustees to account for timely remediation where it finds material risks.

Proposed changes (2026 consultation)

On 16 June 2026 APRA released a consultation paper, Strengthening superannuation investment governance, responding to risks shown by the Shield and First Guardian collapses and by platform and other member-directed investment models. APRA noted that platform products are around 15% of APRA-regulated super assets but trustees offering them accounted for 70% of announced super enforcement actions in 2025–26. The proposals include:

  • Member-level investment limits: where members can choose specific investments, trustees would set and enforce limits to reduce the risk of severe loss from concentrated holdings in higher-risk options.
  • Third-party conflicts: stronger management of conflicts involving third parties such as advisers and promoters.
  • Resources and capability: trustee resources matched to the size, complexity and nature of their investment offerings.
  • Codifying, and in places strengthening, expectations on onboarding, ongoing monitoring, timely remediation, reliable valuations and clear accountability.

Consultation closed on 28 August 2026, and APRA proposed that the new requirements start on 1 January 2028. These are proposals: check APRA's website for the final position.

Common mistakes

  • Accepting managers' valuations without challenge, especially for private equity and private credit.
  • Revaluing only on the calendar, with no clear triggers for off-cycle revaluations when markets move.
  • Valuation committees that aren't independent of the investment team whose performance depends on the valuations.
  • Stress tests that test one thing at a time, missing the combination of market falls, switching and currency hedging calls.
  • Liquidity triggers with no action plan, so the board learns of a problem but nobody knows what to do.
  • Onboarding investments to a platform menu without enough due diligence, or failing to watch-list them when warning signs appear.
  • Board reports that show returns but not risk, liquidity or valuation uncertainty.

Questions a board can ask

These are prompts for discussion, not requirements.

  1. What share of each option is in unlisted assets, and how does that change in our stress scenarios?
  2. When did we last revalue each major unlisted asset, and what triggers would prompt an earlier revaluation?
  3. How do we challenge the valuations our managers give us? When did we last reject or adjust one?
  4. Could we pay a sudden wave of switching and rollovers in a market crash without forced selling or breaching our strategy limits?
  5. What did our last stress testing program show, and what did we change because of it?
  6. When was our investment governance framework last independently reviewed, and what did it find?
  7. If we run a platform, how do we decide what goes on the menu, and what would make us take something off?

Frequently asked questions

Does SPS 530 apply to platform (wrap) super products?

Yes. It applies to every investment option an RSE licensee offers, including investment menus on platforms. APRA's 2026 consultation proposes further requirements aimed specifically at platform and member-directed options.

Must unlisted assets be valued every day?

No. APRA expects valuations at least quarterly, with off-cycle revaluations when triggers are met.

Is a liquidity stress test the same as the stress testing program?

No. The stress testing program covers adverse scenarios for each option's investment strategy. Liquidity stress testing is one required part of it, and also part of the liquidity management plan.

Does SPS 530 require ESG investing?

No. It doesn't require any particular investment approach. APRA's guidance explains how trustees should consider material ESG risks, such as climate risk, as part of managing investment risk in members' best financial interests.

Are the 2026 proposals in force?

No. They were proposals in a consultation that closed on 28 August 2026, with a proposed start of 1 January 2028. Check APRA's website for the final requirements.
