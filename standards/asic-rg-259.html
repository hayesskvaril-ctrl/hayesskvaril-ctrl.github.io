ASIC RG 259 Risk management systems of fund operators

ASIC's expectations for how responsible entities of managed funds (and corporate directors of CCIVs) identify, assess and manage risk, both for their own business and for each fund they run.

Intermediate About 14 minutes Last reviewed: 8 October 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)
Status
Guidance on how to meet the legal obligation in s 912A(1)(h) of the Corporations Act to have adequate risk management systems. The obligation is law; the guide explains ASIC's view of what "adequate" means for fund operators.
Applies to
AFS licensees that are responsible entities of registered managed investment schemes, and corporate directors of corporate collective investment vehicles (CCIVs).
History
First issued in March 2017 as "Risk management systems of responsible entities". Updated and renamed in October 2022 to cover CCIVs.

Key takeaways

  • Every AFS licensee must have adequate risk management systems. RG 259 explains what ASIC expects from fund operators in particular, because they manage other people's money through pooled funds.
  • Risks must be managed at two levels: the operator's own business, and each fund (scheme) it operates.
  • Operators should document their risk appetite and tolerance for each material risk, and review the whole framework at least once a year.
  • ASIC singles out liquidity risk: operators should stress test or run scenario analysis on each fund's liquidity at least annually, and more often where the fund's profile calls for it.
  • The board, risk committee and compliance committee should receive regular risk reporting, with clear escalation of issues. The approach should be proportionate to the nature, scale and complexity of the business.

Why fund operators get their own guide

A responsible entity (RE) runs a pooled fund on behalf of investors, who usually have no say in day-to-day decisions. If the RE mismanages risk, investors can lose money or be unable to get their money out. Fund collapses and frozen funds, such as those seen during the global financial crisis, showed that some REs lacked robust systems for risks like liquidity and valuation. RG 259 sets a clearer benchmark for what adequate risk management looks like in funds management.

RG 259 sits alongside other ASIC guidance for fund operators, including RG 132 on compliance plans and oversight. The compliance plan focuses on meeting legal obligations; the risk management system covers all material risks to the business and to each fund. The two should work together. See Managed investment schemes for how the whole structure fits together.

Two levels of risk

What RG 259 covers

TopicSummary
Establishing the systemA documented risk management framework suited to the operator's business and the funds it runs, with clear roles and responsibilities, and the board ultimately accountable. Size and complexity drive how elaborate it needs to be.
Risk appetiteA documented appetite, with a tolerance set for each identified material risk, so it is clear when a risk needs action or escalation.
Identifying and assessing risksIdentify, analyse, evaluate and record risks at both the operator and fund level, including how each is treated. Consider changes in markets, products and the business.
Managing risksPut in place and monitor controls and treatments, and check they are working.
Liquidity riskUnderstand each fund's liquidity profile against how and when investors can withdraw, and stress test or run scenarios at least annually. Approaches include sensitivity analysis, historical scenarios, hypothetical scenarios and reverse stress testing (finding the scenario that would cause failure).
Reporting and escalationRegular risk reporting to the board, risk committee and compliance committee (as relevant), and a clear path for escalating issues.
ReviewReview the risk management framework at least annually, or more often if the business or funds warrant it.

Practical points

  • Keep fund-level registers. A single business-wide risk register often misses fund-specific risks. Many operators keep a register or risk profile for each fund, or for groups of similar funds.
  • Match liquidity to redemption terms. A fund offering daily withdrawals while holding property, private credit or other hard-to-sell assets carries a liquidity mismatch that needs particular attention and stress testing.
  • Outsourcing doesn't transfer responsibility. REs commonly use external investment managers, custodians and administrators, but remain responsible for the fund. Oversight of these providers is part of the risk system. See Third-party risk.
  • Link risk and compliance. Risk assessments should inform the compliance plan, and compliance breaches and incidents should feed back into risk ratings.
  • Evidence the review. Keep records of the annual framework review, stress test results and board discussion. ASIC's surveillance commonly asks to see them.

For APRA-regulated entities, the equivalent expectations are in CPS 220 Risk Management (and SPS 220 for super). RG 259 is the ASIC benchmark for fund operators, many of which are not APRA-regulated.

Worked example: a small responsible entity's main risks

RG 259 expects risk management to cover both the RE's own business and each scheme it operates. An illustrative summary:

RiskExample controls
Liquidity (fund level)Liquidity policy, regular stress tests, withdrawal terms that match assets
ValuationIndependent valuations, valuation policy, review of unusual movements
Key personSuccession plans and documented processes
Service providersDue diligence and monitoring of custodians, administrators and investment managers
Conflicts and related partiesConflicts register, independent review of related-party transactions
ComplianceCompliance plan, compliance committee, breach reporting process

Frequently asked questions

Does RG 259 apply to wholesale fund managers?

RG 259 is directed at responsible entities of registered schemes. Wholesale managers still have general licensee obligations, including adequate risk management systems.

How often should the risk management system be reviewed?

RG 259 expects regular review, at least annually, and when circumstances change significantly. Check the guide for ASIC's detailed expectations.
