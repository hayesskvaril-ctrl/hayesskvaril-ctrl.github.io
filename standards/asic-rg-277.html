ASIC RG 277 Consumer remediation

ASIC's guidance on how licensees should put things right when their conduct harms consumers: promptly, fairly, and without making people ask.

Intermediate About 10 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)
Status
Guidance, underpinned by the obligation to provide services efficiently, honestly and fairly. Applies to remediations started on or after 27 September 2022 (replacing RG 256).
Applies to
AFS licensees (including super trustees) and Australian credit licensees.

Key takeaways

  • Remediation should start promptly and proactively once a licensee identifies that its conduct may have caused consumer loss, without waiting for complaints or regulator action.
  • Everyone who has or may have been affected should be included in scope, automatically where possible, and remediation should be free for consumers.
  • Where information is missing, licensees should use assumptions that are beneficial to consumers.
  • Compensation should put consumers back in the position they would have been in, including for the time value of money.
  • For former customers owed $5 or less, and money that can't be returned, RG 277 sets out options for residual payments.

The remediation process

Key expectations

AreaWhat RG 277 expects (summary)
When to startInitiate remediation when there are reasonable grounds to suspect that conduct may have caused consumer loss; don't wait for complaints, AFCA or ASIC.
ScopeInclude all consumers who have or may have been affected; don't narrow scope artificially.
Beneficial assumptionsWhere data is incomplete, use assumptions that favour consumers, so people don't fall out of scope or get underpaid.
CompensationReturn consumers to the position they would have been in, including foregone returns or interest.
Free and easyNo cost to consumers; minimise what consumers need to do; clear communication.
Returning moneyPay current customers regardless of amount. For former customers owed $5 or less (after interest), a residual remediation payment may be made without reasonable endeavours to locate them.
Residual fundsWhere money can't be returned and lodging it as unclaimed money isn't feasible, licensees (other than those holding pooled funds) may pay it to registered charities or not-for-profits, within set timeframes.
GovernanceBoard and senior management oversight, clear accountability, record keeping and quality assurance.

Super-specific points

  • Remediation often means correcting account balances and restoring lost investment earnings, not just refunding a fee.
  • Members may have rolled over to another fund, retired or died; tracing and paying them (or their estates) needs careful processes.
  • Where remediation is funded, trustees need to consider who bears the cost (for example, the trustee's own resources rather than other members) and document that decision.
