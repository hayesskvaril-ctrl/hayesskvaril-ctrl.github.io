SPS 250 Insurance in Superannuation

APRA's standard for how super trustees manage the life, total and permanent disability and income protection insurance they make available to members.

Intermediate About 15 minutes Last reviewed: 10 October 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA)
Status
Binding prudential standard. The current version (F2022L00741) has applied since 1 July 2022.
Applies to
All RSE licensees (trustees of APRA-regulated super funds) that make insured benefits available to members, whether through an insurer or, for some defined benefit funds, by self-insuring.
Main related law
Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993 (SIS Act): the insurance covenants (s 52(7)), the best financial interests duty (s 52(2)(c)) and the default cover rules for inactive, low-balance and young members' accounts (ss 68AAA to 68AAF).

Key takeaways

  • Trustees must have an insurance management framework, owned by the Board, covering the insurance strategy, premiums, administration, underwriting, claims, declined cover and how members opt out.
  • The insurance strategy must document how the trustee has regard to the SIS Act factors, how cover is monitored and renewed, its approach to claims and how conflicts are managed.
  • Each insurance arrangement must cover a set list of terms, including service standards, annual claims information and, for arrangements made since 1 July 2022, a right to terminate if it is not in members' best financial interests.
  • Insurers must be chosen through a documented selection process and due diligence, at arm's length, and then monitored against service levels.
  • If the insurer is a connected entity, the trustee needs an independent certification before entering, renewing or materially changing the arrangement and at least every three years, given to APRA within 10 business days.

SPS 250 in plain English

Most super members get default insurance through their fund, and the premiums come out of their retirement savings. That makes the trustee a buyer of insurance on members' behalf, with real conflicts and costs at stake. The SIS Act sets the core duties: the trustee must have an insurance strategy, consider the cost to all members, offer only cover that doesn't inappropriately erode retirement income, and do everything reasonable to pursue a claim with a reasonable prospect of success (s 52(7)). SPS 250 adds the prudential detail: the framework, documents, insurer selection and monitoring, and the extra safeguards when the insurer is related to the trustee.

The requirements, one by one

AreaWhat SPS 250 requiresParagraphs
FrameworkAn insurance management framework suited to the trustee's size, business mix and complexity and the types of cover, with the Board ultimately responsible. People in insurance roles must know it, with controls to monitor compliance.9 to 12, 14
Framework contentsAt a minimum: the insurance strategy; policies on recovering premiums from the fund, monitoring insurance administration, underwriting and claims assessment; roles and reporting lines; a review process; a policy for declined applications, reduced or restricted cover, terminations and reinstatement requests; and an easy way for members to opt out, with how that is communicated.13
RecordsRecords detailed enough for APRA reporting and for a prospective insurer to assess the cover, including at least five years of claims experience, membership, sums insured and premiums.15
Risk managementInsurance is treated as a material risk area in the SPS 220 risk management framework.16
Insurance strategyBesides the SIS Act requirements, document how each s 52(7) factor is considered, how cover is monitored, reviewed and renewed, the approach to claims management (whoever handles claims) and how conflicts are managed.17
Insurance arrangementsThe policy and related agreements must address, at a minimum: level and type of cover and exclusions; term; automatic acceptance limits; opt-in or opt-out; eligibility, cessation and reinstatement; premium structure; claims notification and payment; dispute resolution; service standards and reporting against them; complete annual claims information; liability and indemnity; review and renewal; and termination, including a right to terminate if the arrangement is not in members' best financial interests (for arrangements entered into from 1 July 2022).18, 19
Fair status rulesBe able to show APRA that rules for giving members a status in connection with insurance (for example, which cohort they fall into) are fair and reasonable.20
Telling APRANotify APRA as soon as practicable of issues that might materially affect the ability to provide insured benefits, and of any terminated arrangement, with the transition plan.21, 22

The engagement must be at arm's length and in members' best financial interests.23, 24

Give it to APRA within 10 business days. APRA can also require a certification for any arrangement.25 to 28
MonitoringEnough resources to manage the insurer relationship, including regular contact at the right level and monitoring of performance against service levels, reported to senior management.29

The default cover rules in the SIS Act

SPS 250 sits alongside rules in the SIS Act that stop default cover being provided in some situations unless the member chooses it in writing. They apply to MySuper and choice products in funds with more than six members:

SituationRuleSIS Act
Inactive accountsNo cover if the account has been inactive for a continuous period of 16 months, unless the member has elected to keep it.s 68AAA
Low balancesNo cover if the balance is under $6,000 and has not reached $6,000 since 1 November 2019, unless the member has elected to have it.s 68AAB
Members under 25No cover for members under 25 unless they have elected to have it.s 68AAC
ExceptionsExceptions apply, including for some employer-sponsor contribution arrangements and members in dangerous occupations.ss 68AAE, 68AAF

Worked example: renewing cover with a connected insurer

A trustee's group life insurance is provided by an insurer in the same corporate group, and the arrangement is due for renewal with higher premiums.

  1. Run the documented selection process: compare the connected insurer's terms, exclusions, claims philosophy and premium reasonableness with alternatives, and do due diligence (para 23).
  2. Record why the engagement is at arm's length and in members' best financial interests, including the effect of the higher premiums on retirement balances (para 24; SIS Act s 52(7)).
  3. Obtain an independent certification before renewing, and give it to APRA within 10 business days of receiving it (paras 25 to 27).
  4. Check the renewed arrangement contains the minimum terms, including service standards, annual claims data and a termination right if it stops being in members' best financial interests (para 19).
  5. Report performance against service levels to senior management during the term (para 29), and diarise the next certification (at least every three years).

An illustrative application for learning, not advice about any trustee's obligations.

Common mistakes

  • An insurance strategy that restates s 52(7) without showing how each factor was actually weighed for the fund's membership.
  • Claims are left to the insurer and administrator with no trustee monitoring of timeliness and outcomes.
  • Older arrangements renewed without checking the minimum terms, such as annual claims data and termination rights.
  • Insurance administration errors (for example, cover not switched off for inactive accounts) not caught because nobody tests the rules end to end.
  • The connected-insurer certification is obtained after renewal, or not given to APRA on time.

How SPS 250 fits with other rules

  • SPS 515: the annual outcomes assessment includes whether the insurance strategy and insurance fees are appropriate for members.
  • CPS 230: insurance administration and claims services provided by service providers fall under its service provider requirements (CPS 230 replaced SPS 231, which SPS 250's footnote still mentions).
  • SPS 521: conflicts from insurance arrangements are managed through the trustee's conflicts management framework (see conflicts of interest).
  • Claims handling: handling and settling claims is also a financial service regulated by ASIC (see life insurance).

Questions a board can ask

  • How much do premiums reduce typical members' retirement balances, and how did we decide that is not inappropriate erosion?
  • How quickly are death, TPD and income protection claims decided, and how many are declined or disputed?
  • Do our insurance arrangements contain every term SPS 250 requires, including a termination right?
  • When was the framework last independently reviewed, and what did it find?
