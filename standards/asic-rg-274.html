ASIC RG 274 Product design and distribution obligations

ASIC's guidance on the design and distribution obligations (DDO): issuers must design products for a defined target market, and everyone in the chain must take reasonable steps so products reach the right people.

Intermediate About 20 minutes Last reviewed: 7 October 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)
Status
Guidance on Part 7.8A of the Corporations Act (in force since 5 October 2021). Current version: September 2024 (first issued December 2020).
Applies to
Issuers and distributors of most financial products for retail clients, including super (other than MySuper), insurance, managed funds and credit products.

Key takeaways

  • Issuers must make a target market determination (TMD) before distribution, describing who the product is likely to be consistent with, distribution conditions and review triggers.
  • Issuers and distributors must take reasonable steps so that distribution is consistent with the TMD.
  • Issuers must monitor, review TMDs when triggers occur, and notify ASIC of significant dealings inconsistent with the TMD within 10 business days.
  • Some products are excluded, including MySuper products and ordinary shares.
  • DDO is about outcomes, not documents: ASIC expects genuine product governance.

The product governance cycle

What a TMD must cover

ElementIn brief
Target marketThe class of retail clients the product would likely be consistent with, considering their likely objectives, financial situation and needs.
Distribution conditionsRestrictions on how the product is distributed (e.g. only through advice, or with eligibility checks).
Review triggers and periodsEvents that suggest the TMD may no longer be appropriate (e.g. complaints spikes, significant dealings, poor outcomes), and maximum periods between reviews.
Reporting by distributorsWhat information distributors must report to the issuer (e.g. complaints, dealings outside the target market) and when.

Practical points

  • Be specific. A target market of "anyone who wants insurance" is not meaningful. ASIC has used stop orders against TMDs it considered inadequate.
  • Negative target markets help. Describing who the product is not suitable for makes distribution controls clearer.
  • Watch the data. Claims ratios, cancellation rates, complaints and investment outcomes are signs of whether the product is working for its market.
  • Marketing is distribution. Advertising that targets people outside the target market can be inconsistent with the TMD.
  • Super: choice products and insurance within super are generally covered, but MySuper products are excluded.

Who must do what

RoleMain obligations (summary)
Issuer (product manufacturer)Make a target market determination (TMD) before distribution; take reasonable steps so distribution is consistent with it; review it when review triggers occur; keep records; notify ASIC of significant dealings outside the target market within 10 business days.
DistributorDon't distribute without a TMD; take reasonable steps so distribution is consistent with the TMD; collect and report complaints and other information to the issuer; notify the issuer of significant dealings outside the target market.

What ASIC has done

ASIC has used the design and distribution obligations actively:

ActionWhat happened
Stop ordersASIC can stop a product being offered while a TMD is inadequate. It made its first interim stop orders in July 2022 and had issued about 99 interim stop orders and two final stop orders by September 2026. Most interim orders were revoked after issuers fixed their TMDs.
First court judgment (Firstmac, July 2024)The Federal Court found Firstmac breached the obligations by distributing an investment product to people outside its target market, and ordered a penalty.
First penalty against an issuer (American Express, 19 July 2024)The Federal Court ordered American Express to pay $8 million for failing to review TMDs for two co-branded credit cards when it ought to have known the cards weren't reaching the right customers.
Private credit focus (2026)ASIC's 2026 surveillance of retail private credit and managed investment schemes led to stop orders, including against Stratfund products (July 2026) and private credit products offered by Melbourne Securities Corporation (September 2026).

Common mistakes

  • Target markets so broad they exclude almost no one.
  • Review triggers that never fire, or data that nobody looks at.
  • Distributors that don't report complaints and sales data back to the issuer.
  • Marketing aimed at people outside the target market, such as broad social media campaigns for a niche product.
  • Treating the TMD as a document rather than a product governance process.

Questions a board can ask

These are prompts for discussion, not requirements.

  1. Which of our products have the broadest target markets, and are they justified?
  2. What do complaints, cancellations and claims data say about whether products reach the right people?
  3. When did a review trigger last lead us to change a product or its distribution?

Frequently asked questions

Which products are excluded?

Some products are excluded, including MySuper products and, in many cases, ordinary shares. Check the law and RG 274 for the full list.

Is a stop order a penalty?

No. It stops offers of the product until ASIC's concerns are fixed. But failing to meet the obligations can also lead to civil penalty proceedings.
