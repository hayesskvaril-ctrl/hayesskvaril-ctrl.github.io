ASIC RG 274 Product design and distribution obligations

ASIC's guidance on the design and distribution obligations (DDO): issuers must design products for a defined target market, and everyone in the chain must take reasonable steps so products reach the right people.

Intermediate About 10 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026
Issuer
Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)
Status
Guidance on Part 7.8A of the Corporations Act (in force since 5 October 2021). Current version: September 2024 (first issued December 2020).
Applies to
Issuers and distributors of most financial products for retail clients, including super (other than MySuper), insurance, managed funds and credit products.

Key takeaways

  • Issuers must make a target market determination (TMD) before distribution, describing who the product is likely to be consistent with, distribution conditions and review triggers.
  • Issuers and distributors must take reasonable steps so that distribution is consistent with the TMD.
  • Issuers must monitor, review TMDs when triggers occur, and notify ASIC of significant dealings inconsistent with the TMD within 10 business days.
  • Some products are excluded, including MySuper products and ordinary shares.
  • DDO is about outcomes, not documents: ASIC expects genuine product governance.

The product governance cycle

What a TMD must cover

ElementIn brief
Target marketThe class of retail clients the product would likely be consistent with, considering their likely objectives, financial situation and needs.
Distribution conditionsRestrictions on how the product is distributed (e.g. only through advice, or with eligibility checks).
Review triggers and periodsEvents that suggest the TMD may no longer be appropriate (e.g. complaints spikes, significant dealings, poor outcomes), and maximum periods between reviews.
Reporting by distributorsWhat information distributors must report to the issuer (e.g. complaints, dealings outside the target market) and when.

Practical points

  • Be specific. A target market of "anyone who wants insurance" is not meaningful. ASIC has used stop orders against TMDs it considered inadequate.
  • Negative target markets help. Describing who the product is not suitable for makes distribution controls clearer.
  • Watch the data. Claims ratios, cancellation rates, complaints and investment outcomes are signs of whether the product is working for its market.
  • Marketing is distribution. Advertising that targets people outside the target market can be inconsistent with the TMD.
  • Super: choice products and insurance within super are generally covered, but MySuper products are excluded.
