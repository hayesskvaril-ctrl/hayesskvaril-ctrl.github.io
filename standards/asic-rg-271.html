ASIC RG 271 Internal dispute resolution

ASIC's standards for how financial firms handle complaints: what counts as a complaint, how fast firms must respond, and how complaints data should be used.

Intermediate About 10 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026
Issuer
Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)
Status
Guidance, with enforceable paragraphs made binding by ASIC Corporations, Credit and Superannuation (Internal Dispute Resolution) Instrument 2020/98. Applies to complaints received on or after 5 October 2021.
Applies to
AFS licensees and credit licensees serving retail clients, super trustees, and other financial firms required to have IDR, such as unlicensed product issuers and credit representatives.

Key takeaways

  • A complaint is any expression of dissatisfaction where a response or resolution is expected, including on social media. Firms can't insist it be in writing or labelled a "complaint".
  • Enforceable paragraphs of RG 271 set binding standards, including maximum timeframes: generally 30 calendar days, and 45 days for most super complaints.
  • If a firm can't resolve a complaint in time, or rejects it, it must give a written IDR response explaining the outcome and the right to go to AFCA.
  • Firms must identify and escalate systemic issues, and report complaints data to their boards and to ASIC.

What counts as a complaint?

RG 271 adopts the definition from the Australian/New Zealand standard AS/NZS 10002:2014: in substance, an expression of dissatisfaction made to or about an organisation, about its products, services, staff or complaints handling, where a response or resolution is explicitly or implicitly expected or legally required. A complaint doesn't have to be in writing, use the word "complaint", or be made through a particular channel. ASIC's guidance includes complaints made on a firm's social media platforms.

The key standards

AreaWhat RG 271 expects (summary)
Commitment and cultureBoard and senior management support for effective complaints handling, and a culture that values complaints as feedback.
Enabling complaintsEasy to complain in many ways; information about how to complain is accessible; help for consumers who need it.
TimelinessResolve within the maximum timeframes; give written IDR responses where required, with reasons and AFCA details.
Fairness and objectivityComplaints are handled fairly, with the right people and information; conflicts avoided.
Systemic issuesIdentify, escalate and address systemic issues that complaints reveal (for example, a fee error affecting many members).
Data and reportingRecord complaints data, analyse it, report regularly to the board and senior management, and report IDR data to ASIC.

Why complaints data matters for risk

Complaints are often the first sign of a breach or incident. A spike in complaints about unexpected fees, delayed claims or incorrect balances can reveal a systemic problem long before an audit does. Good practice is to link complaints handling with incident and breach processes, so a complaint that reveals a potential breach is assessed promptly. See Incident and breach management.
