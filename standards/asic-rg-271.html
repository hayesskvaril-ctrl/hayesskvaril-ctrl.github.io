ASIC RG 271 Internal dispute resolution

ASIC's standards for how financial firms handle complaints: what counts as a complaint, how fast firms must respond, and how complaints data should be used.

Intermediate About 19 minutes Last reviewed: 7 October 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)
Status
Guidance, with enforceable paragraphs made binding by ASIC Corporations, Credit and Superannuation (Internal Dispute Resolution) Instrument 2020/98. Applies to complaints received on or after 5 October 2021.
Applies to
AFS licensees and credit licensees serving retail clients, super trustees, and other financial firms required to have IDR, such as unlicensed product issuers and credit representatives.

Key takeaways

  • A complaint is any expression of dissatisfaction where a response or resolution is expected, including on social media. Firms can't insist it be in writing or labelled a "complaint".
  • Enforceable paragraphs of RG 271 set binding standards, including maximum timeframes: generally 30 calendar days, and 45 days for most super complaints.
  • If a firm can't resolve a complaint in time, or rejects it, it must give a written IDR response explaining the outcome and the right to go to AFCA.
  • Firms must identify and escalate systemic issues, and report complaints data to their boards and to ASIC.

What counts as a complaint?

RG 271 adopts the definition from the Australian/New Zealand standard AS/NZS 10002:2014: in substance, an expression of dissatisfaction made to or about an organisation, about its products, services, staff or complaints handling, where a response or resolution is explicitly or implicitly expected or legally required. A complaint doesn't have to be in writing, use the word "complaint", or be made through a particular channel. ASIC's guidance includes complaints made on a firm's social media platforms.

The complaint journey and timeframes

The key standards

AreaWhat RG 271 expects (summary)
Commitment and cultureBoard and senior management support for effective complaints handling, and a culture that values complaints as feedback.
Enabling complaintsEasy to complain in many ways; information about how to complain is accessible; help for consumers who need it.
TimelinessResolve within the maximum timeframes; give written IDR responses where required, with reasons and AFCA details.
Fairness and objectivityComplaints are handled fairly, with the right people and information; conflicts avoided.
Systemic issuesIdentify, escalate and address systemic issues that complaints reveal (for example, a fee error affecting many members).
Data and reportingRecord complaints data, analyse it, report regularly to the board and senior management, and report IDR data to ASIC.

Why complaints data matters for risk

Complaints are often the first sign of a breach or incident. A spike in complaints about unexpected fees, delayed claims or incorrect balances can reveal a systemic problem long before an audit does. Good practice is to link complaints handling with incident and breach processes, so a complaint that reveals a potential breach is assessed promptly. See Incident and breach management.

Maximum IDR timeframes

In summary, from RG 271 (check the guide for every category and exception):

Complaint typeMaximum time for an IDR response
Standard complaints30 calendar days after receiving the complaint.
Superannuation trustee complaints (other than death benefit objections)45 calendar days.
Objections to a proposed super death benefit distribution90 calendar days after the 28-day objection period ends.
Credit complaints about default notices, hardship or postponing enforcement21 calendar days.

Other key rules

  • Delay notices: if a firm can't respond in time, it must send an IDR delay notification before the deadline, explaining why and the right to go to AFCA.
  • Written responses: a complaint resolved by the end of the fifth business day can be closed without a written IDR response, except where the complainant asks for one or the complaint is about hardship, a declined insurance claim or its value, or a super trustee's decision.
  • Systemic issues: firms must identify, escalate and deal with systemic issues found through complaints.
  • Board oversight: the board and senior management are expected to oversee complaints performance and culture.

IDR data reporting

  • Financial firms report their complaints data to ASIC every six months.
  • ASIC's updated IDR data reporting handbook was published on 1 December 2025. Firms first reported under it in the July to August 2026 window, covering complaints received or closed from 1 January 2026.
  • ASIC has decided to publish IDR data at firm level, with changes to how the data is presented to protect privacy and support fair comparisons.

What ASIC has found

  • ASIC's 2022 surveillance of super trustees' IDR found weaknesses, including that complainants weren't told about delays nearly half the time when responses ran past 45 days.
  • Complaints and claims handling remain ASIC enforcement priorities for 2026.

Common mistakes

  • Not recognising a complaint because the customer didn't use the word "complaint", including on social media.
  • Missing the deadline without sending a delay notice.
  • Closing complaints quickly without fixing the cause, so the same issue keeps coming back.
  • Poor data, which now matters more because ASIC publishes firm-level IDR data.

Questions a board can ask

These are prompts for discussion, not requirements.

  1. What are our complaint volumes, themes and response times, and how do they compare with our peers?
  2. What systemic issues have complaints revealed this year, and what did we change?
  3. How often do we miss IDR timeframes, and do we always send delay notices?

Frequently asked questions

Does a complaint have to be in writing?

No. A complaint can be made in any way, including by phone or on social media, and doesn't need to use the word "complaint".

What happens after IDR?

If the complaint isn't resolved, the complainant can go to AFCA, which is free for consumers.
