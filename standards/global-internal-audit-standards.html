IIA Global Internal Audit Standards

The global professional standards for internal auditing, which took effect in January 2025.

Intermediate About 7 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026 Checked against sources
Publisher
The Institute of Internal Auditors (IIA)
Current version
Global Internal Audit Standards, published January 2024, effective 9 January 2025.
Status
Professional standards for internal auditors. Not law, but APRA and audit committees commonly expect conformance.

Key takeaways

  • The Global Internal Audit Standards replaced the IIA's previous standards from 9 January 2025.
  • They are organised into five domains and fifteen principles, each with requirements and guidance.
  • A whole domain is about the board's role in governing internal audit: its mandate, independence and oversight.
  • Topical requirements add minimum expectations for auditing particular risks, starting with cybersecurity from February 2026.
  • For how internal audit works in practice, see Internal audit.

Five domains, fifteen principles

DomainPrinciples (titles, paraphrased)
I. Purpose of internal auditingA statement of what internal audit is for, rather than numbered principles.
II. Ethics and professionalism1. Integrity · 2. Objectivity · 3. Competency · 4. Due professional care · 5. Confidentiality
III. Governing the internal audit function6. Authorised by the board · 7. Positioned independently · 8. Overseen by the board
IV. Managing the internal audit function9. Plan strategically · 10. Manage resources · 11. Communicate effectively · 12. Enhance quality
V. Performing internal audit services13. Plan engagements · 14. Conduct engagement work · 15. Communicate results and monitor action plans

What it means for boards and audit committees

  • Mandate and charter: the board (usually through the audit committee) approves internal audit's mandate and charter.
  • Independence: the head of internal audit reports functionally to the board, with unrestricted access, and the board protects the function from interference.
  • Resources and quality: the board considers whether internal audit has enough resources, and receives results of quality assessments, including an external quality assessment at least every five years.
  • Strategy: the head of internal audit develops an internal audit strategy aligned with the organisation's objectives, which the board reviews.

The Standards are published by the IIA and are freely available on its website. This page summarises them in our own words.
