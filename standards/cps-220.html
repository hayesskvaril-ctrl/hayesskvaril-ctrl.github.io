CPS 220 Risk Management

APRA's foundational risk standard: every bank and insurer (and, through SPS 220, every super trustee) must have a board-approved framework for managing all its material risks.

Intermediate About 13 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026
Issuer
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA)
Status
Binding prudential standard, in force (CPS 220 has applied in its current form since 2015, with later amendments).
Applies to
CPS 220: authorised deposit-taking institutions, general and life insurers, private health insurers and relevant groups. SPS 220: RSE licensees (super trustees), with broadly equivalent requirements framed around beneficiaries.
Guidance
Prudential Practice Guides CPG 220 and SPG 220.
Works with
CPS 230 (operational risk forms part of the framework), CPS 510 (governance, including the board risk committee) and CPS 234 (information security).

Key takeaways

  • The risk management framework is the "totality" of systems, structures, policies, processes and people used to identify, measure, evaluate, monitor, report and control or mitigate material risks.
  • It must cover all material risks, including credit, market and investment, liquidity, insurance, operational and strategic risks.
  • The board must approve a risk appetite statement and a risk management strategy, and form a view of the entity's risk culture.
  • There must be an operationally independent risk management function headed by a Chief Risk Officer, and a designated compliance function.
  • The framework needs a comprehensive, operationally independent review at least every three years, and banks and insurers must give APRA an annual risk management declaration.

The framework at a glance

What it requires, part by part

Requirement (summary)What it means in practice
Board responsibilityThe board is ultimately responsible for the framework and oversees its operation. It sets risk appetite, approves the RAS and RMS, and must form a view of the entity's risk culture, how well it supports operating within appetite, and what needs to change.
Risk management frameworkA framework appropriate to the size, business mix and complexity of the entity that identifies and manages each material risk, and is consistent with the entity's strategy and business plan.
Risk appetite statementConveys the degree of risk the entity is prepared to accept; sets tolerances for each material risk as risk limits; describes how tolerances are set and monitored and what happens when they are breached. See Risk appetite and tolerance.
Risk management strategyA board-approved document describing the key elements of the framework: how each material risk is identified, measured and managed, roles and responsibilities, and how the framework is reviewed.
Business planA rolling plan (typically three years) whose risks are considered within the framework.
Risk management function and CROA designated risk management function that is operationally independent, with the authority and reporting lines to the board, board committees and senior management to do its job; headed by a designated Chief Risk Officer.
Compliance functionA designated compliance function that helps senior management manage compliance risk, staffed by competent people with sufficient authority and a reporting line independent of the business lines.
Management informationSystems that give the board and management timely, accurate information about the risk profile against appetite.
ReviewRegular review of the framework, plus a comprehensive review of its appropriateness, effectiveness and adequacy by operationally independent, appropriately trained and competent people (which can include external consultants) at least every three years, reported to the board risk committee.
Risk management declarationBanks and insurers must give APRA an annual declaration from the board about the framework, within set timeframes after year end (generally three or four months, depending on the entity).

CPS 220 and SPS 220: what's different for super?

SPS 220 applies similar principles to RSE licensees, but framed around the trustee's ability to meet its obligations to beneficiaries rather than depositors or policyholders. Super-specific material risks, such as investment risk borne by members, liquidity for member switches and benefit payments, and outsourcing to administrators, are a big part of the picture. Super trustees also need to read SPS 220 alongside SPS 515 (strategic planning and member outcomes) and SPS 530 (investment governance).

How CPS 220 connects to other standards

  • CPS 230: operational risk management requirements form part of the framework, and CPS 220 framework reviews need to cover operational risk.
  • CPS 510: requires a board risk committee to oversee the framework (and is under review; see Board structure and accountability).
  • CPS 234: information security risk is managed within the framework.
  • CPS 511: remuneration must support effective risk management.
