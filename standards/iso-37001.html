ISO 37001 Anti-bribery management systems

The international standard for a management system to prevent, detect and respond to bribery, updated in 2025.

Intermediate About 13 minutes Last reviewed: 8 October 2026 Checked against sources
Publisher
International Organization for Standardization (ISO)
Current edition
ISO 37001:2025 (second edition), replacing ISO 37001:2016.
Status
Voluntary. Certifiable.

Key takeaways

  • ISO 37001 sets requirements for an anti-bribery management system, covering bribery by and of the organisation, its staff and business associates.
  • Key elements include a bribery risk assessment, an anti-bribery policy and compliance function, due diligence on business associates, financial and non-financial controls, gifts and hospitality rules, reporting channels and investigations.
  • The 2025 edition strengthens the roles of culture, the governing body and the anti-bribery function, and adds conflicts of interest and climate change considerations.
  • Certification is not a legal defence on its own, but a well-run system can help show "adequate procedures" under Australia's failure to prevent foreign bribery offence.

ISO 37001 and Australian law

Since September 2024, companies can be liable for failing to prevent foreign bribery by their associates unless they had adequate procedures. The Attorney-General's Department's guidance on adequate procedures is principles-based, and courts will look at what the company actually did, not at certificates. ISO 37001 is a recognised benchmark for designing those procedures, but it should be tailored to the organisation's own risks.

ISO standards are copyrighted and must be bought from ISO or Standards Australia. This page describes the standard in our own words and does not reproduce its text.

What changed in the 2025 edition

ISO 37001:2025 replaced the 2016 edition in February 2025. It keeps the same overall structure; summary of the main changes, based on published commentary:

AreaChange (summary)
CultureMore emphasis on an anti-bribery culture and ethical leadership.
Conflicts of interestMore attention to conflicts of interest as a bribery risk.
The anti-bribery functionClearer role and responsibilities.
ClimateIncorporates the 2024 climate amendment that applies to many management system standards.
StructureAligned with the harmonised structure used by other ISO management system standards, making it easier to combine with ISO 37301.

Common mistakes

  • Certifying a narrow part of the business while high-risk overseas operations sit outside the scope.
  • Due diligence on third parties done once at onboarding and never refreshed.
  • Gifts registers that nobody reviews.

Frequently asked questions

Does ISO 37001 certification prove "adequate procedures" under Australian law?

No. It can be useful evidence, but a court would look at whether the procedures were actually adequate and followed in the circumstances.

Do existing ISO 37001:2016 certificates need to move to the 2025 edition?

Certification bodies set transition arrangements. Check with the certification body for the timetable.
