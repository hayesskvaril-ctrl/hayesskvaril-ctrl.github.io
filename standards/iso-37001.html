ISO 37001 Anti-bribery management systems

The international standard for a management system to prevent, detect and respond to bribery, updated in 2025.

Intermediate About 6 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026
Publisher
International Organization for Standardization (ISO)
Current edition
ISO 37001:2025 (second edition), replacing ISO 37001:2016.
Status
Voluntary. Certifiable.

Key takeaways

  • ISO 37001 sets requirements for an anti-bribery management system, covering bribery by and of the organisation, its staff and business associates.
  • Key elements include a bribery risk assessment, an anti-bribery policy and compliance function, due diligence on business associates, financial and non-financial controls, gifts and hospitality rules, reporting channels and investigations.
  • The 2025 edition strengthens the roles of culture, the governing body and the anti-bribery function, and adds conflicts of interest and climate change considerations.
  • Certification is not a legal defence on its own, but a well-run system can help show "adequate procedures" under Australia's failure to prevent foreign bribery offence.

ISO 37001 and Australian law

Since September 2024, companies can be liable for failing to prevent foreign bribery by their associates unless they had adequate procedures. The Attorney-General's Department's guidance on adequate procedures is principles-based, and courts will look at what the company actually did, not at certificates. ISO 37001 is a recognised benchmark for designing those procedures, but it should be tailored to the organisation's own risks.
