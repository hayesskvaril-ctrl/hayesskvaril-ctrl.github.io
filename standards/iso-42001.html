ISO/IEC 42001 Artificial intelligence management systems

The first certifiable international standard for managing the development and use of artificial intelligence responsibly.

Intermediate About 14 minutes Last reviewed: 8 October 2026 Checked against sources
Publisher
International Organization for Standardization and International Electrotechnical Commission (ISO/IEC)
Current edition
ISO/IEC 42001:2023.
Status
Voluntary. Certifiable.
Related
ISO/IEC 23894 (AI risk management guidance) and ISO/IEC 22989 (AI concepts and terminology).

Key takeaways

  • ISO/IEC 42001 sets requirements for an AI management system for organisations that develop, provide or use AI.
  • Beyond the common management system clauses, it requires AI risk assessment and AI system impact assessment, looking at effects on individuals, groups and society.
  • An annex sets out a list of AI-specific controls covering areas such as policies, data, the AI system life cycle, third parties and information for users.
  • It works alongside ISO/IEC 27001 and is a useful reference for meeting regulator expectations such as APRA's 2026 AI letter and the Government's Guidance for AI Adoption.

What is distinctive about ISO/IEC 42001

FeatureIn plain English
AI policy and objectivesA policy on responsible AI, with objectives linked to the organisation's role (developer, provider or user).
AI risk assessmentIdentifying risks from AI systems, including to fairness, transparency, safety, security and privacy.
AI system impact assessmentConsidering the consequences of an AI system for people and society, not only for the organisation.
Life cycle controlsControls from design and data through verification, deployment, monitoring and retirement.
Third partiesManaging suppliers of AI models and services, and responsibilities shared with customers.

For the governance picture, including how Australian law and regulators approach AI, see AI governance and Model risk management.

Worked example: starting an AI management system

An insurer wants to use ISO/IEC 42001 to bring order to its growing use of AI:

  1. Inventory: list every AI system it builds, buys or uses, including AI inside vendor products.
  2. Policy and roles: a board-approved AI policy, with an executive owner for each system.
  3. Impact and risk assessment: for each system, assess effects on customers and staff (such as fairness and transparency) as well as risks to the insurer.
  4. Controls: choose controls in proportion to risk, such as human review of claim decisions, testing for bias and monitoring for drift.
  5. Monitor and improve: internal audit and management review; update as systems and laws change.

Illustrative only.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving vendor AI out of scope.
  • Treating 42001 as an IT standard when most risks sit with business decisions.
  • Impact assessments done once and never revisited.

Frequently asked questions

Is ISO/IEC 42001 required in Australia?

No. It is voluntary. The Government's Guidance for AI Adoption and APRA's and ASIC's expectations draw on similar ideas, so 42001 can be a useful way to organise the work.

How does it relate to ISO/IEC 27001?

They share the same management system structure, so organisations often run them together. 27001 protects information; 42001 governs how AI systems are developed and used.
