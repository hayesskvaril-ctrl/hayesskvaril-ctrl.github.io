APS 110 Capital Adequacy and APS 210 Liquidity: the basics

The two foundations of bank soundness: enough capital to absorb losses, and enough liquidity to meet obligations when money flows out.

Intermediate About 10 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA)
Status
Binding prudential standards, based on the international Basel III framework. The capital framework was substantially revised from 1 January 2023.
Applies to
Authorised deposit-taking institutions (banks, building societies and credit unions).

Key takeaways

  • Capital absorbs unexpected losses so a bank can keep operating. Liquidity is having enough cash or easily sold assets to meet payments when due.
  • APS 110 sets minimum capital ratios as a share of risk-weighted assets, with the highest-quality capital, Common Equity Tier 1, at least 4.5% before buffers.
  • Banks hold capital buffers above the minimums, and must run an ICAAP to assess their own capital needs, including under stress.
  • APS 210 requires larger ADIs to meet a Liquidity Coverage Ratio and a Net Stable Funding Ratio of at least 100%; smaller ADIs have a simpler minimum liquidity holdings requirement.
  • APRA has decided to phase out Additional Tier 1 capital from 1 January 2027.

Capital: APS 110

  • Risk-weighted assets (RWA): each asset is weighted by its riskiness, so a bank with riskier lending needs more capital. Weights come from standardised tables or, for banks approved to use them, internal models.
  • Buffers: a capital conservation buffer for all ADIs, extra buffers for domestic systemically important banks, and a countercyclical buffer that APRA can adjust with the credit cycle.
  • ICAAP: each ADI must assess its own capital needs for all material risks, including through stress testing, and hold a board-approved capital management plan. See Scenario analysis and stress testing.

Liquidity: APS 210

MeasureWhat it testsRequirement
Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)Enough high-quality liquid assets to survive a severe 30-day stress.At least 100% (larger ADIs)
Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)Longer-term assets funded by stable sources, such as deposits and long-term borrowing.At least 100% (larger ADIs)
Minimum liquidity holdingsA simpler requirement to hold a minimum share of liabilities in liquid assets.Smaller ADIs
Liquidity risk managementA board-approved liquidity risk framework, funding strategy, stress testing and contingency funding plan.All ADIs

The Banking and ADIs page includes a worked Liquidity Coverage Ratio example. APRA has said it intends to consult on changes to bank liquidity requirements following its 2025 system stress test.
