SPS 310 Audit and Related Matters

APRA's standard on the independent auditor every super fund must have: who can be appointed, what the auditor must give assurance on each year, and what the trustee must do to support the audit.

Intermediate About 12 minutes Last reviewed: 12 October 2026 Checked against sources
Issuer
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA)
Status
Binding prudential standard. The current version (F2024L00636) has applied since 30 June 2024. APRA describes it as supporting SPS 510 Governance.
Applies to
All RSE licensees (trustees of APRA-regulated super funds), and the RSE auditors they appoint.
Main related law
Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993 (SIS Act), including s 35AC (the auditor's functions and duties set by the prudential standards) and the auditor disqualification rules (ss 130D and 130EA); Chapter 2M of the Corporations Act 2001 for financial reports and audit.

Key takeaways

  • Every super fund (RSE) must have an RSE auditor, engaged under a legally binding contract that requires it to follow SPS 310 and the Australian auditing and assurance standards.
  • The auditor must be eligible, fit and proper and independent, and the trustee must give it access to all the data, staff, the Board, the audit committee, internal audit and information APRA has provided.
  • Each year the auditor reports to the Board for each fund, giving reasonable assurance on key APRA reporting forms and compliance with listed laws, and limited assurance on the trustee's compliance controls and its risk management framework.
  • The report goes to ASIC with the fund's financial report, or to APRA within three months of the end of the year of income.
  • APRA can require a special purpose engagement: an audit of a particular area, reported to APRA and the Board, usually within three months.

SPS 310 in plain English

A super fund's financial statements are audited like any other entity's. SPS 310 adds a second, prudential layer: the RSE auditor also checks the information the trustee reports to APRA, whether the trustee has complied with a list of key laws, and whether its systems for staying compliant and its risk management framework work. APRA relies on this work in supervising the fund, so the auditor must prepare its reports knowing APRA may rely on them, while forming its own independent judgement.

The trustee's side of the bargain is to appoint a suitable auditor on proper terms, keep the auditor informed about the prudential requirements, give it full access and pay for the work.

Reasonable and limited assurance

SPS 310 uses the two levels of assurance defined by the Auditing and Assurance Standards Board (AUASB). Reasonable assurance is a high, but not absolute, level: the auditor gathers enough evidence to give a positive opinion, for example that the trustee complied with a provision in all material respects. Limited assurance involves less work, and the auditor reports whether anything has come to its attention suggesting a problem. For the trustee, the difference matters: a limited assurance conclusion on its compliance controls is not a guarantee that they work, so the Board still needs its own assurance from the three lines.

Worked example: preparing for the annual SPS 310 audit

A trustee with one fund and a 30 June year of income plans its audit cycle.

  1. The Board Audit Committee confirms the auditor's engagement letter covers SPS 310, AUASB standards and the required legal references, and that independence and fit and proper checks are current (paras 10, 13).
  2. The compliance team maps each Attachment B provision to the controls and evidence that show compliance, so the auditor can test them, and lists any breaches reported during the year.
  3. The risk team prepares evidence of how the SPS 220 risk management framework operated, because the auditor will give limited assurance on compliance with it (para 22(b)(iii)).
  4. Finance reconciles the APRA reporting forms in Attachment A to the general ledger before the auditor tests them.
  5. The auditor's report goes to the Board, and the trustee lodges it with ASIC alongside the financial report, or with APRA, within three months of 30 June (para 16). Any modified opinion is followed up through the breach and issue processes.

An illustrative plan for learning, not advice about any trustee's obligations.

Common mistakes

  • Treating limited assurance on compliance controls as proof that the controls work.
  • Engagement letters that don't reflect the current SPS 310 or reference the AUASB standards (para 10).
  • Restricting the auditor's access, for example to APRA correspondence or internal audit reports (para 12).
  • Not checking the auditor's independence and fit and proper status each year (paras 13, 14).
  • Missing the three-month lodgement deadline because the audit timetable leaves no room for issues (para 16).
  • Not linking a modified opinion to the breach reporting process: a material breach found by the auditor may also be reportable to ASIC and APRA.

How SPS 310 fits with other rules

  • SPS 510 and SPS 520: auditor independence and rotation rules, the Board Audit Committee, and the fit and proper requirements the auditor must meet.
  • SPS 220: the risk management framework the auditor gives limited assurance on.
  • SPS 114: the ORFR reporting form is one of the forms audited at reasonable assurance, and SPS 114 relies on SPS 310 for external audit of the ORFR strategy.
  • Breach reporting: SIS Act auditors and actuaries have their own duties to report certain contraventions to APRA, separate from the trustee's.
  • Internal audit: SPS 310 deals with the external RSE auditor; internal audit is covered by SPS 510.

Questions a board can ask

  • Which parts of the auditor's report are reasonable assurance and which are limited, and what other assurance do we have over the limited parts?
  • Did the auditor modify any opinion or raise compliance findings, and how are they being fixed?
  • Has the auditor had full access, including to APRA correspondence and internal audit work?
  • When did we last test the auditor's independence and the lead auditor's fit and proper status?

Frequently asked questions

Is the RSE auditor the same as the financial statement auditor?

Often the same firm does both, but they are different roles. The financial statement audit is under the Corporations Act; the RSE auditor's SPS 310 report covers APRA reporting, compliance with listed laws, compliance controls and the risk management framework. The two reports can be lodged with ASIC as a single document.

Can APRA order an extra audit?

Yes. Under a special purpose engagement APRA can require the trustee to engage an auditor, chosen by APRA if it wishes, to report on a particular area. The trustee pays, and the report usually goes to APRA and the Board within three months of APRA's notice.
