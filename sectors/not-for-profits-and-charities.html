Not-for-profits and charities

How charities are governed and regulated, and how smaller organisations can manage risk in proportion to their size.

Beginner About 9 minutes Last reviewed: 28 September 2026 Checked against sources
Regulator
Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission (ACNC) for registered charities; state regulators for fundraising and incorporated associations
Core law
Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission Act 2012

Key takeaways

  • Registered charities must meet the ACNC's five Governance Standards, and those operating overseas must meet the External Conduct Standards.
  • Board members and other responsible people have duties similar to company directors: act with care, honestly in the charity's best interests, and manage conflicts.
  • Reporting depends on size. Since 1 July 2022, small charities have annual revenue under $500,000, medium $500,000 to under $3 million, and large $3 million or more.
  • Risk management should be proportionate. Even a small charity benefits from a simple risk register, clear delegations and basic financial controls.

The five Governance Standards

StandardIn plain English
1. Purposes and not-for-profit natureBe clear about the charity's purposes and show it is not-for-profit.
2. Accountability to membersGive members adequate opportunity to raise concerns about how the charity is governed (if it has members).
3. Compliance with Australian lawsDon't commit a serious offence or breach a law that could lead to a significant penalty.
4. Suitability of responsible peopleTake reasonable steps to ensure board members are suitable and not disqualified.
5. Duties of responsible peopleEnsure responsible people act with care and diligence, honestly in the charity's best interests, don't misuse position or information, disclose conflicts, manage finances responsibly and don't trade while insolvent.
