Listed companies

The governance and disclosure rules that apply to ASX-listed companies, many of which also shape practice in financial services.

Intermediate About 9 minutes Last reviewed: 28 September 2026 Checked against sources
Regulators
ASIC (Corporations Act) and ASX (Listing Rules)
Core rules
Corporations Act 2001; ASX Listing Rules; ASX Corporate Governance Principles and Recommendations (4th edition; draft 5th edition released July 2026)

Key takeaways

  • Directors of all companies have core statutory duties: care and diligence, good faith, proper use of position and information, and preventing insolvent trading.
  • Continuous disclosure requires listed entities to tell the market immediately about information that would materially affect their share price, subject to limited exceptions.
  • The ASX Corporate Governance Principles work on an "if not, why not" basis. A draft 5th edition was released for consultation in July 2026.
  • Listed companies must report on remuneration, and larger ones on climate-related risks under the new sustainability reporting regime.
  • Risk management is expected to be overseen by the board, typically through a risk committee.

Key obligations

AreaIn plain English
Directors' duties (ss 180–184)Act with care and diligence, in good faith in the company's interests, and don't misuse position or information. Plus the duty to prevent insolvent trading. See Board structure and accountability.
Continuous disclosureASX Listing Rule 3.1 and the Corporations Act. Since 2021, private claims for damages (such as shareholder class actions) require proof that the company acted with knowledge, recklessness or negligence.
Corporate governance reportingReport against each ASX recommendation and explain any that aren't followed.
Remuneration reportingAn annual remuneration report, with shareholders voting on it; two consecutive "strikes" can trigger a vote on spilling the board.
Sustainability reportingClimate statements for companies above the thresholds. See Climate-related financial disclosures.
Whistleblower policyRequired for public companies. See RG 270.

The ASX Principles and risk

The ASX Principles recommend that boards have a committee to oversee risk, review the risk management framework at least annually, disclose material exposure to environmental and social risks, and have an internal audit function (or explain alternatives). The draft 5th edition proposes updates across the Principles; listed entities would first report against it for financial years starting on or after 1 July 2027, if the proposed timetable holds.

Key risks

  • Disclosure risk: late or misleading disclosure leading to regulatory action or class actions.
  • Governance failures that erode investor trust. See the HIH case study.
  • Greenwashing in sustainability claims.
  • Cyber incidents that also trigger disclosure decisions. See the Optus and Medibank case study.
