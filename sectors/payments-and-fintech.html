Payments and fintech

A fast-changing sector: new payments licensing, digital asset rules, open data and scams obligations are reshaping how payments and fintech firms are regulated.

Intermediate About 10 minutes Last reviewed: 28 September 2026
Regulators
ASIC (licensing and conduct), RBA (payments system), APRA (major stored value facilities, proposed), ACCC and Treasury (Consumer Data Right), AUSTRAC (AML/CTF)
Status
Several major reforms are in progress. Check the latest position before relying on details.

Key takeaways

  • Treasury released draft legislation in March 2026 to bring payment service providers into the AFS licensing regime, with prudential supervision by APRA for the largest stored value facilities.
  • The RBA has removed card surcharges on designated card networks and reduced interchange fees, mostly from 1 October 2026.
  • Digital asset platforms and tokenised custody platforms need an AFSL under legislation passed in April 2026.
  • The Consumer Data Right is expanding to non-bank lenders during 2026 and 2027.
  • Payments firms face growing scams and AML/CTF obligations.

The main reforms

ReformWhat it doesStatus (September 2026)
Payments licensingBrings payment service providers, such as stored value facility issuers and payment facilitators, into the AFSL regime. Stored value facility providers above a size threshold would be prudentially supervised by APRA.Tranche 1 draft legislation released March 2026; proposed to start 12 months after it becomes law, with transition.
Card surcharging and interchangeRemoves surcharging on eftpos, Mastercard and Visa cards, lowers interchange fee caps and improves fee transparency.Most changes started on 1 October 2026; a cap on foreign card interchange fees and some transparency measures follow on 1 April 2027.
Digital assetsOperators of digital asset platforms and tokenised custody platforms must hold an AFSL and meet platform-specific standards.Law since April 2026, with implementation and ASIC standards to follow.
Consumer Data RightCustomers can direct businesses to share their data with accredited recipients. Extended from banking and energy to non-bank lenders.Non-bank lender product data from 13 July 2026; consumer data from 9 November 2026 for initial providers.
Scams Prevention FrameworkObligations to prevent, detect, report, disrupt and respond to scams for designated sectors, starting with banks, telcos and digital platforms.Law since 2025; sector designations and codes being made. See Consumer protection.
AML/CTF reformsUpdated obligations for existing reporting entities, including remittance and digital currency exchange providers.In force from 31 March 2026. See AML/CTF fundamentals.

Key risks for payments and fintech firms

  • Regulatory change: building licensing, capital and compliance capability ahead of new regimes.
  • Safeguarding customer funds: keeping client money separate and available.
  • Fraud, scams and money laundering through fast, low-friction payments. See Fraud risk management.
  • Operational and cyber resilience, since outages stop customers paying. See Cyber risk management.
  • Reliance on partners: banks, card schemes and cloud providers. See Third-party risk.
  • Growth outpacing controls, a common pattern in fast-scaling fintechs.
