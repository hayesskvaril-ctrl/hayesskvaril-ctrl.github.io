Life insurance

How life insurers (death, TPD, income protection and trauma cover) are regulated: statutory funds, advice and commissions, claims, cover through super and the Life Code.

Intermediate About 21 minutes Last reviewed: 7 October 2026 Checked against sources
Main laws
Life Insurance Act 1995; Insurance Contracts Act 1984; Corporations Act 2001; SIS Act 1993 (cover in super); AML/CTF Act 2006; Financial Accountability Regime Act 2023.
Regulators
APRA (prudential), ASIC (conduct and advice), AUSTRAC (some products); AFCA handles complaints.
Products
Death, total and permanent disability (TPD), income protection, trauma and funeral cover.
Industry code
Life Insurance Code of Practice (Council of Australian Life Insurers).

Key takeaways

  • Life insurers face more overlapping regulators and rules than general insurers, because of advice, cover held through super and very long-lived products.
  • Policyholders' money is held in statutory funds under the Life Insurance Act, overseen by APRA.
  • Advice commissions are capped (60% upfront, 20% ongoing) with a two-year clawback.
  • The duty of utmost good faith and, since 2022, the efficiently, honestly and fairly obligation apply to claims; the TAL case in 2021 shows what a fair process requires.
  • The independent Life Code review reported in June 2026 with 85 recommendations, including on mental health exclusions.

Life insurance in plain English

Life insurance pays out when someone dies, becomes totally and permanently disabled, suffers a serious illness, or can't work because of illness or injury. Policies can run for decades, claims can be life-changing, and many people hold their cover through their super fund without ever choosing it. That combination makes life insurance one of the most heavily regulated parts of insurance: APRA for financial strength, ASIC for sales, advice and claims, super law for cover held in super, and an industry code on top.

The main types of cover

CoverPays whenCommonly held
Death (life cover)The insured person dies (often also on terminal illness).In super and directly
Total and permanent disability (TPD)The person is unlikely ever to work again (definitions vary).Mostly in super
Income protectionThe person can't work for a period because of illness or injury.In super and directly
Trauma (critical illness)A specified serious illness, such as cancer or heart attack, is diagnosed.Outside super only
Funeral insuranceDeath, to cover funeral costs.Directly

Life insurers answer to more regulators, and more overlapping rules, than general insurers, mainly because of advice, super and the long life of their products.

Advice and commissions

Life insurance commissions were a long-running conduct concern. Since the Life Insurance Framework reforms, ASIC's instrument caps the upfront commission an adviser can receive at 60% of the first year's premium (from 1 January 2020, after 80% in 2018 and 70% in 2019) and ongoing commission at 20%. If a policy lapses or is cancelled within two years, some or all of the commission must be repaid (the clawback). These caps aim to reduce incentives to "churn" clients between policies.

Utmost good faith and claims

The duty of utmost good faith (Insurance Contracts Act s 13) applies to life insurers in sales, underwriting and claims, and breaching it can now attract a civil penalty. In 2021 the Federal Court found that TAL Life breached the duty when it avoided an income protection policy for alleged non-disclosure without first giving the customer a proper chance to respond. ASIC also brought proceedings against MLC Limited in November 2021 over claims handling and utmost good faith. Since 1 January 2022, life insurers have also had to handle claims efficiently, honestly and fairly as a licensed financial service.

APRA and ASIC publish joint life insurance claims and disputes statistics, including how often claims are accepted and how long decisions take, broken down by product and by how the cover was sold. They are a useful benchmark for boards.

Income protection sustainability

Individual income protection had years of heavy losses. APRA intervened from 2019, including requiring life insurers to stop offering "agreed value" income protection to new customers from 31 March 2020, followed by further design changes intended to make products sustainable. Boards still watch income protection claims experience closely.

The Life Insurance Code of Practice

The Code sets commitments on sales, underwriting, claims timeframes, vulnerable customers and complaints. An independent review led by Peter Kell began on 1 October 2025, released an interim report in April 2026 and a final report on 30 June 2026 with 85 recommendations covering mental health, vulnerability, First Nations customers, claims timeframes and communication, and enforceability. The Council of Australian Life Insurers has responded, including a commitment not to include terms that automatically exclude cover for all mental health conditions; consumer groups have welcomed the response but say implementation will be the test. Check the Code's website for the updated Code and its start date.

Recent developments

  • CEO roundtable: APRA and ASIC held a life insurance CEO roundtable on 15 April 2026 and published notes in May 2026.
  • Longevity products: APRA finalised changes to the capital treatment of longevity products such as annuities in 2026, applying from 1 July 2026, to support better retirement income products.
  • ASIC priorities: ASIC's 2026 enforcement priorities include insurance claims and complaints handling failures, and its 2026–27 plans include a review of funeral insurance and a report on life insurer service issues.

Common weak spots

  • Long or opaque claims processes, especially for TPD and income protection, including excessive information requests and surveillance.
  • Avoiding policies for non-disclosure without a fair process.
  • Group cover in super that members don't know they have, or that doesn't match their needs.
  • Blanket exclusions, such as for mental health, that may not be fair.
  • Legacy products and systems that make errors in premiums and benefits more likely.

Questions a board can ask

These are prompts for discussion, not requirements.

  1. How do our claim acceptance rates and decision times compare with the APRA and ASIC statistics?
  2. How do we make sure decisions to avoid policies or decline claims are fair and well reasoned?
  3. How are we responding to the Life Code review, especially on mental health and vulnerability?
  4. What does our income protection claims experience tell us about product sustainability?
  5. Where do our super fund partners' and our own obligations overlap, and how do we coordinate?

Frequently asked questions

Is insurance in super regulated as life insurance?

Both. The cover is usually a group policy issued by a life insurer, which is regulated by APRA and ASIC as a life insurer. The super trustee that buys it has its own obligations to members under the SIS Act and SPS 250.

Can a life insurer refuse a claim for non-disclosure?

Only within the limits of the Insurance Contracts Act, and it must act with the utmost good faith. For consumer contracts since 5 October 2021, the test is whether the customer took reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation.

Who do customers complain to?

First to the insurer (or the super fund, for cover in super), then to AFCA.
