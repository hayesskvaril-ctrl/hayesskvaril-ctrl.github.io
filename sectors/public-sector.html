Public sector (Commonwealth)

How risk, fraud and governance are managed in Commonwealth entities, under a framework built around the PGPA Act.

Intermediate About 9 minutes Last reviewed: 28 September 2026 Checked against sources
Core law
Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013 (PGPA Act) and PGPA Rule
Key policies
Commonwealth Risk Management Policy (2023); Commonwealth Fraud and Corruption Control Framework (2024); Protective Security Policy Framework
Oversight bodies
Department of Finance, the Australian National Audit Office (ANAO) and the National Anti-Corruption Commission (NACC)

Key takeaways

  • Section 16 of the PGPA Act requires the accountable authority of every Commonwealth entity to establish and maintain appropriate systems of risk oversight and management and internal control.
  • The Commonwealth Risk Management Policy (updated from 1 January 2023) has nine elements and is mandatory for non-corporate Commonwealth entities.
  • The Fraud and Corruption Control Framework, in effect since 1 July 2024, extended fraud control rules to cover corruption.
  • Entities must have audit committees, and the ANAO audits performance and financial statements.
  • State and territory governments have their own frameworks; this page covers the Commonwealth.

The framework

ElementWhat it requires (summarised)
PGPA Act s 16Appropriate systems of risk oversight, management and internal control, including measures to ensure officials comply with finance law.
Commonwealth Risk Management PolicyNine elements covering, for example, embedding risk management in decision-making, having a risk management framework, managing shared risks and developing a positive risk culture. Mandatory for non-corporate Commonwealth entities and recommended for others.
Fraud and Corruption Control FrameworkA legislative rule setting minimum standards for all PGPA Act entities, a policy binding non-corporate entities, and guidance. Covers governance, risk assessment, prevention, detection, response and reporting. See Fraud risk management.
Audit committeesEvery entity must have an audit committee that reviews financial and performance reporting, risk oversight and internal control.
Protective Security Policy FrameworkSecurity requirements, including implementing the Essential Eight to Maturity Level Two for non-corporate entities.
IntegrityThe NACC investigates serious or systemic corrupt conduct. See Anti-bribery and corruption.

How public sector risk differs

  • Objectives are policy outcomes, not profit, so risk appetite is framed around delivering services and public value.
  • Shared risks across entities and levels of government need explicit coordination.
  • Accountability to Parliament and the public raises the stakes for integrity and transparency.
  • Program and grants risk: large programs delivered through third parties carry fraud and delivery risk.
