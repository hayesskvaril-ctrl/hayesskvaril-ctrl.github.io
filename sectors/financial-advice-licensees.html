Financial advice licensees

What it takes to run an advice business compliantly: licensee obligations, oversight of advisers, and the risks regulators focus on.

Intermediate About 8 minutes Last reviewed: 28 September 2026 Checked against sources
Main regulator
ASIC
Core law
Corporations Act 2001, Chapter 7 (including Part 7.7A on advice)
Licence
Australian financial services licence (AFSL) authorising personal advice to retail clients

Key takeaways

  • Advice licensees are responsible for the conduct of their advisers, and must supervise and monitor them.
  • On top of general AFSL obligations, advice licensees have specific duties: reporting certain misconduct by advisers (including other licensees' advisers), and notifying, investigating and remediating affected clients.
  • Advisers must be registered with ASIC and meet professional standards; the licensee must check and record this.
  • Advice licensees pay levies that fund ASIC and the Compensation Scheme of Last Resort, which have risen sharply because of advice failures.
  • The detailed advice rules are on the Financial advice regulation page.

For your sector: Obligations that usually apply · Compare sectors

What makes an advice licensee different

AreaWhat licensees must do (summarised)
Supervision and monitoringTake reasonable steps to ensure representatives comply with the law: file reviews, audits of advice, monitoring of complaints and product flows.
Adviser standardsEnsure advisers are registered, qualified, meet continuing professional development and follow the Code of Ethics.
ReportingReport significant breaches to ASIC (RG 78), including certain serious compliance concerns about another licensee's advisers.
Notify, investigate, remediateWhere advice may have caused loss, notify affected clients, investigate, and remediate within set timeframes (RG 277).
Conflicts and remunerationManage conflicts (RG 181) and comply with the conflicted remuneration ban.
Compensation arrangementsAdequate professional indemnity insurance.
ComplaintsIDR and AFCA membership (RG 271).

Key risks

  • Inappropriate advice, such as switching super into high-risk products. The Shield and First Guardian collapses are the leading recent example.
  • Lead generation and referral arrangements that create conflicts or pressure.
  • Fees for no service and failures in ongoing fee arrangements. See the Hayne Royal Commission.
  • Weak adviser oversight, especially for authorised representatives operating their own practices.
  • Record keeping and file quality that can't demonstrate the best interests duty was met.
  • Levy and insurance costs that affect the viability of smaller licensees.
