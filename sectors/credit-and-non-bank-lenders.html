Credit licensees and non-bank lenders

The rules for lenders and brokers that provide consumer credit, from banks to non-bank lenders, brokers and buy now pay later providers.

Intermediate About 11 minutes Last reviewed: 28 September 2026
Main regulator
ASIC (conduct and licensing). APRA supervises ADIs, and has data collection and certain rule-making powers for non-ADI lenders.
Core law
National Consumer Credit Protection Act 2009, including the National Credit Code
Licence
Australian credit licence (ACL)

Key takeaways

  • Anyone who provides consumer credit, or acts as an intermediary such as a mortgage broker, generally needs an Australian credit licence from ASIC.
  • Responsible lending obligations require lenders and brokers to make reasonable inquiries, verify the borrower's situation, and not provide credit that is unsuitable.
  • Borrowers in financial hardship can ask for changes to their repayments, and lenders must respond within set timeframes.
  • Buy now pay later became regulated credit from 10 June 2025, with modified responsible lending obligations.
  • Mortgage brokers must act in the best interests of consumers, and credit reporting is regulated under the Privacy Act.

Who is covered

TypeExamplesKey points
ADIs (banks)Home loans, credit cards, personal loansCredit licence plus APRA prudential regulation. See Banking and ADIs.
Non-bank lendersMortgage and personal lenders funded by securitisation or wholesale fundingCredit licence; report data to APRA; APRA can make rules to address financial stability risks from their lending.
Mortgage brokers and intermediariesBrokers arranging home loansCredit licence or authorised credit representative; a best interests duty since 2021.
Buy now pay later providersPay-in-instalment productsRegulated as low cost credit from 10 June 2025, with a choice of modified responsible lending obligations.
Small amount credit providersShort-term loans of $2,000 or less ("payday loans")Caps on fees and additional protections.

Core obligations

  1. Responsible lending

    Make reasonable inquiries about the consumer's requirements, objectives and financial situation, take reasonable steps to verify them, and assess whether the credit is not unsuitable. ASIC's guidance is in RG 209.

  2. Disclosure

    Credit guides, pre-contractual disclosure and key facts sheets for home loans.

  3. Design and distribution

    Target market determinations and distribution consistent with them. See RG 274.

  4. Hardship and collections

    Responding to hardship notices within the required timeframes, and fair debt collection practices.

  5. Credit reporting

    Accurate reporting under Part IIIA of the Privacy Act and the Credit Reporting Code, including financial hardship information.

  6. Complaints and breaches

    Internal dispute resolution and AFCA membership (RG 271), and breach reporting (RG 78 applies to credit licensees).

Key risks for credit businesses

  • Responsible lending failures, for example relying on benchmark living expenses without verifying the borrower's actual expenses.
  • Hardship handling: slow or unfair responses to customers in difficulty, especially vulnerable customers.
  • Broker and third-party conduct, including fraud in loan applications.
  • Credit reporting errors that damage consumers' credit scores.
  • Funding and liquidity risk for non-bank lenders that rely on wholesale markets.
