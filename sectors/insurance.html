Insurance

How insurers are regulated so they can pay claims when disasters happen, and so customers are treated fairly from sale to claim.

Intermediate About 17 minutes Last reviewed: 8 October 2026 Checked against sources
Main laws
Insurance Act 1973 (general insurance); Life Insurance Act 1995; Private Health Insurance (Prudential Supervision) Act 2015; Insurance Contracts Act 1984; Corporations Act 2001; Financial Accountability Regime Act 2023.
Regulators
APRA (prudential); ASIC (conduct for general and life insurance); for private health insurance, the ACCC and the Commonwealth Ombudsman also have roles, and the Department of Health, Disability and Ageing sets product rules. AFCA handles complaints.
Three industries
General insurance (home, motor, business, liability); life insurance (death, total and permanent disability, income protection, trauma); private health insurance.

Key takeaways

  • Insurers take in premiums now and pay claims later, sometimes years later and in huge catastrophes. Prudential rules focus on capital, reinsurance, reserving and actuarial advice.
  • Every insurance contract carries a duty of utmost good faith on both sides (Insurance Contracts Act s 13).
  • Handling and settling claims is a financial service: since 1 January 2022 (after a transition from 1 January 2021), general and life insurers must handle claims efficiently, honestly and fairly.
  • Post-Royal Commission reforms banned hawking, introduced a deferred sales model for add-on insurance and applied unfair contract terms laws to insurance.
  • Industry codes of practice add customer commitments. Both the general and life insurance codes are being redrafted.

Go deeper for your type of insurer: General insurance · Life insurance · Private health insurance. Each has its own guide and its own obligations page, because the rules differ a lot between them.

Obligations across the insurance lifecycle

The ASIC conduct rules on this page apply mainly to general and life insurance. Private health insurance isn't a "financial product" under the Corporations Act, so its conduct rules come from private health insurance laws, consumer law and its own code.

Prudential regulation

Each industry has its own standards series: GPS for general insurers, LPS for life insurers and HPS for private health insurers. They also follow the cross-industry CPS standards.

AreaWhat APRA requires (in brief)
CapitalA risk-based prescribed capital amount covering insurance, asset, concentration (catastrophe) and operational risks. Insurers typically hold a board-approved target above the minimum.
ReinsuranceA reinsurance management strategy approved by the board. For general insurers, reinsurance is central to surviving catastrophes such as floods, bushfires and cyclones.
ActuarialAn Appointed Actuary who gives independent advice, including an annual Financial Condition Report to the board. Actuaries also value insurance liabilities (claims reserves).
Governance and riskCPS 510, CPS 520, CPS 220, CPS 230, CPS 234, CPS 511 and CPS 190 (recovery and exit planning).
Climate riskAPRA's guide CPG 229 sets out its expectations for managing climate-related financial risks. For general insurers these are very real, through catastrophe losses and affordability.

The Insurance Contracts Act

RuleIn plain English
Utmost good faith (s 13)Both insurer and insured must act with the utmost good faith, which is more than honesty and includes fairness and consistency. ASIC can take action where an insurer fails to meet it.
Duty to take reasonable care (s 20B)For consumer contracts entered into or renewed from 5 October 2021, customers must take reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation. This replaced the old duty of disclosure, and the insurer must ask clear, specific questions.
Remedies for misrepresentation or non-disclosureThe insurer's remedy is generally limited to putting itself in the position it would have been in, unless the conduct was fraudulent.
Unfair contract termsSince 5 April 2021, the unfair contract terms laws apply to insurance contracts.

Sales and distribution reforms

  • Hawking ban (from 5 October 2021): no offering insurance and other financial products during unsolicited contact with retail clients.
  • Add-on insurance deferred sales model (from 5 October 2021): add-on insurance sold alongside cars and other goods generally can't be sold until four days after the customer commits to the main purchase.
  • Design and distribution: target market determinations for most retail insurance (RG 274).
  • Life insurance advice commissions are capped under the Life Insurance Framework reforms.
  • Pricing: ASIC has taken action where insurers didn't deliver the loyalty or multi-policy discounts they promised, which has led to large remediation programs.

Claims handling

Claims are the moment of truth for insurance. The Hayne reforms made handling and settling claims a financial service from 1 January 2021, with a transition period. Since 1 January 2022, insurers have needed an AFS licence authorisation covering it and must handle claims efficiently, honestly and fairly. Common risk areas include:

  • delays, especially after catastrophes and in complex income protection or TPD claims
  • excessive or intrusive information requests, and surveillance
  • poor handling of vulnerable customers
  • oversight of outsourced claims managers, assessors, builders and investigators. See Third-party risk.

Codes of practice

CodeStatus (as at September 2026)
General Insurance Code of Practice (Insurance Council of Australia)The 2020 Code applies. A redrafted Code has been consulted on, with lodgement for ASIC approval expected in late 2026 and a transition period of about two years after approval.
Life Insurance Code of Practice (Council of Australian Life Insurers)The current version took effect on 1 March 2025. An independent review ran in 2025–26.
Private Health Insurance Code of Conduct (Private Healthcare Australia and Members Health Fund Alliance)Self-regulatory industry code for private health insurers, with a Code Compliance Committee.

Code breaches don't usually count as breaches of the law by themselves, but they can be relevant to the "efficiently, honestly and fairly" obligation and to AFCA decisions. They also have their own monitoring bodies.

Accountability

General, life and private health insurers have been covered by the Financial Accountability Regime since 15 March 2025.

Check your understanding

1. A customer renewing their home insurance in 2026 gave an inaccurate answer to a vague question about previous claims. Which duty applied to the customer?

For consumer contracts from 5 October 2021, s 20B applies. How clear the insurer's question was is one of the circumstances considered.

2. Why can ASIC take action over poor claims handling?

Because claims handling is a financial service, the licensee's general obligations, including acting efficiently, honestly and fairly, apply to it.

Common weak spots across insurance

  • Claims handling, especially after disasters, where delays and poor communication drive complaints.
  • Pricing, including loyalty penalties and pricing promises that weren't kept.
  • Distribution, where target market determinations and sales practices don't match.
  • Legacy systems and products that make it hard to apply discounts or terms correctly.

Questions a board can ask

These are prompts for discussion, not requirements.

  1. What do our claims complaints and AFCA outcomes tell us about how we treat customers?
  2. Where could our pricing be unfair to long-standing customers?
  3. How do we know our products reach the customers they are designed for?

Frequently asked questions

Which guide should I read?

Start with General insurance, Life insurance or Private health insurance. This page gives the overview that applies across them.

Is claims handling a financial service?

Yes, since the 2021 reforms, for many insurance products. This brings the general obligations of AFS licensees, including acting efficiently, honestly and fairly, to claims.
