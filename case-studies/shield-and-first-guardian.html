The Shield and First Guardian collapses

How thousands of Australians' super ended up in two collapsed schemes, and the lessons for advisers, platform trustees and everyone in between.

Intermediate About 12 minutes Last reviewed: 28 September 2026 Checked against sources
What
Collapse of the Shield Master Fund and First Guardian Master Fund
When
Investments from about 2020; collapses in 2024 and 2025; proceedings continuing in 2026
Who
Investors, lead generators, financial advisers, platform super trustees, responsible entities and ASIC
Outcome (so far)
Court declarations against platform trustees, compensation of some members, bans and multiple proceedings

Key takeaways

  • Around 11,000 Australians invested about $1.1 billion, mostly super, into two schemes that collapsed.
  • Many were cold-called, advised to switch super, and invested through platform super funds that listed the schemes as options.
  • ASIC has taken action across the chain. The Federal Court has declared that platform trustees Macquarie (March 2026) and Netwealth (August 2026) contravened the Corporations Act.
  • The biggest lessons are for platform trustees: due diligence on options, monitoring unusual inflows, and managing conflicts in the distribution chain.

A developing story

Many proceedings are still before the courts. Allegations are unproven unless admitted by the party or decided by a court. This page records outcomes as they are published and was last checked on 28 September 2026.

  1. 2020 to 2024Around 11,000 Australians invest about $1.1 billion in the Shield Master Fund and the First Guardian Master Fund, mostly by rolling super out of existing funds into superannuation platforms that offered these schemes as investment options. Many were contacted through cold calls and lead generators, then advised to switch.
  2. February 2024ASIC acts to stop further investment in Shield.
  3. February 2025ASIC obtains court orders freezing First Guardian's assets. Both schemes are later placed in liquidation.
  4. 2025ASIC brings a series of actions across the chain, including against advice businesses, platform trustees and others. Macquarie admits Shield contraventions and commits to pay affected members of its platform the amounts they invested, less withdrawals, about $321 million.
  5. February 2026ASIC starts contacting investors directly with information on how to seek redress, because fewer than 2,000 of around 11,000 investors had lodged complaints with AFCA.
  6. March 2026The Federal Court declares that Macquarie, as platform trustee, contravened the Corporations Act by failing to place the Shield options on a watch list for heightened monitoring.
  7. 2026ASIC sues Equity Trustees over alleged First Guardian onboarding failures, calls platform trustees to account over persistent failures, and in August the Federal Court declares that Netwealth contravened the Corporations Act in relation to First Guardian.

How the harm happened: the chain

What went wrong

The failures ASIC has alleged or courts have found span the whole chain:

  • Lead generation and advice: people were cold-called and steered into switching super, sometimes with conflicted payments between scheme-related entities and advice businesses (alleged in ASIC's proceedings).
  • Platform trustees: the key question is whether trustees did enough due diligence before adding high-risk options to their platforms, and enough monitoring once money flowed in. The Federal Court declared Macquarie should have placed Shield on a watch list for heightened monitoring.
  • Scheme operators: how the money was invested, and whether the schemes were operated properly.
  • Gatekeepers: ASIC has also looked at the roles of research houses and others that gave the schemes credibility.

The regulatory response

  • ASIC made Shield and First Guardian a top enforcement priority, with multiple proceedings against advisers, trustees and other parties.
  • Court-enforceable undertakings and court declarations against platform trustees, and compensation of affected members in some cases.
  • Direct outreach to investors about AFCA complaints and redress.
  • A spotlight on platform trustees' oversight of investment menus, and on the Compensation Scheme of Last Resort and its funding.
  • A pause, then renewed progress, on financial advice reforms. See Financial advice regulation.

Lessons for risk and compliance

  • Platform trustees need real due diligence and monitoring of every option, including watch lists, concentration limits and triggers for unusual inflows. See SPS 530.
  • Unusual patterns are warning signs: rapid inflows from a small group of advisers into a single high-risk option.
  • Conflicts in the distribution chain (payments to lead generators and advisers) need to be identified and managed. See RG 181.
  • Design and distribution obligations apply: products should reach the people they are designed for. See RG 274.
  • Remediation and communication with affected members matter as much as enforcement. See RG 277.

What would you have done?

You run investment governance for a platform trustee. A little-known managed fund option receives $40 million in three months, almost all from members advised by three advisers. What do you do?

Treat it as a red flag. Put the option on a watch list, review your due diligence, look at who the advisers are and how members were sourced, consider pausing new investments, and escalate to the investment committee and risk. Document the reasoning either way.

A member calls asking to switch their whole balance into a high-risk option recommended by an adviser they met through a cold call. What should the fund consider?

Whether warnings are appropriate, whether the adviser and the pattern of switches raise concerns that need escalating, whether the trustee's own design and distribution and monitoring obligations are engaged, and whether the member understands the risks.

Check your understanding

1. Roughly how much did Australians invest in Shield and First Guardian?

ASIC has said around 11,000 Australians invested about $1.1 billion.

2. What did the Federal Court declare Macquarie should have done?

The court declared Macquarie contravened the Act by failing to place Shield on a watch list.

3. Which link in the chain is responsible for which investment options are available to members of a platform super fund?

Platform trustees decide which investment options are on their menus and must monitor them.
