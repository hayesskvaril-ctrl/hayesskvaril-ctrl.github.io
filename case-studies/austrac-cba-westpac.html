AUSTRAC v CBA (2018) and AUSTRAC v Westpac (2020)

Two record penalties for anti-money laundering failures, and why they started with new products and automated systems nobody checked.

Intermediate About 13 minutes Last reviewed: 8 October 2026 Checked against sources
What
Civil penalty proceedings under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006
When
CBA: 2017 to 2018. Westpac: 2019 to 2020.
Who
AUSTRAC, Commonwealth Bank of Australia and Westpac
Outcome
Penalties of $700 million (CBA) and $1.3 billion (Westpac)

Key takeaways

  • CBA admitted 53,750 contraventions linked to intelligent deposit machines it rolled out without assessing their money laundering risk, and paid a $700 million penalty.
  • Westpac admitted more than 23 million contraventions, mainly failures to report international funds transfers, and paid a $1.3 billion penalty.
  • Both cases show how untested systems and unassessed new products can create huge numbers of breaches.
  • They made AML/CTF a board-level issue across the financial sector.
  1. 2012CBA introduces intelligent deposit machines (IDMs), which accept large cash deposits and transfer funds, without assessing their money laundering risk.
  2. August 2017AUSTRAC starts civil penalty proceedings against CBA.
  3. June 2018CBA agrees to a $700 million penalty, then the largest civil penalty in Australian corporate history, admitting 53,750 contraventions.
  4. November 2019AUSTRAC starts proceedings against Westpac.
  5. September to October 2020Westpac agrees to a $1.3 billion penalty, approved by the Federal Court in October 2020, admitting more than 23 million contraventions.

What went wrong

FeatureCBA (2018)Westpac (2020)
Core failureIDMs were rolled out without assessing their money laundering and terrorism financing risk; criminals used them to deposit cash.Systems failed to report international funds transfer instructions to AUSTRAC.
Reporting53,506 threshold transaction reports (for cash of $10,000 or more through IDMs) were lodged late, between 2012 and 2015.More than 19.5 million international funds transfer instructions, worth over $11 billion, were not reported properly.
MonitoringFailures to monitor transactions and report suspicious matters on time.Inadequate monitoring, including of transactions associated with possible child exploitation.
Penalty$700 million$1.3 billion

The regulatory response

  • Record civil penalties, which signalled that AML/CTF failures would be treated as seriously as any other breach.
  • Wider consequences followed: executive departures, remediation programs, and scrutiny of governance and accountability.
  • AUSTRAC continued to bring large cases against other reporting entities, and Australia's AML/CTF laws were substantially reformed from 2026. See AML/CTF fundamentals.

Lessons for risk and compliance

  • Assess risk before launching new products or channels. CBA's failure started with a new product rolled out without an ML/TF risk assessment. See Change risk.
  • Automated reporting needs testing. Systems can fail silently for years. Reconcile what should be reported with what was reported.
  • Scale multiplies harm. A single systems flaw can create millions of contraventions.
  • Transaction monitoring must be tuned and reviewed, including for new typologies such as child exploitation payments.
  • Treat regulators' early concerns seriously and remediate quickly.

What came after

AUSTRAC has continued to bring large cases, now mainly in gambling:

CaseOutcome or status

Frequently asked questions

Why were the penalties so large?

Because each failure to report was a separate contravention, and there were millions of them. Courts also weighed how long the failures lasted and the risk that criminals exploited them.

What would you have done?

Your bank is about to launch a new real-time payments feature. The launch date is fixed. What must happen first from an AML/CTF perspective?

An ML/TF risk assessment of the new product, with controls such as limits, monitoring rules and reporting built in before launch, and approval by the AML/CTF compliance officer. If the risk assessment isn't done, the launch should wait.

A reconciliation shows the number of international transfer reports sent to AUSTRAC is lower than the number of transfers processed. What do you do?

Treat it as a potential reporting failure: investigate the cause and the period affected, fix the system, lodge overdue reports, and assess notification to AUSTRAC and other reporting obligations. Look for other data flows that might fail the same way.

Check your understanding

1. What was the root of CBA's AML/CTF failure?

CBA introduced IDMs without an ML/TF risk assessment, and criminals exploited them.

2. Westpac's main reporting failure involved:

Over 19.5 million international funds transfer instructions were not properly reported.

3. Which lesson applies most directly to launching a new product?

A risk assessment before launch is a core AML/CTF program requirement.
