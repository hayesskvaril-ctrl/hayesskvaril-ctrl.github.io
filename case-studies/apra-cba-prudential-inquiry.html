APRA Prudential Inquiry into CBA (2018)

How APRA's inquiry into Australia's largest bank found that success had dulled its sensitivity to risk, and why its findings became a benchmark for the whole industry.

Intermediate About 14 minutes Last reviewed: 8 October 2026 Checked against sources
What
Prudential Inquiry into the Commonwealth Bank of Australia
When
Announced August 2017; final report May 2018
Who
Commonwealth Bank of Australia (CBA) and APRA
Outcome
35 recommendations accepted, an enforceable undertaking and a $1 billion capital add-on

Key takeaways

  • APRA's inquiry found shortcomings in CBA's governance, culture and accountability, especially in managing non-financial risks.
  • The panel concluded that continued financial success had dulled the bank's sensitivity to warning signs.
  • CBA accepted all 35 recommendations under an enforceable undertaking, and APRA applied a $1 billion capital add-on.
  • APRA asked other large institutions to assess themselves against the findings, and found the weaknesses were not unique to CBA.
  1. 2017A series of incidents damages CBA's reputation, including AUSTRAC's money laundering proceedings and problems in its financial advice and insurance businesses.
  2. 28 August 2017APRA announces a prudential inquiry into CBA's governance, culture and accountability. The panel is Dr John Laker, Jillian Broadbent and Professor Graeme Samuel.
  3. 1 May 2018The final report is released with 35 recommendations. APRA accepts an enforceable undertaking from CBA and applies a $1 billion add-on to CBA's minimum capital requirement.
  4. 2018APRA asks the 36 largest regulated entities to assess themselves against the report's findings. Its 2019 information paper finds similar weaknesses elsewhere.
  5. 2021CBA reports completing its remedial action plan.

What went wrong

The panel's overarching conclusion was that CBA's continued financial success had dulled its senses to signals that should have alerted the board and senior executives to a deteriorating risk profile. The findings centred on non-financial risks (operational, compliance and conduct):

AreaFinding (summarised)
Board oversightInadequate oversight and challenge of non-financial risks by the board and its committees.
Executive leadershipWeak executive oversight of non-financial risks, and slow responses to known issues.
Risk managementA risk function lacking the stature and resources to challenge the business effectively.
AccountabilityUnclear accountabilities, with a collective, consensus-driven style that let issues drift.
RemunerationRemuneration had little effect as a consequence for poor risk outcomes.
CultureComplacency, a reactive approach to risk, and a focus on process rather than outcomes.

The regulatory response

  • An enforceable undertaking requiring CBA to implement a remedial action plan, with independent reviews of progress.
  • A $1 billion capital add-on reflecting the elevated operational risk.
  • APRA used the report as a benchmark for the industry, asking large entities to self-assess, and increased its focus on governance, culture, remuneration and accountability, including the later revision of CPS 511.

Lessons for risk and compliance

  • Success can hide risk. Strong financial results can make boards and executives less alert to warning signs.
  • Non-financial risk needs the same rigour as financial risk, with clear metrics, ownership and board challenge. See Board risk reporting design.
  • Known issues must be closed. Slow remediation of identified issues was a major theme. See Issue and action management.
  • The second line needs stature, meaning authority, resources and access to the board.
  • Accountability must be clear, which later became central to the FAR.

From the inquiry's findings to today's rules

The inquiry's themes run through APRA's later standards:

Finding (2018)Where it shows up now
Unclear accountability for non-financial risksThe Financial Accountability Regime and accountability maps
Pay that didn't reflect risk outcomesCPS 511 Remuneration
Complacency and weak risk cultureCPS 220's requirement for the board to form a view of risk culture
Non-financial risk treated as less important than financial riskCPS 230 Operational Risk Management
Slow issue resolutionSupervisory focus on issue management and remediation

Frequently asked questions

Why did APRA add $1 billion to CBA's capital requirement?

As a signal and a buffer: APRA treated the weaknesses in managing non-financial risk as a reason to hold more operational risk capital until they were fixed. See APS 115.

What would you have done?

You are a new non-executive director. Profits are at record levels, but the risk report shows dozens of overdue audit actions. How do you raise it?

Ask why the actions are overdue, who is accountable, and what the trend is. Ask for thematic analysis and whether any overdue actions relate to customer harm or regulatory obligations. Record the discussion and ask for a follow-up at the next meeting.

As CRO, you believe the risk function lacks the resources to challenge the business. What do you do?

Put the case to the CEO and to the board risk committee directly, with evidence: coverage gaps, overdue reviews, and comparison with peers. Under CPS 220 the CRO has direct access to the board for exactly this kind of issue.
