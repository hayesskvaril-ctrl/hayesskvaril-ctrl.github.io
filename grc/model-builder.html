GRC model builder

Describe an organisation in five short steps and get a linked, illustrative GRC model: frameworks, a three-level risk taxonomy with controls and KRIs, obligations and notification clocks, committees, the data model and a maturity roadmap, ready to export to Excel.

About 15 minutes Last reviewed: 1 October 2026

Key takeaways

  • Answer five short steps, or start from one of six examples. Suggestions come from the guides on this site, and you can change every selection.
  • Every record links to the next: obligations to Level 2 risks, risks to controls and key risk indicators, indicators to appetite metrics, and all of them to the committees that oversee them.
  • Consistency checks flag gaps, such as a regime whose risks are not in your taxonomy or a group without entity-level reporting.
  • Everything runs in your browser. Your answers stay on this device unless you copy a share link, and you can export the model to Excel, CSV, JSON or PDF.
  • The model is general and illustrative. It is not advice and is not a complete list of the legal requirements for your organisation.

How the builder works

The builder combines your answers with a reference library drawn from the guides on this site.

  • Entity types suggest the regimes, risk categories and operating features that usually apply. Counting more than one entity turns the model into a group model, which adds a group governance framework, a Level 2 risk for intra-group arrangements and reporting for each entity.
  • Regimes bring their documents, obligation themes, notification clocks and calendar items. Each obligation theme links to the Level 2 risks it relates to, and through them to controls, so you can see which controls support which obligations.
  • Risk categories follow a three-level taxonomy: up to 16 Level 1 categories, more than 70 Level 2 sub-types, and an example Level 3 risk for each sub-type with causes, impacts, controls and key risk indicators. A sub-type is included when it fits your entity types or operating model, or when a regime you chose needs it.
  • Size, structure and maturity shape the committees, the suggested operating model, the target maturity and the length of the indicative roadmap. Your challenges set the quick wins and the order of the implementation waves.

IDs follow one pattern so the records link up. OPS-02 is a Level 2 sub-type; R-OPS-02.1 is its first example risk, C-OPS-02.1 its first control and K-OPS-02.1 its first key risk indicator; OB-CPS230-07 is the seventh CPS 230 obligation theme; and D-01 is the first framework document.

What the builder does not do

  • It does not tell you which laws apply. Applicability depends on licences, products, size and thresholds, so check the official sources and get advice.
  • It does not assess or rate your risks. The example risks, controls, indicators and appetite positions are prompts to adapt, not your risk profile.
  • The obligation themes summarise; they are not an obligations register. A full register breaks each theme into individual obligations with citations, interpretations and owners.
  • The roadmap durations are indicative. Real programs vary widely with scope, budget, people and technology choices.

The thinking behind each part of the model is explained across the GRC systems section. Start with establishing a GRC system.
