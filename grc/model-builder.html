GRC model builder

Answer four questions about an organisation and get an illustrative GRC model to start from: the framework documents, a three-level risk taxonomy, committees, the data model that links it all, a calendar and a roadmap.

Intermediate About 10 minutes Last reviewed: 1 October 2026

Key takeaways

  • The builder suggests the regimes and risk categories that usually apply to each type of organisation. You can change every selection.
  • The output follows the structure used across this section: policy hierarchy, enterprise and Level 1 to 3 risks, committees, data model, obligations, calendar and roadmap.
  • Everything runs in your browser. Your answers are saved only on this device, and you can download the result as a spreadsheet or print it.
  • The model is general and illustrative. It is not advice and is not a list of legal requirements for your organisation.

How the builder works

The builder combines three things: a core set of GRC documents every organisation needs (a risk management framework, risk appetite, methodology, compliance framework, incident and issue processes, control framework and internal audit charter); the documents, regulators and features that each regime you select usually brings (for example CPS 230 adds critical operations, tolerance levels and service provider management); and a library of Level 1 risk categories with Level 2 sub-types and example Level 3 risks. Committees scale with the structure you choose, and the roadmap scales with structure and maturity.

The approach is explained across the GRC systems section. Start with establishing a GRC system to see how a model like this is turned into a working system.

What the builder does not do

  • It does not tell you which laws apply to your organisation. Applicability depends on licences, products, size and thresholds; check the official sources and get advice.
  • It does not assess your risks or rate them. The example Level 3 risks are prompts, not your risks.
  • It does not replace the design work. Use it to start discussions about scope, structure and priorities.
