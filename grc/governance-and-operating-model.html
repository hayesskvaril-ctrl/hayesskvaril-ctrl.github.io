GRC governance and operating model

The operating model is the human side of GRC: which committees decide what, who does each task across the three lines, how documents fit together, and the rhythm of reporting and review across the year.

Intermediate About 15 minutes Last reviewed: 1 October 2026 Checked against sources

Key takeaways

  • Committees should map to the risk hierarchy: the board and its risk committee oversee enterprise and Level 1 risks; executive and category committees manage Level 2; business unit forums manage Level 3.
  • The three lines need written GRC roles: the first line owns risks, obligations and controls; the second line sets the framework and challenges; internal audit gives independent assurance.
  • A policy hierarchy (framework, policy, standard, procedure) prevents contradictions and makes clear who can approve each level.
  • Escalation rules link appetite to action: what happens, and how fast, when a metric breaches a trigger or limit.
  • An annual GRC calendar ties planning, appetite review, assessments, testing, reporting and the framework review together.

Committee structure

Each committee needs a charter that says what it decides, what it recommends upwards, what it receives, how often it meets and who attends. A common failure is committees that receive the same reports at three levels without anyone making a decision. Link the committee structure to the risk hierarchy: the board risk committee oversees enterprise and Level 1 risks against appetite; category committees own Level 2 types; business unit forums manage Level 3 risks.

The policy hierarchy

LevelPurposeTypical approverExamples
1. Board-level frameworks and statementsSet direction, appetite and the overall approachBoardRisk management framework, risk appetite statement, risk management strategy, compliance framework, business continuity framework
2. PoliciesSet principles and mandatory requirements for a risk type or topicBoard or board committeeOperational risk, information security, outsourcing and service providers, conflicts, whistleblower, AML/CTF, privacy
3. StandardsSet detailed, measurable minimum requirementsExecutive or second-line ownerControl standards, rating scales, incident classification, testing methodology
4. Procedures and guidelinesExplain how to do the workBusiness ownerProcess procedures, desk instructions, templates

Escalation rules

Appetite only works if breaches lead to action. Each appetite metric needs a trigger (early warning) and a limit, and the framework should state who must be told and how fast. For example: amber trigger reported to the category committee at its next meeting with an action plan; red limit reported to the chief risk officer within two business days and to the board risk committee chair within five, with a remediation plan at the next meeting. Regulatory notification timeframes (for example under CPS 230 or breach reporting rules) run separately and must be built into incident workflows.
