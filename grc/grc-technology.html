GRC technology: choosing and running the platform

Technology makes an integrated GRC system practical at scale, but only if it follows the design. This guide covers the usual modules, how to set requirements, build versus buy, integration and data, and how to keep the platform healthy after go-live.

Intermediate About 14 minutes Last reviewed: 1 October 2026 Checked against sources

Key takeaways

  • Choose technology after the design: requirements should come from your taxonomies, data model and operating model, not a vendor demonstration.
  • GRC platforms are usually modular. Start with the modules that support your highest-priority processes and data links.
  • Spreadsheets can work for small organisations, but they break down when many people must maintain linked records across entities.
  • Integration with HR, finance, IT service management and data sources is what turns a register into a live system.
  • After go-live, a design authority must control configuration changes, reference data and access, or the system drifts.

Typical modules

Commercial GRC platforms (and in-house builds) are usually organised into modules that share common reference data. Names vary by product; this site does not recommend particular vendors.

ModuleWhat it supports
Risk managementRisk registers at every level, assessments (RCSA), ratings, treatment plans, risk profile against appetite
Compliance and obligationsObligations library, applicability, attestations, compliance monitoring plans and results
Controls and testingControl library, testing workflows and workpapers, assurance mapping
Incidents and breachesIncident capture, triage, significance and reportability assessment, regulatory notification tracking, losses
Issues and actionsIssues from all sources, action plans, due dates, validation and closure
Key risk indicatorsIndicator library, thresholds linked to appetite, data feeds, breach workflows
Regulatory changeHorizon scanning, impact assessment, change actions
PoliciesPolicy register, approvals, review dates, attestations
Operational resilienceCritical operations, tolerance levels, process and resource mapping, scenario testing (CPS 230)
Third partiesService provider register, due diligence, contracts, monitoring, concentration and exit planning
Internal auditAudit universe, plans, workpapers, findings (often a separate module with restricted access)
ReportingDashboards and committee packs built from the linked data

Writing requirements

Good requirements describe what the organisation must be able to do, using its own design. Examples:

  • Hold a three-level risk taxonomy and enforce that every Level 3 risk links to exactly one Level 2 type.
  • Link controls many-to-many to risks, obligations, processes and critical operations, and roll control test results up to residual risk ratings.
  • Tag every record to legal entity and business unit, and produce each entity's risk profile and the group view from the same data.
  • Track regulatory notification deadlines from incident detection, with alerts.
  • Keep full version history and show the profile as at any past date.
  • Give different access to different users (for example restricting internal audit workpapers and whistleblower-related incidents).
  • Import and export in open formats, so the organisation is not locked in.

Build, buy or improve what you have

OptionStrengthsWatch-outs
Well-governed spreadsheets and shared registersCheap, flexible, quick to changeLinks break, version control is weak, hard to scale across entities, poor audit trail
Configure a commercial GRC platformProven workflows, audit trails, vendor support, regulatory content optionsLicence and implementation cost; risk of adopting vendor defaults instead of your design; customisation can make upgrades hard
Extend an enterprise platform you already use (for example IT service management or low-code tools)Shared data with IT processes, existing skills and licencesNeeds strong design discipline; GRC-specific features may need building
Build in-houseFits exactlyExpensive to maintain, key-person risk, slow to change

Whatever the choice, a GRC platform that holds critical operational and customer data may itself be an important system, and if it is provided by a third party it should go through the organisation's service provider and information security processes (see third-party risk and CPS 234).

Integration points

  • HR system: people, roles and reporting lines, so owners update automatically when people move.
  • Organisation and entity master data: legal entities and cost centres from finance.
  • IT service management: technology incidents, changes, assets and configuration items.
  • Data sources for KRIs: operational systems and the data warehouse.
  • Complaints, whistleblowing and fraud systems: with careful access control.
  • Board and committee reporting tools: to publish packs from the linked data.

Data migration

Migration is where integration succeeds or fails. Clean before you load: rationalise controls, rewrite risks as events, map obligations to the new structure and confirm owners. Load in waves, reconcile counts and ratings with the old registers, and get business owners to confirm their records. Budget generously: in large organisations this is often the biggest single workstream.

After go-live

  • A design authority approves changes to taxonomies, reference lists, workflows and reports.
  • A small platform team supports users, maintains integrations and manages releases.
  • Data quality checks run monthly: orphan records, missing owners, overdue reviews, risks without controls.
  • Access reviews run at least yearly, as for any important system.
