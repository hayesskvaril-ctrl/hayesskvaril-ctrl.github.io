The GRC data model: how everything links

The data model is what makes GRC integrated. It defines every type of record in the system and how each one links to the others, so that one control test, incident or KRI breach shows up everywhere it matters.

Intermediate About 18 minutes Last reviewed: 1 October 2026 Checked against sources

Key takeaways

  • A GRC system holds around twenty types of record, from strategy and appetite to controls, incidents and service providers. The value is in the links between them.
  • Control objectives are the bridge: risks and obligations link to control objectives, and control objectives link to controls.
  • Everything should be tagged to the organisation hierarchy (legal entity and business unit) and, where relevant, the process hierarchy, so any view can be cut by entity or process.
  • Follow a thread to test your design: from a strategic objective to a KRI breach to the board report, every step should be a link, not a manual reconciliation.
  • Good data design rules matter as much as the diagram: unique identifiers, one owner per record, version history, and controlled reference lists.

Some objects only apply to some organisations: critical operations (CPS 230), information assets (CPS 234), accountable persons (FAR) and the AML/CTF program (reporting entities). The GRC model builder shows the version that fits a given organisation.

Follow one thread

The best test of a GRC design is to follow a single thread from the board to the front line and back. Here is one for a super fund (illustrative).

  1. Strategy and objectives

    "Deliver accurate, timely member services."

  2. Risk appetite

    Operational risk appetite: no disruption to critical operations beyond tolerance; metric: contributions allocated within 3 business days 99% of the time.

  3. Risk hierarchy

    Level 1 Operational → Level 2 Third party → Level 3 "Contributions processing disrupted because the administrator's system fails and recovery is not tested".

  4. Critical operation and provider

    The risk is linked to the critical operation "contributions processing" (with its tolerance levels) and to the administrator, a material service provider.

  5. Obligations

    The same control objective also supports obligations to allocate contributions correctly and to manage material service providers under CPS 230.

  6. Control objective and controls

    "Contributions continue to be processed within tolerance during a disruption", achieved by the administrator's tested recovery plan, a manual workaround and daily allocation monitoring.

  7. Testing

    Internal audit finds the recovery plan has not been tested end to end. The control is rated "needs improvement".

  8. Issue and action

    An issue is raised with the accountable executive, due in 90 days; the residual rating of the Level 3 risk rises automatically.

  9. KRI and incident

    Meanwhile the allocation KRI hits amber after an outage; the outage is logged as an incident, linked to the same risk and critical operation, and assessed for reportability.

  10. Reporting

    The board risk committee sees the operational risk profile against appetite, with the incident, the open issue and the KRI trend, all drawn from the linked records.

Design rules for the data

  • Unique identifiers for every record (for example R-OPS-0142, C-0731, OB-ASIC-0099), never reused.
  • One owner per record: a role and a person, kept current as people change jobs.
  • Controlled reference lists for the taxonomy, rating scales, entities, processes and regulators, changed only through approval.
  • Many-to-many links held as links, not as text in a comments field.
  • Version history and effective dates, so the organisation can show what the risk profile looked like on any past date (useful for regulators and audits).
  • Tag everything to the organisation hierarchy, so a group view and each legal entity's view come from the same data.
  • Record the evidence, not just the conclusion: test workpapers, incident timelines and approvals.
