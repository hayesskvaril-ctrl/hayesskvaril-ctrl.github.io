Financial Accountability Regime (FAR)

The FAR makes it clear, in writing and on a regulator's register, which senior person is accountable for each part of a bank, insurer or super fund, and what happens if they fall short.

Intermediate About 13 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026 Checked against sources

Key takeaways

  • The Financial Accountability Regime Act 2023 (Cth) applies to banks (from 15 March 2024) and to insurers and super trustees (from 15 March 2025). It replaced the Banking Executive Accountability Regime (BEAR).
  • It is jointly administered by APRA and ASIC.
  • Directors and senior executives with senior responsibility for key parts of the business are accountable persons and must be registered with the regulators.
  • Each accountable person has an accountability statement; each entity has an accountability map.
  • At least 40% of accountable persons' variable remuneration must be deferred for at least four years, and reduced for failures to meet obligations.

Put it into practice: Board briefing

Why the FAR exists

Before the FAR, when things went badly wrong in large financial institutions, it was often hard to say who was actually responsible. Responsibility was spread across committees, matrix structures and unclear job descriptions. The 2019 Financial Services Royal Commission recommended extending the banking accountability regime (BEAR, introduced in 2018) to insurers and super trustees and strengthening it. The FAR is the result.

The idea is simple: for every significant part of the business, there should be a named, senior person who is accountable, who knows it, and whom the regulators know about.

How the FAR fits together

Who is covered?

  • Accountable entities: authorised deposit-taking institutions (banks) and their authorised holding companies; general, life and private health insurers and their holding companies; and RSE licensees (super trustees). Significant related entities in the group can also be covered.
  • Accountable persons: people with actual or effective senior executive responsibility for management or control of the entity or a significant or substantial part of it, and people who hold positions or responsibilities prescribed in the Minister Rules. The rules prescribe board membership as a position, and responsibilities that capture roles such as the CEO, CFO, Chief Risk Officer and Head of Internal Audit. A person isn't accountable for an activity merely because they carry it out.

What accountable persons must do

Accountable persons must:

  • act with honesty and integrity, and with due skill, care and diligence;
  • deal with APRA and ASIC in an open, constructive and co-operative way; and
  • take reasonable steps in conducting their responsibilities to prevent matters arising that would adversely affect the entity's prudential standing or prudential reputation.

They must also take reasonable steps to make sure the entity complies with key obligations within their area of responsibility.

What the entity must do

ObligationIn brief
AccountabilityMeet the same core standards as accountable persons (honesty, integrity, care, co-operation) at an entity level.
Key personnelMake sure every responsibility that must be covered is allocated to an accountable person, and take reasonable steps so that accountable persons meet their obligations and are suitable.
Registration and documentsRegister accountable persons with the regulators (applications are generally due at least 21 days before the person takes up the role), with an accountability statement for each, and maintain an accountability map for the entity. Material changes must be notified, generally within 30 days.
Deferred remunerationDefer at least 40% of each accountable person's variable remuneration for at least four years, and reduce it where the person has failed to meet their obligations.
NotificationNotify the regulators of specified changes and events, such as changes to accountable persons and their responsibilities.

Try it: an accountability map

An illustrative accountability map for a super trustee. Pick a responsibility to see who holds it. Real maps are more detailed, and roles vary between entities.

A good accountability map has no gaps (every important responsibility is allocated) and no unexplained overlaps (shared responsibilities are clearly described). Building one often exposes areas where nobody, or everybody, thought they were in charge.

Enforcement

  • Entities face civil penalties for breaching their obligations. The maximum is very large, linked to penalty units, the benefit obtained and annual turnover.
  • Individuals can be disqualified from being accountable persons, and their deferred variable remuneration can be reduced.
  • APRA and ASIC can also direct entities to reallocate responsibilities, and investigate jointly.

As at 2026, public enforcement under the FAR has been limited, particularly in super. Regulators have focused on implementation, including publishing observations on how entities have set up their accountability maps and statements.

Proposed simplifications (announced June 2026)

As part of the Government's Better Regulation reforms, APRA and ASIC announced changes to reduce FAR's administrative burden. Proposed legislative changes would mean accountability statements and maps are provided only on request, not up front, and would give entities more time to register accountable persons. The regulators also proposed to remove the key functions requirements from the FAR rules, raise the materiality threshold for notifying changes, and stop requiring information on accountable persons' direct reports in accountability maps. Some changes need legislation and others need rule changes, so check the current position before relying on the requirements described above.

FAR in practice: questions an accountable person might ask

  • Is my accountability statement specific enough that I know exactly what I'm accountable for?
  • What information do I receive that tells me whether my areas are working, and is it good enough?
  • If a regulator asked what "reasonable steps" I took to prevent a problem, what evidence could I show?
  • Where my area depends on another executive's area or a service provider, how do we coordinate?
