Internal audit

The independent function that tells the board whether risk management, controls and governance are really working.

Intermediate About 11 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026

Key takeaways

  • Internal audit provides independent, objective assurance to the board on governance, risk management and controls. It is the third line in the Three Lines model.
  • It reports functionally to the board audit committee, which approves its plan, protects its independence and oversees its findings.
  • The Institute of Internal Auditors' Global Internal Audit Standards took effect on 9 January 2025, with five domains and 15 principles, plus new topical requirements such as cybersecurity from February 2026.
  • APRA requires an independent internal audit function, and uses internal audit in reviews under CPS 220, CPS 230 and CPS 234.
  • Internal audit's value depends on focusing on the biggest risks, reporting clearly and following up that agreed actions are actually done.

What internal audit does

Internal audit is the third line in the Three Lines model. It gives the board and senior management independent, objective assurance and advice on whether governance, risk management and internal controls are working. Unlike the first line (the business) and the second line (risk and compliance functions), it is independent of management and reports functionally to the board audit committee.

Internal audit is…Internal audit is not…
Independent assurance over risks and controls across the whole organisationA replacement for management's own controls or the second line's oversight
Risk-based: it focuses on the areas that matter mostA checklist review of every process every year
Able to give advice, if its independence is protectedResponsible for designing or running the controls it audits
Reporting to the audit committeeReporting only to the executives it audits

The Global Internal Audit Standards

The Institute of Internal Auditors' (IIA) Global Internal Audit Standards took effect on 9 January 2025, replacing the previous International Professional Practices Framework standards. They are organised into five domains and 15 principles. Summarised in our own words:

DomainWhat it covers
1. Purpose of internal auditingWhy internal audit exists: to strengthen the organisation's ability to create, protect and sustain value through independent assurance and advice.
2. Ethics and professionalismIntegrity, objectivity, competence, due professional care and confidentiality.
3. Governing the internal audit functionThe board's role: mandate, charter, independence and oversight of the function.
4. Managing the internal audit functionStrategy, planning, resources, methodology, quality assurance and communication.
5. Performing internal audit servicesPlanning, conducting and reporting on engagements, and monitoring actions.

The IIA is adding topical requirements that set minimum expectations for auditing specific risks. The first, on cybersecurity, applies from 5 February 2026. Others, such as third-party risk and organisational behaviour, are being developed.

How an internal audit works

The audit committee

The board audit committee oversees internal audit and external audit. Typical responsibilities include approving the internal audit charter and plan, ensuring internal audit has enough resources and independence, meeting the head of internal audit without management present, reviewing significant findings and whether actions are completed, and assessing the function's performance. Under APRA's governance standards, the head of internal audit must have a reporting line, and unfettered access, to the audit committee.

APRA's expectations

  • Governance standards (CPS 510, and SPS 510 for super) require an independent, adequately resourced internal audit function and a board audit committee. APRA has proposed consolidating these into a new cross-industry CPS 510. See APRA's proposed governance standard.
  • CPS 220 requires the risk management framework to be reviewed each year for compliance and effectiveness (by internal or external audit), and a comprehensive review by independent, competent people at least every three years.
  • CPS 230 gives internal audit specific roles: periodically reviewing the business continuity plan and testing, reviewing proposed outsourcing of critical operations, and reporting to the board on compliance with the service provider management policy. CPS 234 expects internal audit to review information security controls.

Rating findings and opinions

Most internal audit functions rate each finding (for example low, medium, high, critical) based on its impact and likelihood, and give an overall opinion on the area audited (for example effective, needs improvement or ineffective). Consistent ratings matter because they drive how quickly issues must be fixed and what is reported to the board. The follow-up of agreed actions is covered in Issue and action management.
