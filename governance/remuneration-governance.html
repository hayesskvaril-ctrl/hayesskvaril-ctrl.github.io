Remuneration governance

People do what they're rewarded for. Remuneration governance is about making sure pay rewards good outcomes and sound risk management, not just short-term results.

Intermediate About 15 minutes Last reviewed: 9 October 2026 Checked against sources

Key takeaways

  • Poorly designed incentives were a recurring cause of the misconduct examined by the 2019 Royal Commission and earlier reviews.
  • APRA's CPS 511 Remuneration (fully in force since 2024) requires remuneration frameworks that promote effective risk management and good customer outcomes, with the ability to reduce variable remuneration for poor risk and conduct outcomes.
  • Large entities ("significant financial institutions") face extra rules: material weight on non-financial measures, minimum deferral (60% over six years for the CEO) and clawback.
  • The FAR separately requires at least 40% of accountable persons' variable pay to be deferred for four years.
  • Conflicted remuneration for financial advice is largely banned, and listed companies face the "two strikes" rule on remuneration reports.

Why remuneration is a risk issue

If a salesperson earns a bonus for every product sold, they have a reason to sell products customers don't need. If an executive's bonus depends only on this year's profit, they have a reason to cut corners on long-term risks. Remuneration shapes behaviour, and behaviour shapes risk.

In 2017, the Retail Banking Remuneration Review led by Stephen Sedgwick examined product sales commissions and bonuses for bank staff and made 21 recommendations to better align incentives with good customer outcomes. The 2019 Financial Services Royal Commission then found that incentive arrangements had contributed to misconduct across the industry and recommended stronger regulatory oversight of remuneration, which led to APRA's revised CPS 511.

The building blocks of pay

ComponentWhat it isRisk considerations
Fixed remunerationBase salary and superannuationShould be enough that people don't rely on bonuses to live
Short-term incentive (STI)An annual bonus based on performance over the yearCan encourage short-termism if based only on financial results
Long-term incentive (LTI)Awards (often shares or rights) that vest after several years if targets are metBetter alignment with long-term outcomes, but complex
Deferred remunerationPart of variable pay held back for several yearsAllows adjustment if problems emerge later

APRA's CPS 511 Remuneration

CPS 511 applied to significant financial institutions (SFIs) that are banks from 1 January 2023, to SFI insurers and super trustees from 1 July 2023, and to all other APRA-regulated entities from 1 January 2024. In summary:

All APRA-regulated entities

  • A board-approved remuneration framework that supports the entity's strategy and risk appetite and promotes effective risk management, sustainable performance and good customer outcomes.
  • Board oversight of remuneration for senior managers and other specified roles.
  • Variable remuneration must be capable of being adjusted downwards, potentially to zero, for adverse risk and conduct outcomes, in proportion to how serious they are. Tools include in-period adjustment, malus and clawback.
  • Public disclosure about remuneration arrangements (APRA added disclosure requirements, published by entities as "CPS 511 remuneration disclosures").

Significant financial institutions (SFIs) also

  • Must have a board remuneration committee.
  • Must give material weight to non-financial measures (such as risk, compliance and customer outcomes) in variable remuneration.
  • Must defer variable remuneration: at least 60% for the CEO over at least 6 years, and at least 40% for other senior managers and executive directors over at least 5 years, with pro-rata vesting permitted from year 4, and at least 40% for highly paid material risk-takers over at least 4 years, vesting from year 2 (CPS 511 paragraph 41).
  • Must be able to claw back vested variable remuneration for at least 2 years after vesting.

The FAR and remuneration

Under the Financial Accountability Regime, entities must defer at least 40% of each accountable person's variable remuneration for at least four years and reduce it where the person hasn't met their accountability obligations. For SFIs, CPS 511's longer deferral periods generally go further than the FAR minimum.

Other remuneration rules

  • Conflicted remuneration ban: AFS licensees and their representatives generally can't accept benefits that could reasonably be expected to influence the financial products recommended or the advice given to retail clients (Corporations Act Part 7.7A, Division 4; ASIC RG 246). See Conflicts of interest.
  • "Two strikes" rule: shareholders of listed companies vote on the remuneration report. If 25% or more vote against it at two consecutive AGMs, shareholders vote on a "spill" resolution; if that passes with 50% or more, directors must stand for re-election at a spill meeting within 90 days.
  • Super trustee pay: CPS 511 applies to RSE licensees, and trustees must also be able to show that expenditure, including on remuneration, is in members' best financial interests.

Good practice for remuneration committees

  • Get input from the Chief Risk Officer and compliance on risk and conduct outcomes before bonuses are decided.
  • Use a consequence management framework so that similar failures lead to similar pay outcomes.
  • Test incentive designs for unintended consequences before launch, for example with scenario analysis ("what behaviour would this reward if pushed to the limit?").
  • Look at outcomes across the population, not just executives: front-line incentives often carry the most conduct risk.
  • Explain decisions clearly in remuneration disclosures.

A remuneration committee's year (illustrative)

WhenTypical business
Start of the yearApprove scorecards for senior executives, including non-financial measures such as risk and member outcomes.
Mid-yearReview risk and conduct events so far, with input from the CRO.
Before year-endConsider in-period adjustments for incidents, breaches and audit findings.
Year-endApprove variable pay outcomes, apply malus where needed, and record the reasons.
Each yearCheck compliance with CPS 511, and every three years arrange an independent review of the framework's effectiveness.

Common mistakes

  • Risk adjustments decided after pay is agreed, so they never change outcomes.
  • Scorecards dominated by financial measures, with risk measures that everyone passes.
  • No consistent record of why adjustments were or weren't made.

Questions a board can ask

These are prompts for discussion, not requirements.

  1. How did risk and conduct outcomes change this year's pay, and for whom?
  2. Would our consequence decisions look consistent if they were published?
  3. What does the CRO think of our remuneration outcomes?

Frequently asked questions

Do these rules apply to all staff?

CPS 511 covers all staff through the remuneration framework, but the strictest rules (deferral and clawback) apply to senior managers and some highly paid staff at significant financial institutions. See CPS 511.
