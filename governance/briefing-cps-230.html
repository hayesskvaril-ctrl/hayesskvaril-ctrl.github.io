Board briefing: CPS 230 Operational Risk Management

A two-page briefing for directors and executives: what it is, why it matters to the board, the key numbers, and questions to ask management.

CPS 230 Operational Risk Management

What it is

APRA's cross-industry standard for banks, insurers and super trustees on managing operational risk, staying resilient through severe disruption and controlling the risks of relying on service providers. It applies proportionately: significant financial institutions face the full set of requirements.

Why it matters to the board

  • The board is ultimately accountable for operational risk management, business continuity and the management of service providers.
  • The board approves tolerance levels for critical operations: the maximum disruption the entity will accept.
  • Serious events must be notified to APRA within tight deadlines, so the board needs early warning.

Key numbers and deadlines

WhatRequirement
Material operational risk incidentNotify APRA within 72 hours
Critical operation disrupted outside toleranceNotify APRA within 24 hours
Entering or materially changing a material service provider arrangementNotify APRA within 20 business days
Material service provider registerSubmitted to APRA each year
Business continuity planTested, including an annual exercise with severe but plausible scenarios

Questions to ask management

  1. Which of our operations are critical, and are we confident they are complete?
  2. What tolerance levels have we approved, and could we actually stay within them in a severe but plausible disruption?
  3. What did our last business continuity exercise show, and what has been fixed?
  4. Who are our material service providers, and do we have credible, tested exit plans for them?
  5. How quickly would we know, and notify APRA, if a critical operation went outside tolerance?
  6. Which control weaknesses are in our operational risk profile, and how long have they been open?

Red flags

  • Tolerance levels that have never been tested against a realistic scenario.
  • A material service provider with no exit plan, or one that has never been tested.
  • Incidents that reach the board weeks after they happen.
