What the levels mean

Beginner 23 pages

What is it, and why does it matter?

No prior knowledge assumed. Explains the purpose, the big picture and the key vocabulary.

Written for: Newcomers, students, people moving into a risk, compliance or governance role, and directors new to financial services.

Intermediate 76 pages

What does it require, and how do organisations do it?

Assumes the Foundations. Covers specific obligations, processes, timeframes and practical tools.

Written for: Working practitioners who need to apply requirements day to day.

Advanced 18 pages

How do you exercise judgement when it's hard?

Assumes working knowledge. Legal tests and case law, quantitative methods, design trade-offs, grey areas, research evidence, interactions between regimes, and board and regulator perspectives.

Written for: Senior practitioners, specialists, board members and postgraduate students.

A page is tagged by its entry level: the level you need to start reading it. Some pages include sections that go deeper, marked as such. Jump to Beginner, Intermediate or Advanced.

Beginner (23 pages)

Foundations

  • Core frameworks compared: ISO 31000, COSO and Three Lines
    Four frameworks come up again and again in risk and governance. They are not competitors: each answers a different question, and most organisations draw on several.
  • Regulatory landscape map: who regulates what
    Australia spreads regulation across several agencies. This page maps who does what, so you know which regulator cares about which part of your work.
  • The Three Lines model (and internal audit's role)
    A simple way to describe who does what in risk management: the people who run the business, the specialists who support and challenge them, and the independent auditors who check it all.
  • What is compliance?
    Compliance means meeting your obligations, and being able to show that you have.
  • What is governance?
    Governance is how an organisation is directed, controlled and held to account: who decides, how decisions are checked, and who answers for the results.
  • What is risk management?
    Risk management is the discipline of thinking ahead about what could affect your goals, and deciding in advance what to do about it.

Risk management

  • Credit, market and liquidity risk (the basics)
    The three classic financial risks: someone doesn't pay you back, prices move against you, or you can't get cash when you need it.

Compliance

  • AML/CTF fundamentals
    Criminals need legitimate businesses to hide and move money. Anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing (AML/CTF) law makes those businesses the first line of defence.
  • Licensing basics (AFSL, RSE licence and more)
    In Australia you need a licence to provide most financial services. The licence is the regulator's gate, and the conditions attached to it are the rules you live by afterwards.
  • Privacy law
    Organisations hold huge amounts of information about people. Privacy law sets the rules for collecting, using, sharing and protecting it, and those rules are getting tougher.

Governance

  • Board structure and accountability
    The board sits at the top of every company and super trustee. This page covers who is on it, what the law expects of them, and how accountability flows through the organisation.
  • Conflicts of interest
    A conflict of interest arises when someone who is meant to act for others has an interest, or another duty, that could pull them in a different direction.
  • Whistleblower protections
    Whistleblowers often see problems long before auditors or regulators do. The law protects them so that misconduct comes to light, and organisations must make it safe to speak up.

Standards library

  • Key ASIC regulatory guides
    ASIC's regulatory guides explain how ASIC reads the law and what it expects. This page maps the ones financial services compliance teams use most, with detailed explainers for four of them.
  • ASIC RG 104 General obligations and RG 105 Organisational competence
    ASIC's guidance on the core, ongoing obligations every AFS licensee must meet, and on having competent responsible managers.
  • ASD Essential Eight
    The Australian Signals Directorate's baseline set of cyber security mitigation strategies, and the maturity model used to measure them.
  • ISO 31000 Risk management
    The world's most widely referenced risk management guideline, with Australian roots. It describes what good risk management looks like for any organisation and any type of risk.

Sectors

  • Not-for-profits and charities
    How charities are governed and regulated, and how smaller organisations can manage risk in proportion to their size.

Case studies

  • The Hayne Royal Commission (2019)
    The inquiry that exposed widespread misconduct in Australian financial services and reshaped regulation for the following decade.
  • The HIH Insurance collapse (2001)
    Australia's largest corporate collapse at the time, and how it reshaped insurance regulation, corporate governance and APRA itself.
  • The Optus and Medibank data breaches (2022)
    Two of Australia's largest data breaches, weeks apart, that changed how boards, regulators and the public think about cyber and privacy risk.

Tools and templates

  • Resource library
    Every free template, interactive tool, self-check and learning resource on RiskLens Australia, in one list.
  • Risk assessment template
    A ready-to-use risk register spreadsheet. Describe each risk, rate it before and after controls, check it against your appetite, and plan treatment. The heat maps update automatically.

Intermediate (76 pages)

Risk management

  • Assurance mapping and combined assurance
    A way to see who checks what across the organisation, so that the biggest risks get enough independent assurance and effort isn't wasted on duplication.
  • Business continuity
    Disruptions will happen. Business continuity is about making sure the services people depend on keep running, or come back fast enough, when they do.
  • Change, project and reputational risk
    Why so many problems happen during change, how to keep projects honest, and how to manage the reputational fallout of other risks.
  • Climate risk management
    How climate change creates financial risks for banks, insurers and super funds, and how those risks are identified, measured and managed within the normal risk framework.
  • Control design and testing
    A control is only worth having if it targets the right risk, is performed consistently, and can be shown to work. This page covers how to design controls well and how to test them.
  • Cyber risk management
    How organisations understand and manage the risk of cyber attacks and technology failures, from baseline controls to incident response and regulatory obligations.
  • Enterprise risk management (ERM)
    ERM means managing all of an organisation's risks together, as one portfolio, linked to its strategy, rather than in separate silos.
  • Fraud risk management
    How organisations prevent, detect and respond to fraud by staff, customers, criminals and suppliers, and how fraud connects to scams.
  • Incident and breach management
    When something goes wrong, the way an organisation responds matters as much as the event itself: containing harm, assessing it properly, telling regulators on time, fixing the cause and putting people right.
  • Issue and action management
    How organisations record, rate, fix and verify control weaknesses, so problems found by audits, testing and incidents actually get fixed.
  • Model risk management
    How organisations manage the risk that the models they rely on for decisions, from credit scores to actuarial valuations and AI, are wrong or misused.
  • Operational risk
    Operational risk is the risk that things go wrong in how an organisation runs: its people, processes, systems, suppliers, or events outside its control.
  • Risk appetite and tolerance
    Risk appetite is the board's answer to the question "how much risk are we prepared to take, and of what kinds?" Tolerances and limits turn that answer into numbers people can manage to.
  • Risk assessment methodologies
    How organisations find their risks, size them up and decide which matter most, from simple heat maps to statistical models.
  • Third-party, vendor and outsourcing risk
    You can outsource the work, but not the responsibility. This page covers how organisations manage the risks of relying on other companies to deliver their services.

Compliance

  • Anti-bribery and corruption
    The laws against bribery in Australia and overseas, the 2024 "failure to prevent" offence for companies, and what an anti-bribery program looks like in practice.
  • Breach and incident reporting obligations
    A regime-by-regime guide to who must be told what, and how fast, when something goes wrong in an Australian financial services business.
  • Climate-related financial disclosures and sustainability reporting
    Large Australian companies, super funds and managed funds must now report on climate-related risks, opportunities and emissions in a "sustainability report" in their annual report.
  • Consumer protection
    Since the 2019 Royal Commission, Australia's consumer protection laws for financial services have shifted from "tell the customer" towards "design, sell and treat the customer fairly".
  • Designing a compliance program
    A compliance program is the set of people, processes and systems an organisation uses to know its obligations, meet them, and prove it. This page walks through how to build one.
  • Disclosure obligations
    Financial products are complex and the stakes are high, so the law requires businesses to tell customers key facts at key moments, and never to mislead them.
  • Financial advice regulation
    The rules that apply when someone gives financial product advice to retail clients: who can advise, what the advice must be, how it's documented and how advisers can be paid.
  • Modern slavery reporting
    Large organisations must report each year on the risk of modern slavery in their operations and supply chains, and stronger obligations are on the way.
  • Sanctions compliance
    How Australian sanctions laws work, why a breach can be a crime even without intent, and how organisations screen customers and payments to stay compliant.

Governance

  • AI governance
    How organisations oversee artificial intelligence so they get the benefits without harming customers, breaking the law or losing control of their own decisions.
  • Culture and conduct risk
    Frameworks and policies set the rules, but culture decides whether people follow them. Conduct risk is what happens to customers and markets when culture goes wrong.
  • Financial Accountability Regime (FAR)
    The FAR makes it clear, in writing and on a regulator's register, which senior person is accountable for each part of a bank, insurer or super fund, and what happens if they fall short.
  • Internal audit
    The independent function that tells the board whether risk management, controls and governance are really working.
  • Remuneration governance
    People do what they're rewarded for. Remuneration governance is about making sure pay rewards good outcomes and sound risk management, not just short-term results.

Standards library

  • APS 110 Capital Adequacy and APS 210 Liquidity: the basics
    The two foundations of bank soundness: enough capital to absorb losses, and enough liquidity to meet obligations when money flows out.
  • ASIC RG 181 AFS licensing: Managing conflicts of interest
    ASIC's guidance on how financial services licensees must identify and manage conflicts of interest. Reissued in December 2025 with higher expectations.
  • ASIC RG 259 Risk management systems of fund operators
    ASIC's expectations for how responsible entities of managed funds (and corporate directors of CCIVs) identify, assess and manage risk, both for their own business and for each fund they run.
  • ASIC RG 270 Whistleblower policies
    ASIC's guidance on the whistleblower policy that public companies, large proprietary companies and super trustees must have.
  • ASIC RG 271 Internal dispute resolution
    ASIC's standards for how financial firms handle complaints: what counts as a complaint, how fast firms must respond, and how complaints data should be used.
  • ASIC RG 274 Product design and distribution obligations
    ASIC's guidance on the design and distribution obligations (DDO): issuers must design products for a defined target market, and everyone in the chain must take reasonable steps so products reach the right people.
  • ASIC RG 277 Consumer remediation
    ASIC's guidance on how licensees should put things right when their conduct harms consumers: promptly, fairly, and without making people ask.
  • ASIC RG 78 Breach reporting by AFS and credit licensees
    ASIC's guidance on the reportable situations regime: what licensees must report to ASIC, how to decide, and how fast.
  • ASIC RG 97 Disclosing fees and costs in PDSs and periodic statements
    ASIC's guidance on how super funds and managed funds must show their fees and costs, so people can see what a product really costs and compare it with others.
  • COSO ERM and Internal Control frameworks
    COSO publishes two widely used frameworks. One covers enterprise risk management and how risk connects to strategy. The other covers internal control and how an organisation makes sure its controls work.
  • CPS 190 Recovery and Exit Planning and CPS 900 Resolution Planning
    Two APRA standards that make financial institutions plan ahead for a severe crisis: how they would recover, how they would leave the market in an orderly way, and how APRA could step in if needed.
  • CPS 220 Risk Management
    APRA's foundational risk standard: every bank and insurer (and, through SPS 220, every super trustee) must have a board-approved framework for managing all its material risks.
  • CPS 230 Operational Risk Management
    APRA's cross-industry standard requiring banks, insurers and super trustees to manage operational risk, stay resilient through severe disruption, and control the risks of relying on service providers.
  • CPS 234 Information Security
    APRA's standard requiring banks, insurers and super trustees to protect their information assets from cyber and other security threats, test that protection, and tell APRA quickly when things go wrong.
  • CPS 510 Governance and CPS 520 Fit and Proper (and SPS equivalents)
    APRA's core rules on how boards are structured and run, and on making sure the people in key roles have the competence, character and integrity to hold them.
  • CPS 511 Remuneration
    APRA's standard for how banks, insurers and super trustees design and govern pay, so that incentives support sound risk management and good outcomes.
  • IIA Global Internal Audit Standards
    The global professional standards for internal auditing, which took effect in January 2025.
  • ISO 22301 Business continuity management systems
    The international standard for setting up and running a business continuity management system that can be certified.
  • ISO/IEC 27001 Information security management
    The international standard for an information security management system (ISMS): a structured, risk-based way to protect information, which organisations can be independently certified against.
  • ISO 37001 Anti-bribery management systems
    The international standard for a management system to prevent, detect and respond to bribery, updated in 2025.
  • ISO 37301 Compliance management systems
    The international standard for building a compliance management system, with Australian roots.
  • ISO/IEC 42001 Artificial intelligence management systems
    The first certifiable international standard for managing the development and use of artificial intelligence responsibly.
  • SPS 515 Strategic Planning and Member Outcomes
    APRA's standard requiring super trustees to plan strategically around member outcomes, spend members' money wisely, and check every year whether members are getting good results.
  • SPS 530 Investment Governance
    APRA's standard for how super trustees govern the investment of members' money: setting objectives and strategies, choosing and monitoring investments, valuing them, and managing liquidity and stress.

Sectors

  • Banking and ADIs
    How banks, building societies and credit unions (together, authorised deposit-taking institutions, or ADIs) are regulated to keep deposits safe and treat customers fairly.
  • Credit licensees and non-bank lenders
    The rules for lenders and brokers that provide consumer credit, from banks to non-bank lenders, brokers and buy now pay later providers.
  • Financial advice licensees
    What it takes to run an advice business compliantly: licensee obligations, oversight of advisers, and the risks regulators focus on.
  • Insurance
    How insurers are regulated so they can pay claims when disasters happen, and so customers are treated fairly from sale to claim.
  • Listed companies
    The governance and disclosure rules that apply to ASX-listed companies, many of which also shape practice in financial services.
  • Managed investment schemes
    Managed funds pool investors' money and have it managed by someone else. For retail investors, the law places a single responsible entity in charge and surrounds it with compliance checks.
  • Payments and fintech
    A fast-changing sector: new payments licensing, digital asset rules, open data and scams obligations are reshaping how payments and fintech firms are regulated.
  • Public sector (Commonwealth)
    How risk, fraud and governance are managed in Commonwealth entities, under a framework built around the PGPA Act.
  • Superannuation trustee governance
    The obligations that shape how an APRA-regulated super fund is run: who regulates it, the duties trustees owe members, and the main standards, tests and reporting that sit around them.

Case studies

  • APRA Prudential Inquiry into CBA (2018)
    How APRA's inquiry into Australia's largest bank found that success had dulled its sensitivity to risk, and why its findings became a benchmark for the whole industry.
  • AUSTRAC v CBA (2018) and AUSTRAC v Westpac (2020)
    Two record penalties for anti-money laundering failures, and why they started with new products and automated systems nobody checked.
  • The Shield and First Guardian collapses
    How thousands of Australians' super ended up in two collapsed schemes, and the lessons for advisers, platform trustees and everyone in between.

Advanced (18 pages)

Risk management

  • Control testing: sampling and assurance confidence
    How many items to test, what a clean (or not-so-clean) sample really tells you, and how strong the evidence needs to be.
  • Human factors and cognitive bias in risk decisions
    The predictable ways human judgement goes wrong in risk assessment, escalation and decisions, and practical ways to design processes that counter them, with a calibration test.
  • KRI design and statistical thresholds
    How to choose indicators that actually predict risk, set amber and red thresholds that mean something, and test whether a KRI works, with an interactive control chart.
  • Mapping critical operations end to end
    A practical method for tracing a critical operation from start to finish, recording what it depends on, and finding the single points of failure, with a worked super fund example.
  • Quantitative operational risk
    How risk teams put numbers on operational losses: how often losses happen, how big they get, and how bad a really bad year could be.
  • Risk aggregation and correlation
    How hundreds of individual risks become one enterprise risk profile, and why the way risks move together matters as much as their size.
  • Root cause analysis: techniques and pitfalls
    How to find causes the organisation can actually fix, comparing 5 whys, fishbone, barrier analysis, bow-tie and fault trees, with a worked example and a fault tree calculator.
  • Scenario analysis and stress testing
    How organisations test what would happen in a severe but plausible crisis, and work backwards from failure to find the scenarios that would break them.
  • Service provider concentration, fourth parties and exit planning
    How dependence on service providers builds up, why fourth parties and system concentration matter, and how to plan an exit that would actually work, with a substitutability scorer.
  • Setting CPS 230 tolerance levels
    A practical method for deciding how long a critical operation can be disrupted, how much data can be lost, and what service must continue, with worked examples.
  • Liquidity stress testing for super funds
    Why liquidity risk in super is distinctive, how the denominator effect and stale valuations catch funds out, and how to design stress tests that matter, with a simulator.

Compliance

    How APRA sets the capital banks must hold for operational risk: the business indicator, the tiered formula, a calculator and the debates behind the design.
