Risk heat map tool

Practise risk assessment: rate each risk before and after controls, see where it lands on a heat map, and check it against a risk appetite.

Interactive tool Last reviewed: 27 September 2026

How it works

  • Inherent risk is the rating before taking controls into account. Residual risk is the rating with current controls working as they do today.
  • Score = likelihood (1–5) × consequence (1–5). Scores of 1–4 are Low, 5–9 Medium, 10–14 High and 15–25 Extreme.
  • Choose an appetite level to see which residual risks would need treatment or escalation.
  • Your risks are saved only in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Example rating scales

Every organisation defines its own scales, approved as part of its risk management framework. These are simplified examples for practice only.

RatingLikelihood (example)Consequence (example)
1Rare: may occur only in exceptional circumstances (e.g. less than once in 20 years)Insignificant: negligible impact on members, finances or reputation
2Unlikely: could occur at some time (e.g. once in 5–20 years)Minor: small, contained impact, fixed in normal operations
3Possible: might occur (e.g. once in 1–5 years)Moderate: noticeable member or financial impact, possible reportable breach
4Likely: will probably occur (e.g. about once a year)Major: significant member harm, regulatory action or disruption outside tolerance
5Almost certain: expected to occur (e.g. several times a year)Severe: threatens critical operations, solvency or licence; widespread harm

Use heat maps with care

Heat maps are great for communicating risk, but the ratings are structured judgement, not measurement. Two risks in the same cell can be very different, and a risk with a severe consequence but rare likelihood can look deceptively "low". Use them alongside other information such as scenarios, key risk indicators and control testing results. See Risk assessment methodologies.
