How we check content

How every page is researched, checked against official sources, expert reviewed and kept current, and how to tell us when something is wrong.

About 4 minutes Last reviewed: 1 October 2026

Key takeaways

  • Every content page lists its sources, a Last reviewed date and a review label: Checked against sources, or Expert reviewed.
  • Specific requirements, thresholds and dates are checked against the official source. Uncertain or proposed changes are labelled as such.
  • Every page is re-checked at least once a year, and sooner when the rules change.
  • Anyone can suggest a correction from the link at the bottom of each page.

What we publish

RiskLens Australia is a free educational library. It explains what the rules are, why they exist and how organisations put them into practice. It is not advice: it doesn't take anyone's circumstances into account, and readers should always go to the official source and get professional advice before acting.

How a page is made

Every content page lists its sources, shows the date it was last reviewed and when its next review is due, and carries a review status near the title.

What the review labels mean

LabelWhat it means
Checked against sourcesThe page has been researched and drafted, and its specific requirements, thresholds and dates checked against the official sources listed at the bottom of the page. It has not yet been reviewed by the site's creator against practice.
Expert reviewedThe site's creator, a risk and compliance professional, has reviewed the page and signed it off. The badge shows the month of that review. If the page later changes in substance, it is reviewed again.

Expert review is under way, starting with the creator's specialist areas: incident and breach management, remediation, disclosure, CPS 230 and operational risk, control design and testing, fee governance and third-party risk.

Which sources we trust most

  1. The law itself: Acts and regulations on the Federal Register of Legislation.
  2. Regulator instruments and guidance: APRA prudential standards and guides, ASIC regulatory guides, AUSTRAC rules and guidance, and OAIC guidance.
  3. Regulator statements and reports: speeches, reviews, enforcement outcomes and media releases, read for what they say about expectations.
  4. Peer-reviewed research for the theory and evidence on Advanced pages, listed in the Research library.
  5. Industry and professional material only to explain common practice, and labelled as such.

When sources disagree, or a point is uncertain or still a proposal, the page says so rather than presenting it as settled. We never invent citations, cases, statistics or research: if something can't be verified, it is left out.

Our own words

Standards such as ISO 31000 and the COSO frameworks are copyrighted, so we explain them in our own words and don't reproduce their text or figures. Legislation and regulator guidance are summarised in plain English with links to the official versions.

Use of AI

AI tools help with research, drafting, diagrams, code and checks. A person decides what is published. Every specific requirement is checked against its source, and pages are progressively expert reviewed. The review label on each page tells you how far along a page is.

Corrections

Every page has a Suggest a correction link at the bottom. It opens a short public form on the site's GitHub page (a free GitHub account is needed) with the page address filled in. Tell us what is wrong and, if you can, link the official source. Please don't include personal or confidential information.

Corrections are made as quickly as possible. Notable changes are listed on What's new, and the page's Last reviewed date is updated.

Keeping pages current

  • Every page is re-checked at least once every 12 months; its "Next review due" date is shown at the bottom.
  • Pages are updated sooner when the law, a standard or guidance changes. Upcoming changes are tracked on the regulatory tracker.
  • Automated checks look for broken links, dates that have passed and pages overdue for review.

Independence

The site is a personal, non-commercial project. It has no advertising, sponsorship, tracking or paid content, and it isn't affiliated with any regulator, employer, industry body or training provider. Its content doesn't represent the views of any employer.
