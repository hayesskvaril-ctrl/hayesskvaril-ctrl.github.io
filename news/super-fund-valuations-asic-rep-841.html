Commentary 22 October 2026

Super fund valuations: ASIC says trustees and auditors must dig deeper

ASIC's second review of super fund financial reporting and audit found continuing weaknesses in how unlisted investments are valued and checked, and a reminder that trustees can't hand that responsibility to fund managers.

About 5 minutes Last reviewed: 22 October 2026

In brief

  • On 14 October 2026 ASIC released Report 841, its second review of super fund financial reporting and audit, covering 42 fund financial reports and six audits.
  • It found persistent concerns about how unlisted investments are valued, by trustees relying on external fund managers and by auditors who didn't test those values hard enough.
  • ASIC says trustees cannot outsource responsibility for accurate valuations, and plans more reviews in 2026–27, now including managed investment schemes.

What ASIC found

ASIC's Report 841 follows Report 816 from 2025, and covers the funds ASIC didn't review the first time. Its main findings:

Main findings of ASIC Report 841
AreaWhat ASIC found
Trustees: externally managed assetsTrustees often relied on external fund managers for the fair value of unlisted investments, which may limit their ability to review and challenge significant valuation assumptions and estimates.
Trustees: member balancesSome trustees did not adjust member liabilities in a timely way after significant investment write-downs.
Trustees: disclosureFair value hierarchy disclosures still gave only high-level descriptions and references to external pricing sources, with little on valuation techniques, inputs and judgements.
Auditors: externally managed assetsSome auditors did not adequately test the reliability of prices from external fund managers, or the methods, assumptions and data behind them.
Auditors: internally managed assetsSome auditors did not obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence for valuations.

ASIC Commissioner Kate O'Rourke said trustees "cannot outsource responsibility for ensuring investment valuations are accurate and reliable, even where external fund managers are involved".

What happens next

In 2026–27 ASIC plans to review the financial reports of 20 super funds and 20 managed investment schemes, carry out four fund audit surveillances and four scheme audit surveillances, and consider regulatory action, including audit deficiency reports and enforcement where it sees a significant breach of the Corporations Act.

Our analysis: why valuations are a member-fairness issue

Valuations are not just an accounting question. In a unitised fund, the value of unlisted assets flows straight into unit prices, so a stale or overstated value moves money between members: those who leave are paid too much, and those who stay or join bear the cost. ASIC's finding that some trustees were slow to adjust member liabilities after write-downs is exactly that risk.

The findings sit alongside APRA's requirements. SPS 530 already requires a board-approved valuation governance framework, including triggers for revaluing assets between scheduled valuations, and APRA released proposals to strengthen the standard on 30 September 2026. On the audit side, the RSE auditor's work under SPS 310 and the financial statement audit both depend on testing valuations rather than accepting a manager's number.

Questions trustees and their auditors may be asking:

  • For each externally managed unlisted investment, what information do we receive about the valuation method and key assumptions, and could we challenge it?
  • How quickly do write-downs reach unit prices and member balances, and who decides?
  • Would our fair value disclosures help a member understand how uncertain the values are?
  • How did our auditor test the reliability of external managers' prices this year?

General information for learning, not advice about any fund's obligations.
