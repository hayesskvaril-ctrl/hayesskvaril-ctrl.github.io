Regulatory roundup: winter 2026

The main Australian risk, compliance and governance developments from June to September 2026, and what's coming up next.

About 8 minutes Last reviewed: 27 September 2026

In brief

  • 1 July 2026 was a big start date: Payday Super, AML/CTF "tranche 2" and APRA's targeted CPS 230 amendments all began.
  • APRA and ASIC proposed cutting FAR paperwork, while APRA's draft CPS 510 would consolidate and tighten governance rules from early 2028.
  • ASIC issued a sharp warning to super platform trustees about failures to protect members' savings.
  • Scams Prevention Framework rules started on 1 September 2026, with most obligations expected from March 2027.

Governance and accountability

FAR: less paperwork proposed (June 2026)

APRA and ASIC announced changes to reduce the Financial Accountability Regime's administrative burden, as part of the Government's Better Regulation reforms from the 2026–27 Budget. Proposals include:

  • providing accountability statements and maps only on request
  • more time to register accountable persons
  • removing key functions requirements from the regulator rules
  • a higher materiality threshold for notifying changes
  • no longer listing accountable persons' direct reports in accountability maps.

ASIC also said it would reduce evidence requirements for responsible managers of FAR entities from October 2026. Some changes need legislation, so they aren't in force yet. See Financial Accountability Regime.

Draft CPS 510: governance consultation closed (28 August 2026)

APRA's draft cross-industry CPS 510 Governance would replace five standards (CPS/SPS 510, CPS/SPS 520 and SPS 521) with one. Proposals include a 12-year default tenure limit for non-executive directors, and a minimum of five directors extending to super trustees. The final standard is expected in late 2026, applying from early 2028. See our commentary: APRA's governance reset.

ASX Corporate Governance Principles, 5th edition

ASX released a consultation draft of the 5th edition on 21 July 2026, and submissions closed on 14 September 2026. Listed entities are expected to start reporting against the new edition for financial years ending on 30 June 2028 or 31 December 2028. See What is governance?

Superannuation

ASIC puts platform trustees on notice (29 June 2026)

ASIC's Report 833 reviewed six platform trustees holding over $300 billion in retirement savings. It found persistent gaps in monitoring harmful advice fee deductions, unusual fees and investment patterns, and high-risk switching. ASIC is also pursuing Equity Trustees and Diversa over alleged failures linked to the Shield and First Guardian collapses. See our commentary: Platform trustees in the spotlight.

Payday Super started (1 July 2026)

Employers must now pay super guarantee contributions with wages, and contributions must generally reach the fund within seven business days of payday, rather than quarterly. For trustees, this means more frequent, smaller contributions to process and allocate. See Superannuation trustee governance.

Performance test review

Treasury consulted from May to June 2026 on options to strengthen the annual super performance test, including how to treat assets without a suitable market benchmark. Outcomes are pending.

Operational risk

APRA's targeted amendments to CPS 230, finalised on 30 April 2026, took effect on 1 July 2026. They include limited exemptions from some contract requirements for certain "non-traditional" service providers. It is now just over a year since CPS 230 itself started.

Financial crime and scams

  • AML/CTF "tranche 2" began on 1 July 2026, bringing lawyers, accountants, real estate professionals and others into the regime. Changed obligations for existing reporting entities began on 31 March 2026. See AML/CTF fundamentals.
  • Scams Prevention Framework: AFCA became the external dispute resolution scheme for scam complaints from 1 July 2026, and the framework rules started on 1 September 2026. Most obligations for banks, telcos and digital platforms are expected to apply from March 2027. See Consumer protection.

Insurance

The Insurance Council of Australia extended the timetable for lodging its redrafted General Insurance Code of Practice with ASIC to late October 2026, after a large volume of consultation feedback. The redraft proposes making the Code enforceable as part of consumer contracts, with a transition of about two years after approval. See Insurance.
