Roundup 22 October 2026

Regulatory roundup: mid-October 2026

The main Australian risk, compliance and governance developments from 1 to 22 October 2026, and what's coming up next.

About 7 minutes Last reviewed: 22 October 2026

In brief

  • ASIC published reviews of super fund valuations (Report 841) and mortgage brokers' best interests duty practices (Report 840).
  • Enforcement outcomes on cyber security (Fortnum, $1.35 million) and insurance claims (Hollard, $2 million, the first utmost good faith penalty).
  • ASIC used and extended its product powers: a short-lived stop order over a private credit fund's target market, and a proposal to keep the CFD protections for another 10 years.
  • APRA and New Zealand's central bank found both banking systems resilient in their first joint stress test since 2017, and the government responded to the parliamentary inquiry into financial abuse.

The main Australian risk, compliance and governance developments from 1 to 22 October 2026. A full October roundup will follow in early November.

Superannuation

Super fund valuations: ASIC Report 841 (14 October)

ASIC's second review of super fund financial reporting and audit found continuing concerns about how unlisted investments are valued, by trustees relying on external fund managers and by auditors who did not test those values enough. See our article on Report 841 and SPS 530.

Cyber security and operational risk

Fortnum Private Wealth penalised $1.35 million (mid-October)

The third outcome ASIC has obtained against a licensee over cyber security failures, this time centred on oversight of a network of advice practices. See our article.

Insurance

Hollard: first utmost good faith penalty (early October) and new life claims data (16 October)

The Federal Court ordered Hollard Insurance Partners to pay $2 million over a home insurance claim that took more than three years to finalise. APRA published its latest life insurance claims and disputes statistics, for the 12 months to 30 June 2026. See our article.

Products and investors

Private credit: a stop order made and lifted

In mid-October ASIC made an interim stop order preventing Fidante Partners from issuing Class A units in the Ares Global Credit Income Fund to retail clients, because it considered the fund's target market determination inappropriate. The determination suggested the fund suited investors seeking capital preservation, could make up as much as half of an investor's assets, and gave an inappropriate timeframe for getting money out. ASIC revoked the order on 21 October 2026 after Fidante amended the determination, including removing capital preservation, reducing the suggested allocation and rating the risk and return profile as high. The action came from ASIC's surveillance of private credit funds. See RG 274 Design and distribution obligations.

CFD protections: proposed 10-year extension (12 October)

ASIC proposed extending its product intervention order for contracts for difference (CFDs), which is due to expire on 23 May 2027, for a further 10 years, subject to Ministerial approval. The changes would also cover CFDs settled through digital assets and clarify margin rules. ASIC said retail clients' losses on CFDs exceeded $458 million over a recent 12-month period, including $73 million in fees. Feedback is due by 9 November 2026 (CS 64).

Credit

Mortgage brokers' best interests duty: ASIC Report 840 (mid-October)

ASIC reviewed 13 credit licensees in eight large mortgage broker groups, covering about two-thirds of broker representatives. Seven of the eight groups reported that brokers did not recommend the cheapest loan option in more than 20% of cases. That can be justified, but ASIC found cases without clear evidence of why a more expensive loan was in the customer's best interests. Complaints about the best interests duty also took longer to resolve, and some were missed, withdrawn without full investigation or delayed. ASIC pointed licensees to RG 273 and RG 271.

Digital assets

ASIC consults on standards for the new regime (mid-October)

ASIC's Consultation Paper 392 sets out proposed guidance and standards for the new laws for digital asset platforms and tokenised custody platforms, which commence on 8 April 2027. Operators will need an Australian financial services licence (or a variation), disclosure documents and platform rules, and to meet asset-holding, trading and settlement and financial requirements. Consultation closes on 27 November 2026. See payments and fintech.

Banking

Trans-Tasman stress test (15 October)

APRA and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand completed their first joint stress test since 2017. The scenario combined a geopolitical escalation, an oil price shock and a deep recession in both countries, plus a funding shock, market closure and a cyber attack on a bank-owned offshore operation. The results indicate both banking systems would remain resilient, with banks continuing to lend through the stress. APRA will publish detailed findings later in 2026. See scenario analysis and stress testing.

Leverage ratio rated compliant (15 October)

The Basel Committee assessed Australia's leverage ratio rules as compliant with its standards, the highest grade. In Australia the leverage ratio applies to the six banks approved to use the internal ratings-based approach, mainly through APS 110.

Financial abuse

Government responds to the parliamentary inquiry (14 October)

The government supported, or supported in principle, 58 of the 61 recommendations of the Parliamentary Joint Committee on Corporations and Financial Services inquiry into financial abuse. It released a consultation on relief where abusers use the tax and social security systems to create debts, and on letting courts transfer such debts to the responsible spouse in family law proceedings. It also restated work already under way, including draft legislation later this year to stop perpetrators receiving victims' super death benefits and to address coerced directorships, and powers for the ATO to block rollovers into newly established self-managed super funds where it has concerns about fraud or abuse.
