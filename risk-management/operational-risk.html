Operational risk

Operational risk is the risk that things go wrong in how an organisation runs: its people, processes, systems, suppliers, or events outside its control.

Intermediate About 21 minutes Last reviewed: 7 October 2026 Checked against sources

Key takeaways

  • Operational risk comes from four drivers: people, processes, systems and external events. Every organisation has it; it can't be avoided by choosing a different business.
  • It is very broad. APRA's CPS 230 says it includes legal, regulatory, compliance, conduct, technology, data and change management risk.
  • The core toolkit: risk and control self-assessment, key risk indicators, incident and loss data, scenario analysis, control testing and issue management.
  • CPS 230 has applied to APRA-regulated banks, insurers and super trustees since 1 July 2025. It has three pillars: operational risk management, business continuity and service provider management.
  • Key CPS 230 notification timeframes include 72 hours for material operational risk incidents and 24 hours for disruptions to critical operations outside tolerance.

What is operational risk?

A widely used definition, from the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, describes operational risk as the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems, or from external events. APRA's CPS 230 frames it similarly: entities must manage operational risks that may result from inadequate or failed internal processes or systems, the actions or inactions of people, or external drivers and events.

In plain English, operational risk is everything that can go wrong in the running of the organisation, as distinct from the risks it takes on deliberately in its business (such as lending money or investing members' savings).

Types of operational loss event

The Basel Committee's framework for banks groups operational loss events into seven top-level types. Although designed for banks, the categories are widely used elsewhere, including for incident classification.

Event typeExamples
Internal fraudAn employee diverts member benefits to their own account; unauthorised trading.
External fraudIdentity theft used to make a fraudulent early release claim; hacking; cheque fraud.
Employment practices and workplace safetyDiscrimination claims; workplace injury; underpayment of staff.
Clients, products and business practicesCharging fees for no service; mis-selling; breaching disclosure obligations; privacy breaches.
Damage to physical assetsFlood or fire at an office or data centre.
Business disruption and system failuresCore system outage; telecommunications failure; failed software upgrade.
Execution, delivery and process managementData entry errors; missed deadlines; unit pricing errors; failures by outsourced providers.

Many of the most costly problems in Australian financial services in recent years, such as the "fees for no service" failures examined by the Financial Services Royal Commission, fall into the "clients, products and business practices" category and led to large customer remediation programs.

The operational risk toolkit

ToolWhat it doesLooks
Risk and control self-assessment (RCSA)The business assesses its own risks and how well its controls work, with second-line challenge.Forward
Key risk indicators (KRIs)Metrics that warn when a risk is rising, with triggers and limits.Now and forward
Incident and loss dataRecords of what actually went wrong, including near misses, with causes and costs.Backward
Scenario analysisStructured "what if" exercises on severe but plausible events, such as a major cyber attack or loss of a key supplier.Forward
Control testingChecks whether controls are designed and operating effectively.Now
Issues and actions managementTracks weaknesses from all sources (audit, testing, incidents) through to fixing them.Now
Change risk assessmentAssesses the risks of new products, systems, projects and outsourcing before they go ahead.Forward

The tools work best when joined up. For example, an incident should prompt a review of the related RCSA rating and controls, and a KRI breach should trigger a look at recent incidents.

APRA's CPS 230 Operational Risk Management

CPS 230 is APRA's cross-industry standard for operational risk. It commenced on 1 July 2025 and applies to authorised deposit-taking institutions, general and life insurers, private health insurers and RSE licensees (super trustees). It replaced several older standards, including CPS 231 and SPS 231 (outsourcing) and CPS 232 and SPS 232 (business continuity).

Operational risk management

  • The board is ultimately accountable for oversight of operational risk management; senior management is responsible for implementing it.
  • The entity must identify, assess and manage its operational risks, including (among others) legal, regulatory, compliance, conduct, technology, data and change management risk.
  • It must design, implement and embed internal controls in line with its risk appetite, and regularly monitor, review and test them for design and operating effectiveness. See Control design and testing.
  • It must maintain an operational risk profile, including control gaps and weaknesses until they are remediated, and fix material weaknesses in a timely way, addressing root causes.
  • It must have processes for operational risk incidents and near misses, and notify APRA as soon as possible, and no later than 72 hours, after becoming aware of an operational risk incident it determines is likely to have a material financial impact or a material impact on its ability to maintain critical operations.

Business continuity

  • The entity must identify its critical operations. Some must be treated as critical unless the entity can justify otherwise, for example payments for banks, claims handling for insurers and investment management for super funds.
  • The board must approve tolerance levels for each critical operation.
  • It must maintain a credible business continuity plan and test it through a systematic testing program, including an annual exercise against severe but plausible scenarios.
  • It must notify APRA as soon as possible, and no later than 24 hours, after a disruption to a critical operation outside tolerance.

More detail: Business continuity.

Management of service providers

  • The entity must have a service provider management policy and identify its material service providers, keeping a register that is submitted to APRA annually.
  • Material arrangements need due diligence, a formal legally binding agreement covering specified matters, and ongoing monitoring.
  • It must notify APRA within 20 business days after entering into or materially changing an agreement for a service it relies on to undertake a critical operation, and before entering into material offshoring arrangements.
  • For contracts that existed before 1 July 2025, the requirements applied from the earlier of the next renewal date or 1 July 2026.

More detail: Third-party, vendor and outsourcing risk.

Recent changes (as at September 2026)

  • On 30 April 2026 APRA finalised targeted amendments to CPS 230, its practice guide CPG 230 and the material service provider register template, effective 1 July 2026. They give limited exemptions from some contractual requirements for material arrangements with certain "non-traditional" service providers, such as central banks and clearing and settlement facilities, where standard contract terms aren't practicable.
  • Smaller entities (those that are not "significant financial institutions") were given an extra 12 months, to 1 July 2026, for some business continuity and scenario analysis requirements.

Always check the current version of CPS 230 and CPG 230 on APRA's website.

Related standards

  • CPS 234 Information Security (in force since 1 July 2019) covers information security, including notifying APRA within 72 hours of certain material information security incidents.
  • CPS 220 / SPS 220 Risk Management sets the overall risk management framework that CPS 230 operates within.
  • APS 115 sets how banks calculate capital for operational risk, using the Basel III standardised measurement approach (applied by most ADIs from 1 January 2023).

Check your understanding

Which Basel event type?

1. A staff member keys a rollover amount as $52,000 instead of $25,000.

An unintentional processing error is an execution, delivery and process management event.

2. Criminals use stolen identity documents to take over a member's account and withdraw funds.

Fraud by a third party (not an employee) is external fraud.

3. Members were charged advice fees for years without receiving any advice.

Failing to meet obligations to clients, including charging for services not provided, falls under clients, products and business practices.

4. A faulty software release takes the member portal offline for two days.

A system outage is business disruption and system failures (the underlying cause might also involve change management failures).

Measuring operational risk: example indicators

Indicators only help if they have thresholds that trigger action. See KRI design and thresholds.

AreaExample key risk indicators
ProcessesProcessing backlogs; error and rework rates; manual workarounds in use.
PeopleStaff turnover in key teams; vacancies in critical roles; overdue mandatory training.
SystemsSystem outages and their length; unpatched critical vulnerabilities; failed changes.
Service providersService level breaches; overdue assurance reports; provider incidents.
Incidents and issuesIncidents by severity; time to report; overdue high-rated actions; repeat issues.
ConductComplaints volumes and themes; remediation backlog.

Common mistakes

  • Treating operational risk as a compliance exercise, with assessments updated once a year and filed.
  • Risk ratings that never change despite incidents, test failures and audit findings.
  • Tools that don't talk to each other: incidents, RCSAs, KRIs and issues in separate places.
  • Ignoring change: new systems, products and providers are where many incidents start.

Questions a board can ask

These are prompts for discussion, not requirements.

  1. Which operational risks are outside appetite, and what is being done?
  2. What do our incidents and near misses tell us about our controls?
  3. Which key indicators have breached their thresholds this quarter?
  4. How do we know our most important controls actually work?

Frequently asked questions

Is operational risk the same as non-financial risk?

The terms overlap. Many organisations use "non-financial risk" to cover operational, compliance, conduct and technology risks together. CPS 230 defines operational risk broadly enough to include most of them.

Do super funds hold capital for operational risk?

Super trustees must hold financial resources for operational risk under SPS 114 (the operational risk financial requirement). Banks and insurers hold capital under their own prudential standards; see APS 115 for banks.
