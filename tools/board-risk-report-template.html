Board risk report template

A structured template for a Chief Risk Officer's report to a board risk committee, with guidance on what good looks like in each section.

Intermediate Word document Last reviewed: 28 September 2026
Download the template (.docx)

Word document, about 15 KB. Opens in Word, Google Docs and LibreOffice. Free to use and adapt.

Key takeaways

  • Lead with insight and the CRO's own view, not a list of everything that happened.
  • Report the risk profile against the board's appetite, with trends, so directors can see what is changing.
  • Every amber or red metric needs an explanation, an owner and a date.
  • Put decisions the committee must make at the front.

What's in the document

Cover and decisions
Meeting, period, purpose and decisions requested.
Sections 1 to 9
CRO summary; risk profile against appetite; metrics outside appetite; key and emerging risks; incidents, breaches and remediation; operational resilience and service providers; issues, actions and risk acceptances; regulatory matters; assurance and risk culture.
Appendices and checklist
Suggested appendices and a five-question check before the report is issued.

What makes a board report useful

Weak reportUseful report
Long lists of every incident and metricA short summary of what matters most, with detail in appendices
Describes what happenedExplains why it matters and what is being done
Everything greenHonest status, including concerns and data limitations
No clear askDecisions requested stated up front

See board risk reporting for more on what boards need and the lessons from past inquiries.

A starting point, not a finished framework

Adapt the sections to your organisation's risk appetite statement, committee charter and reporting cycle. Not every section will be needed every time.
