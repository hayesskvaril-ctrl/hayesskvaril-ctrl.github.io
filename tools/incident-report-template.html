Incident report template

A structured Word template to record an incident from first report to closure, including a checklist of the notification regimes that may apply.

Intermediate Word document Last reviewed: 27 September 2026
Download the template (.docx)

Word document, 4 pages, about 16 KB. Opens in Word, Google Docs and LibreOffice. Free to use and adapt.

Key takeaways

  • Record the date and time the organisation became aware. Many reporting clocks start then, not when the investigation ends.
  • Work through every notification regime that could apply. One incident can trigger ASIC, APRA, OAIC, AUSTRAC and customer notifications, each with its own test and deadline.
  • Record the reasoning for significance decisions, especially decisions not to report.
  • Don't close an incident until notifications, remediation and root-cause fixes are done and evidenced.

What the template covers

SectionWhat you record
1. Incident detailsID, title, when it occurred, was identified and became known to the organisation, who identified it and how, business units, service providers and owner.
2. DescriptionWhat happened, a timeline, and what is still unknown.
3. Impact assessmentCustomers or members, financial, operations (including critical operations and tolerance), data and privacy, regulatory and reputation, with an overall severity.
4. ContainmentImmediate actions to stop or limit harm, with owners and dates.
5. Breach and notification assessmentA checklist of regimes (ASIC reportable situations, APRA CPS 230 and CPS 234, SIS Act s 29JA, OAIC data breaches, AUSTRAC, customers and others) with a summary of each test and deadline, plus the significance reasoning.
6. Root cause analysisRoot causes, contributing factors, failed controls (design or operating failure) and whether it could happen elsewhere.
7. RemediationCustomer or member remediation (scope, method, beneficial assumptions, cost) and actions to fix controls.
8. Lessons learnedWhat worked, what should change, and who needs to know.
9. Review and closureClosure criteria, sign-offs and version history.

Tips for a good incident report

  • Facts, not blame. Describe what happened neutrally. A blame culture discourages people from reporting incidents early.
  • Report early, update often. An initial report with known facts beats a perfect report that's late. Use the version history as facts change.
  • Link it to the register. Each report should match an entry in the incident or breach register, so trends can be analysed.
  • Think beyond the incident. Ask whether the same root cause could affect other products, systems or customers.

Check the deadlines

The notification checklist summarises the position as at September 2026. Requirements depend on your licences and change over time. Always confirm the current rules on the regulators' websites. See Breach and incident reporting obligations for the detail and sources.
