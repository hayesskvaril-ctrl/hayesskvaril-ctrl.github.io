Example risk appetite statement

A worked example of a complete risk appetite statement for a fictional super fund, showing how board-level statements turn into measurable triggers and limits.

Key takeaways

  • A risk appetite statement says how much risk, of what kinds, the board is willing to take to achieve its strategy.
  • APRA-regulated super trustees, banks and insurers must have a board-approved statement (SPS 220 / CPS 220) with a tolerance for each material risk.
  • The example pairs each qualitative statement with metrics, amber triggers and red limits.
  • The fund, figures and thresholds are all fictional and illustrative. They are not benchmarks.

What's in the document

Purpose, strategy and capacity
Why the statement exists, the fund's strategy, and what risk capacity means for a super trustee.
Overall principles
Five principles, including members first and no appetite for deliberate breaches.
Appetite by material risk
Ten risk areas (investment, liquidity, operations, compliance and conduct, operational resilience, cyber, service providers, change, people, trustee financial resilience), each with an appetite level, a statement and a metrics table.
Monitoring, escalation, roles and review
What happens at amber and red, who does what, and how the statement is reviewed.

How the pieces fit together

One risk area from the example
LayerExample: liquidity
Appetite levelLow
StatementWe must always be able to pay benefits, rollovers and switches when due, including in severe but plausible stress.
MetricLiquid assets under a severe stress scenario compared with projected 90-day outflows
Trigger (amber)120% to 150%: management action plan
Limit (red)Below 120%: escalate to the CRO and Risk Committee Chair

A starting point, not a finished framework

This is a learning example, not a model to copy. A real statement must reflect the organisation's own strategy, risk profile, data and obligations, and be approved by its board. Check the current prudential standards for what a statement must contain.
