Review a disclosure document or campaign

How to review customer-facing material before it is published: confirm what the document must contain, test the overall impression, check that every claim is supported, approve the right version and monitor it after release.

Intermediate About 10 minutes Last reviewed: 1 October 2026 Checked against sources

Key takeaways

  • The overall impression counts: fine print rarely fixes a misleading headline.
  • Every factual claim needs current evidence on file.
  • Check consistency with the PDS and target market determination.
  • Approve with version control, then check what actually went out.

Use this when

  • A PDS, FSG, periodic statement, website page, advertisement, campaign or member letter is ready for review
  • Fees, features, performance or sustainability claims are changing
  • A document is due for its periodic review

You'll need

  • The draft and its purpose and audience
  • The substantiation file: evidence for every factual claim
  • The current PDS and target market determination

Who's involved

  • The business owner who drafted it
  • Product, legal and compliance reviewers
  • Tax, actuarial or investment specialists where relevant
  • An authorised approver

The steps

0 of 7 steps done

Ticks are saved in this browser only. Use them as a working checklist; they are not a record.

  1. 1 Understand the brief

    • Confirm the purpose, the audience and the channel.
    • Identify which document type it is and any content it must include.

    Output: A clear brief and list of required content. Read more: Disclosure obligations

  2. 2 Check the substantiation file

    • Link every factual claim (numbers, rankings, "no fees", sustainability) to current evidence.
    • Claims about returns, fees, "best" or "lowest", or sustainability must be accurate and substantiated.

    Output: A complete substantiation file.

  3. 3 Test the overall impression

    • Read it as the intended audience would. The overall impression counts, and fine print rarely fixes a misleading headline.
    • Silence can mislead where there is a reasonable expectation that something would be disclosed.
    • Check benefits are balanced with prominent risks, fees and key limitations.

    Output: A view on the dominant message. Read more: Misleading or deceptive conduct: the legal tests

  4. 4 Check consistency

    • Make sure it is consistent with the PDS, target market determination and other current documents.
    • Check sustainability or ESG claims match how the product is actually managed.

    Output: Consistency confirmed.

  5. 5 Specialist review

    • Product, legal and compliance check content requirements, accuracy, balance and overall impression.
    • Bring in tax, actuarial or investment teams where relevant.

    Output: Review comments resolved.

  6. 6 Approve with version control

    • Sign-off by an authorised approver, with an audit trail.
    • Set a review or expiry date.

    Output: An approved, dated version.

  7. 7 Check what was published, and monitor

    • Confirm the version approved is the version published.
    • Track expiry dates and review triggers such as rate, fee or law changes.

    Output: Publication checked; review triggers set.

Common mistakes

  • Reviewing each sentence but not the overall impression.
  • Accepting claims without evidence on file.
  • Sustainability claims that go further than how the product is managed.
  • Publishing a different version from the one approved.
